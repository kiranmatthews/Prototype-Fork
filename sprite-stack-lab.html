Short answer

Yes, the look works for this game, and it is running here on the real animations. Two parts of the plan need to change.

  1. It holds up at the game’s normal cameras. From behind the rider, on foot and on the board, the stack reads clearly.
  2. Side-on rooms break flat slices. Seen edge-on they turn into a pile of lines. Drawn thick, or as solid cubes, they hold.
  3. Slices cannot be worked out from four pictures. They are cut from the 3D rider the game already has; pictures can only supply colour. Four is also one short: the camera looks down on the rider, so it needs a picture from above.
  4. Repainting the stills is the weak step. A line drawn round the edge of one picture is not on the edge from any other side, so on the model it lands as smears. Tested with a stand-in made in code, not a real image model.
  5. Add the look while drawing instead. Design it once, then apply it as rules: a palette, and an outline worked out from the model. It is the same from every angle and in every frame, with nothing to repaint.
  6. The price is the live animation. Today the rider is posed fresh every frame: moves blend into each other and limbs stretch with speed. A stack plays back fixed poses, so every blend would have to be baked as frames of its own.
