Honest answer

It can be done, and it is running here on the real animations. I would not rebuild the rider this way.

  1. It does work as a way to draw him. From the game’s normal cameras, behind the rider on foot and on the board, the stack reads clearly.
  2. But look at what it replaces. The cubes are 5 cm, so his whole head is 16 squares across. Each animation becomes a handful of fixed poses, where the game now poses him fresh every frame, blending moves and stretching limbs.
  3. The plan’s pipeline does not hold. Slices cannot be worked out from four pictures. They have to be cut from the 3D rider, so the model stays the real source either way, and colour needs a fifth picture from above.
  4. The image-model step can add little. A still keeps 56 by 72 squares once it is on the stack, and a line drawn round the edge of a picture lands on the model as smears. What survives is a flat change of colour. Tested with stand-ins made in code, not a real image model.
  5. A look added while drawing does that job for free. A palette, and an outline worked out from the model, are the same from every angle and in every frame.
  6. Side-on rooms need a second way of drawing it. Seen edge-on, flat slices turn into a pile of lines; they have to be drawn thick or as solid cubes.

Where it would fit: as an extra, not a replacement. An unlockable skin, a retro bonus room, the figures on a menu or map: things seen from above that only turn.
