# Bail and breakup behavior

The damage result and the presentation are separate. The controller still owns collision, shield use, lives, checkpoint respawn, and supported recovery. Breakup moves existing semantic joints after collision and animation have finished, then restores them before the next simulation step. It never creates attacking limbs or changes movement tuning.

| Incident | Presentation | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Failed balance, missed trick, PvP knockdown, ordinary cliff tumble | Intact physics tumble and supported shoulder-roll recovery | Loss of control alone does not imply a destructive impact. |
| Protected skating crash | Intact, shorter bail; existing mask/combo/board rules | A shield visibly softens the same crash. |
| Wall/clothesline, low trip, rail impact, or huge-drop landing at any nonfatal speed | Connected body with incident-specific tumble, loose spring limbs, and supported recovery | Severity changes the motion, never separates a recoverable body. |
| Fatal TNT/nitro/explosion | All pieces scatter away from the actual blast centre with an upward fan | Explosion direction comes from the source. |
| Fatal descending metal crate, active crusher, rolling stone, overhead descending hopper, or direct chief claw slam | Low outward scatter, downward impulse and individual segment compression/rebound | A crushing hit spreads pieces close to the floor instead of launching a firework. |
| Ordinary enemy/projectile contact, pit, lethal floor or water | Intact authored defeat; unsupported corpses continue into a tumble | Keep quiet/unsupported deaths legible and avoid gratuitous scattering. |
| A bail becomes fatal | Blast/crush can start the existing fatal breakup; other fatal causes stay intact | Charge one life and hand over to the established death path. |

`src/character/wipeoutPolicy.ts` permits breakup only for fatal blast/crush incidents. The nonfatal `bail()` path never constructs or requests debris, including protected hits. Damage thresholds, masks, combo loss, thrown boards, gameplay RNG, rescue input and fatal presentation remain controller-owned.

Intact bails combine the existing contact-normal rebound with a pooled 14-joint spring rig (`src/character/bailMotion.ts`). Independent stiffness, damping, rest biases and launch momentum vary per incident. Elbows, wrists, knees, ankles, chest and head lag behind rotation and react to each impact; no repeating flail sine controls nonfatal limbs. Anatomical hinge limits bound the motion. Segment compression/rebound multiplies the shared editable elasticity channels; it never scales the skeleton or changes the gameplay collider. Two spring substeps per normal frame keep fast limbs stable; long pauses have bounded work. The layer composes after authored clips and restores before the next pose, including with the animation bridge disabled.

A short contact roll retains momentum before a 0.56-second supported recovery (previously 0.72); stable-ground qualification is 0.08 seconds (previously 0.12). The spring layer releases before planted recovery, and loss of support resumes the tumble. Existing input mashing can still accelerate recovery.

Each new impact selects a posed head, shoulder, elbow, wrist, knee or ankle contact and applies torque from its lever about the waist. Twelve joint spheres keep this bounded; they read existing matrices without another hierarchy update or mesh scan. The detailed surface probes still own ground seating. World angular momentum survives changes to the rebound heading, with a 16 rad/s contact limit. Contact compression propagates into delayed wrists/ankles, briefly softer springs and a fresh asymmetric fold. Ground friction turns the remaining skid into a short roll along actual travel; all residual motion releases into the existing supported recovery. Fully faded springs stop running.

Missed skating jumps and already-bailing bodies rebound from solid sides instead of stopping at the face. The response preserves 86% of tangential velocity and reflects 34% of inward velocity, so linear energy falls at every contact and missed pit jumps keep descending. Eligible ledge grabs and wallrides run first. Separating contacts cannot kick again. Camera-lane and travel-zone steering cannot turn a ragdoll back into the face; normal controls resume after recovery. The ordinary grounded low/mid trip and fatal paths retain their existing owners.

Low and mid-height obstacles throw the connected skater forward over their tops. Launch lift has a height-based clearance minimum. The specific solid that caught the feet releases its upright side collision during the rising/clearing arc, preventing the next frame from cancelling forward momentum; unrelated walls remain solid. That release ends on descent below the top, grounding, a new bail or respawn. Rail trips travel across continuously without a far-side position jump. Slow crate trips carry their entry speed, and the same crate cannot turn its own trip into a rescue stomp during clearance.

The initial trip pitches the upper body along actual travel, independent of the camera/input frame's left/right handedness. Angular drag eases the caught-foot fold into a body-first descent before the first ground impact, preventing a fast initial somersault from putting the feet in front again. The drag integrates exactly across frame subdivisions; impact-driven tumbling resumes on contact. Direction checks measure the posed head against both ankles throughout the initial airborne fall, including camera lanes and varied cosmetic seeds. This adds no geometry, world probes or per-frame allocations, and leaves fatal and other bail rotation paths unchanged.

Bail-only skin bounds use cached per-bone influence envelopes, including changing morphs, instead of full vertex scans. These bounds are conservative; ordinary attacks keep exact measurement. Geometry/weights/bind edits invalidate the cache and unsupported custom/projective/negative-weight skins use the exact fallback. Surface contact still uses sparse posed surface points, so empty box corners cannot lift a body. Grounded bails reuse their current support for shadow presentation. No new physics bodies, world queries, geometry clones or per-frame random draws are added by the spring solver.

Detached joints use measured local boxes with orientation-dependent support, swept wall contacts, floor normals, bounded surface probes and finite settling onto a stable broad face. Newly solid floors enter the bounded collision set, and moving or removed supports wake resting pieces. Segment recoil uses shared editable elasticity amplitudes. The generic effect retains its internal docking support for diagnostic use; gameplay bails never enter it. Fatal pieces stay loose until normal respawn. Gameplay bounds are measured on the restored character, and existing render batching/interpolation carries the visible pieces.

CPU evidence is recorded in `docs/performance/intact-bail-motion.json`: 24 warmed incidents per case, about 34–42% lower active-step CPU and 46–48% lower total incident CPU on this machine. These are Node fixed-step measurements, not whole-browser frame or GPU timings. The lite/full browser verifier checks connected bails, recovery, life cost and fatal breakup with the actual loaded character.

The later contact/tumble iteration is measured in `docs/performance/ragdoll-impact-motion.json`. Active-step medians remain about 0.61–0.68 ms with the extra motion; total incident medians are slightly lower. The new contact feature's initial surface-probe implementation cost about 0.129 ms per contact; its fixed joint-probe version costs about 0.00075 ms, with zero mesh scans or hierarchy updates at impact. That isolated comparison is not a 99% whole-game speedup. The review also uses a fixed timing ring, reuses camera vectors and updates diagnostics only while expanded.
