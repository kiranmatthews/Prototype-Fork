Layers: street; bowls, pipes and the walkway; awnings and low ledges; second- and third-storey ledges and signs (the main grind layer); roof edges, the booth roof and cables; the blimp sign; the giant circular sign at the top. Heights are not measured.

Circulation: the street is a long loop round the walkway and the full pipes; the cables form a second loop overhead (lantern cable west, booth cables north, cable to the yellow sign east, bridge tracks south).

## Spawn and first view

Spawn is on top of the long roll-in in front of the judges' booth at the north end, facing south down the roll-in (TAS3 6:25; G3 p136 "from the starting point atop the roll-in, turn around ..."). QPs flank the roll-in, three neon rails run behind the booth, and the funbox directly ahead is the Bento Box Gap (G3 p138). The TAS opening combo is entirely in this view.

Visible from spawn: the walkway, the facades with their signs, the blimp. The secret area is hidden; its key (the circular sign) is high on the east facade.

## Guidance and sightlines

- Signs are the level: almost every ledge, awning and sign is a rail, and the gap list names them by sign (fish, Tokion, Jeep, hamburger, Happy Fun Buns, Dualshock 2). The facades are a vertical rail grid (G3 p131).
- The giant circular sign is the target in plain view; the way to break it is to grind the cables that hold it, at the end of a cable chain that starts at the opposite end (J3 p026).
- The lantern cable crosses the west street and connects the SW signs, the bus stop and the north end: the backbone of several stat-point and secret-area routes.
- Hidden half pipes sit behind signs; a stat point and the remake logo lead into the one behind the SE blue sign (G3 p134, p145; PS34).
- The bullet train announces itself with a warning bell (G3 p147).
- The blimp's red plexi sign hangs over the centre: a visible high perch, reached only with a full special meter and a Boneless from the vert ramp between the full pipes (G3 p139).
- G3's "How bad do you want it?" box recommends maxing Air, Hangtime, Ollie and Speed for the hardest stat points (p133): the guide treats stats as a gate.

## Design logic

1. The final competition is the most vertical: points come from chaining facade ledges and signs at third-storey height, not from open-floor tricks (G3 p131).
2. Destruction is the key: the secret area opens only when the biggest sign is destroyed by grinding the cables that hold it (J3 p026; G3 p141).
3. The key is a lap of the high layer: the J3 chain goes south bowl → bridge → west lantern cable → north booth → east ledges → sign, so the player has to know the whole cable network.
4. A secret area without collectibles: G3 notes that the Neon Freeway has no stat points (p148). Its reward is a set of 400-1500-point gaps for a judged heat.
5. Events change the reachable set: the cutout left by the sign becomes a ramp to a stat point (G3 p134).
6. Hidden vert spots behind signs reward those who look behind the facade ("Destruction is good", G3 p132).
7. Extreme-value gaps for extreme lines: Loop Balance and Fugu Harumaki are 5000, Men Agari 1500. Value tracks difficulty and rarity (G3 p136, p146).
8. Safety-net pairs: the Wussy (north, funbox between) and Lucky (south, no funbox) Harumaki transfers teach the same jump with and without a cushion (G3 p146).
9. A timed moving rail: the train comes after a bell and offers three ways on, from three heights (G3 p147).
10. The judges are harsher: J3 says well over 110,000 points are needed to place third (p025). The TAS still wins with one ≈ 142k combo per heat (TAS3 6:28-6:31).
11. The remake keeps the cable-chain unlock (a "short cutscene" opens the area with the cash) but adds a direct ramp-and-rail entry from the start side for its platinum goal (PS34).

## Original vs remake

THPS 3+4 keeps the layout, the sign-breaking unlock and the secret area, and adds a second entrance. The PS1 edition is a different, simpler layout.

- Still a medal competition; no tape added (PS34 tokyo; PS34 all-tapes list).
- Platinum pro goal: Sal Flip the Chanko Nabe Gap: start → left up the ramp → rail up the building on the left → straight up the middle into the new area → kicker over to the other side (PS34). The secret area gets a second, start-side entrance; the screenshots show a smashed wall opening onto a derelict lot with lanterns and QPs (PS34 screenshots 17-19).
- Cable chain kept: grind the lantern wire and "follow the grind trail until you trigger a short cutscene"; cash waits in the opened area (PS34).
- Collectibles added: cash ×3 (one in a hologram, one in the net under the airship), hidden deck on a rooftop (QP by the Vans ramp, Boneless to the rail), Iron Galaxy logo in the hidden half pipe behind the pools' back wall, panda on a narrow ledge below the new area's entrance ramp, stat points ×3 (PS34).
- PS1 edition (VG, "Tokyo Freeride Skate Off"): a different layout: start plaza with a funbox at one end, an elevated walkway ring round a sunken centre with wavy neon humps and a rock or machine sculpture, two bowls at the far end, cables from facades; no bridge, no blimp and no secret area drawn.

## Transfer to this game

1. Sign-breaking crystal: hang a giant round sign on four cables high on a facade; grinding the cables in sequence (the end of a lap of the high cable layer) breaks the sign and opens a hidden area behind it; the crystal waits inside.
2. Blimp perch: a crystal on a sign hanging under a slow blimp; reach it with a spine transfer at full charge (or a trampoline under the sign), then manual its length; a diagonal launch off its curved end lands on a full-pipe top.
3. Train ride: a travelling rail (a train) crosses a bridge after a bell; the crystal rides on its roof, reachable from the bridge grind, from a hanging rail lip, or from a hidden half pipe behind a sign.
4. Facade grid: three storeys of ledges and awnings on both sides of a narrow street, linked by grind-ollies (≈ 2.4 m pop) and a lantern cable; put breadcrumb gems along the ledges toward a crystal on the top storey.
5. Behind-the-sign pipe: a half pipe hidden behind a billboard, entered by grinding the billboard top and dropping in; the crystal floats above its far deck.
