Layers: lower floor (water hazard, corridor); ground floor (fountain atrium, walkways); second floor (balconies,
directory 2); the upstairs section above it (Flying Leap, "three stories" above the water level, G1 p077); the
suspended light rods between the walkway and the water floor. Height estimates: one storey ≈ 4–5 m in this mall.

Circulation: one-way overall. The fountain atrium offers three parallel routes (left flagpole/Coffee Grind,
centre fountain jump to the second balcony, right flagpole/planter and the right walkway); they rejoin at the
water hazard. The two escalators near Z4 let you go back up; the outside atrium (Z9) can be worked back and forth
at the end of a run (G1 p076).

Route (G1's "easier" way, p069 and p074; PS12; observed in LP1 8:48–8:58):

1. **From the first escalator, take the right side** (G1 p069: from the top of the first escalator take the right
   flagpole to hit the long planter below; "this way you can be lined up for the car jump and balcony jump to the
   high rails"). LP1 grinds the K planter ledge first (8:46–8:48).
2. **Ride the right walkway along the right wall** "above and before the hazard" (G1 p074). LP1 8:50–8:52.
3. **Jump the car with the ramp over it, or ride past it** (kicker). PS12: "a ramp over a wrecked car". G1:
   "Either clear the car or avoid it but just be sure to have good line".
4. **Ollie off the jump at the end of the walkway on a line that crosses the two rods** (ollie, blind launch).
   Variant (G1 "really daring", done by LP1 8:52–8:53): 50-50 the rail along the left edge of the walkway its
   whole length and jump from its end; LP1 passes through the 200 icon in this air ("50 50 GRIND + BONUS 200").
5. **Land in a grind on a rod** (grind). LP1 8:54 ("530 X2").
6. **Grind the rod down its whole length** (≈ 30–40 m, LP1 ≈ 4 s; "a long haul", G1 p074).
7. **At the far end, ollie between the two rods to collect the tape** (rail-transfer/ollie). G1 p074: "you have
   to ollie between them at just the right moment". LP1 collects it at the end of the grind (8:57, timer 1:15,
   "HIDDEN TAPE!").
8. **Drop to the floor by directory 3**, or, if there is time, give "a quick grind to the rails below" (G1 p074).
   The course continues to directory 3 and the big escalator.

Alternative lines:

- **Second balcony** (G1 p074, "the hardest way ... but that will get you a gap bonus"): from the second balcony
  (reached through the fountain or the second escalator; start back by directory 2) clear the ledge and "the
  massive gap" to the light fixtures with a lot of speed. LP12 0:11:40–0:11:47 (remake) shows a human player
  smashing directory 2 on the balcony, grinding the balcony rail and transferring onto the high rails to the tape.
- **Flying Leap and from below** (TAS1 1:48–1:52): switch nollie off the far end of the upstairs section (The
  Flying Leap, 100) down three storeys, land in a grind on the A rail over the water (A collected), hop to the
  planter, and fastplant straight up between the rods' low end to the tape (timer 1:42), then directory 3. This is
  the shortest line but relies on the extra height of a fastplant.
- **1+2 lower beams** (PS12, gap Across The Light Beam 250): after the car, ollie at the end, drop below the light
  rails and grind one of the beams below. The remake adds a scored alternative next to the tape route.

## Guidance and sightlines

- **The rods are visible to everyone below them.** They hang over the water hazard and the A rail, on the route
  every line rejoins; the tape glints at their far end.
- **The launch is not.** From the walkway the camera does not show the rods well; G1 says you launch "almost
  blindly" and must already know the line. The level relies on the player having seen the rods from below.
- **Choose early.** The tape route is decided at the top of the first escalator (right flagpole, G1 p069) or at
  the second balcony, a section before the rods. A linear level teaches that a hidden collectible can depend on a
  branch chosen in advance.
- **Bonus icons on the route.** A 200 icon sits in the launch air at the end of the walkway and a 100 beside the
  tape (VG; LP1 8:53). They confirm the line once you are on it.
- **Directories as waypoints.** Each directory stands across the main route and marks progress downhill.
- **Hidden on purpose:** the walkway's launch towards the rods; the second-balcony leap.

## Design logic

1. **Visible from below, reached from above.** As in School, the tape sits over a main-line feature (the A rail
   over the water) but can only be reached by an upper approach. The Mall moves the upper approach from a roof to
   suspended rails.
2. **The tape uses the level's star feature.** Tony's favourite spot in the level is grinding the hanging rods
   (G1 p067); the guide notes that hopping between them is the skill the tape needs (G1 p068). The secret is the
   hardest use of the signature object.
3. **A linear level makes the choice early.** In a one-way course the approach must be chosen a section before
   the rods (right walkway, second balcony, or the upstairs Flying Leap); the player has to plan, not react.
4. **Blind launch, readable landing.** The launch hides the rods, but the rods are long (≈ 30–40 m) and paired,
   so a good line has a wide landing window; the difficulty is commitment, not precision at the start.
5. **Precision at the end.** The tape sits between the two rods at their far end; you must hop between them at the
   right moment. The route ends in a timing test rather than a jump for distance.
6. **Failure is expensive.** A miss puts you below and past the launch on a one-way course; the retry costs the
   climb back via the escalators. After two levels of cheap failure, the third tape raises the cost.
7. **Three graded approaches.** Walkway and car ("easier"), walkway left rail ("really daring"), second balcony
   ("hardest", gap bonus) — plus the TAS's Flying Leap and jump from below. The same target serves several skill
   levels, as in School.
8. **Letters are the course.** In a linear level the S-K-A-T-E tour equals the route; letters teach the run's
   rhythm, not a loop.
9. **Directories as gates.** Smashable signs across the path mark the course in order; the last one blocks the
   exit to the garage, so collecting it means finishing the course.
10. **Speed from the slope.** The whole level falls about three storeys; the tape route needs no pumping, only a
    held line from the walkway.
11. **Progression across the first three tapes.** Warehouse: one jump from spawn into a room (difficulty 1). School:
    climb to a roof, grind a pipe, ride a canopy (2). Mall: blind launch onto suspended rails, long grind, timed hop,
    costly miss (3). Each tape adds one layer of commitment.
12. **The remake rewards the neighbourhood.** 1+2 adds Across The Light Beam (lower beams below the light rails)
    and makes The Flying Leap a goal, so players who fall short of the tape still score near it.

## Transfer to this game

1. **Hanging-rod crystal.** Hang two parallel travelling-rail-style rods (static) on cables over a water or pit
   area, ≈ 30–40 m long, falling ≈ 2–4 m from the launch end to the far end; put the crystal between them at the
   far end so the skater must ollie from one rod across to the other to touch it. A grind ollie pops ≈ 2.4 m above
   the rail, enough for a sideways hop of ≈ 1.5–2 m between rods.
2. **Launch from a side walkway.** End an elevated walkway with a kicker aimed along the rods' axis; put a pickup in
   the launch air (the 200 icon's role) to confirm the line. Use snap radius (2.1 m) generously at the rod's near
   end so a slightly blind launch still lands in a grind.
3. **Show it from below first.** Route the main line under the rods (the A role: a low rail over the hazard) so
   every player sees the crystal from below before they reach the launch.
4. **Offer three approaches.** Walkway kicker (easy), walkway edge rail into the rods (harder), a balcony leap
   from the level above (hardest, with a named gap or trick gate). Keep one approach from below for experts (a
   charged ollie or trampoline near the far end).
5. **Make the miss costly but recoverable.** On a one-way course, provide a return route (an escalator ramp or
   speed pad back up) of ≈ 15–20 s so a miss costs time but does not end the attempt.
6. **Out of foot reach.** Keep the rods' far end and the crystal ≥ ≈ 6.5–7 m above any standable surface (double
   jump 4.6 m plus body height), or over water/pit with no footing below.
