Vertical layers (original and remake): street and plaza ground; ledges, benches, kiosks and handrails (0.5-1 m); the campus wall top and awnings (≈ 3-4 m, reached by wallride, wallie or a plywood kicker); building-front quarter pipes whose copings sit at about first-floor height; roofs (≈ 8-14 m) with halfpipes; wires (power lines over the road, bunting over the campus, black wires between roofs) and the clock-tower top. The level is a ring: the outer road (Z1-Z3) wraps the campus (Z4-Z7) and the two are joined by the main gate arch, the side arches and the stone bridge, so a player can circulate without dead ends.

Vert areas: the purple and gold pool/bowl, building-front concrete quarter pipes, the roof halfpipes, Tony's wooden spine at the school, the silver court quarter pipes (remake) and the garbage truck used as a spine. Secret area: the clock-tower portal (all three versions) that relocates the skater to the top of the tower.

## Spawn and first view

**Original (PS2).** The career opens on the sidewalk of the outer road (LP4 0:05:26-0:05:30): ahead a green steel arch spans the street, the clock tower rises above the roofs beyond it, a white SUV is parked at the kerb, and a tutorial box tells the player to talk to people with arrows over their heads (the nearest, a Skate Rat by the shops, is 10 s away, LP4 0:05:36). Nothing is collectible yet: in THPS4 the letters only appear after a goal giver starts the S-K-A-T-E or C-O-M-B-O goal (the Professor gives S-K-A-T-E in LP4 0:13:30; Ollie the Bum gives C-O-M-B-O in LP4 0:10:00). So the original's "first view" sells the tower and the goal givers, not the collectibles.

**Remake.** Each 10:00 run opens with a goal flythrough that shows every goal's location, including "Get the secret tape" (LP34 1:06:45-1:07:05: the S on the Woodland School facade, the frat balcony, the Professor's red car, the "VOTE DICK" banner, then the tape beside the corner of a brick building with a pennant string). PS34's directions place the start beside the outer road at the campus edge ("from level start go left down the street", "look to the right and wallride up to grind the first banner"); LP34 1:07:10 shows a curving concrete walk and by 1:07:15 the player is at the tennis courts. The clock tower is the vanishing point of the main street axis (LP34 1:09:46), so the tape's landmark is in view for much of the level even though the tape itself is small.

**PS1.** VG marks START on the outer road at the south-west campus gate (the corner where the wooden campus wall meets the street); goals are icons in the world (numbered on the map) rather than people.

**Route — TAPE-R**

1. **ride** — outer street below the campus gate → campus gate. Ride up the outer street toward the gate; the clock tower stands at the end of the axis (LP34 1:09:46-1:09:49).
2. **ride** — campus gate → central plaza. Cross the plaza and its fountain ring (LP34 1:09:50-1:09:54).
3. **grind** — plaza steps → tower terrace. Grind the curved handrail up the steps toward the tower (LP34 1:09:55-1:09:57).
4. **ledge** — tower terrace → back of the tower. Ride or grind the terrace ledge round to the rear of the tower (LP34 1:09:58-1:09:59).
5. **portal** — open green double door with a small wooden kicker at its threshold → tower top. Roll through the door: a portal relocates the skater to the top of the tower and launches him onto the wire (LP34 1:10:00-1:10:01; PS34 screenshot of the door).
6. **wire** — tower top → tape. Hold a 50-50 down the long descending wire; the tape registers as the wire nears the brick building (LP34 1:10:02-1:10:04, "COMPLETE! Get the secret tape" at 7:19).
7. **ollie** — wire end → brick roof. Land on the brick building's flat roof (LP34 1:10:05) and drop back to the plaza.

**Alternative route — uphill rooftop chain (hard)** (WEB gamerant college tape guide; WEB screenrant)

1. **wallride** — outer street left of spawn → campus wall top. Wallride the wall before the first pillar and grind its top for about a second.
2. **rail-transfer** — wall top → banner (bunting) string. Jump right into a grind on the bunting string over the street.
3. **wire** — bunting string → first rooftop. Halfway along, hop left onto a black wire and grind up to a rooftop; trick during the grind to keep speed and jump off before the wire ends.
4. **ledge** — first rooftop halfpipe lip → second black wire. Grind the right-hand lip of the rooftop halfpipes; the grind continues onto a second wire across the campus.
5. **ollie** — end of second wire → brick building roof. Jump left, turn sharply and drop onto the brick building (a gap in its roof punishes an over-turn).
6. **wire** — brick roof edge → tape. Start a grind on the roof's left edge, which links into the steep, long uphill wire; flip and grab while grinding to keep momentum until the tape.

## Guidance and sightlines

- Clock tower as beacon: visible from the original spawn (LP4 0:05:30) and as the end point of the remake street axis (LP34 1:09:46); the tape is on its wire.
- Goal flythrough (remake): each run starts by showing every goal location, including the tape beside the brick building (LP34 1:06:45-1:07:05); WEB gamerant notes the flythrough hints every tape.
- Open door with a kicker at the back of the tower base is the only cue to the portal (PS34 screenshot); it is out of sight from the front, so finding it is exploration.
- Arrows over goal givers' heads and the tutorial text direct the original player to people, not places (LP4 0:05:30).
- Bunting and pennant strings strung between buildings read as grindable wires in the remake (PS34 screenshot 31; WEB gamerant).
- Letters ride on obvious launchers: S over the door at the end of a handrail, T over a QP, A over a truck, K on a power line above a sloped gate arm (G4m p003).
- Election banners and graffiti mark the long wall as a grind surface (G4m p011; LP34 1:07:00 "VOTE DICK" flythrough).
- Hidden on purpose: the tower portal, Central Hall's second storey (PS1), the remake elevator to a rooftop, and the rooftop wire network.

## Design logic

1. The tape uses the level's tallest and most visible landmark; the challenge is not seeing it but finding the way up (EXPLORE), and the portal makes the way up a secret rather than a feat (remake TAPE-R; PS1 Clock Tower gap).
2. A tower-top wire exists in all three versions (PS2 gap "Clock tower Grind", PS1 "High Wire Act" 200 from the tower top to Central Hall and "Clock Tower" 350 for the portal, remake tape wire). Inferred: Iron Galaxy put the tape on a line the 2002 versions already rewarded with gaps, so the tape points players at an old secret.
3. Two approaches to one tape: a fast secret (door, downhill wire, ≈ 4 s) and a skilled public route (rooftop wire chain, uphill, tricks to keep speed). The collectible rewards either knowledge or execution.
4. Wire placement escalates: K on a power line reached from a gate arm (one hop from the ground), then remake car-park wires, then roof-to-roof wires, then the tower wire. Each wire collectible starts higher than the last.
5. Original letters are a guided tour: the S-K-A-T-E order runs from the outer street (S, K, A) into the campus (T, E) and each letter adds one verb (grind, wire, spine transfer, vert air, air-to-grind).
6. THPS4 hides nothing until a goal is accepted: letters and goal objects only appear after talking to a giver, so the original's "hard-to-reach" layer lives in gaps and pro goals, not in tapes.
7. Free roam removes the 2-minute pressure from exploration but each goal still has a clock (S-K-A-T-E 2:00; record 56.37 s), so letter routes stay short and local even in a 300 m map.
8. Buildings are permeable: frat windows (three named gaps), Central Hall's second storey (PS1), the tower portal and the remake elevator all turn walls into routes and give secret-area rewards.
9. Long boundary walls become grind lines through goals (banners on the campus wall; frat boys on the wall top), which teaches wallride and wallie as entry moves before the rooftop routes need them.
10. Failure is cheap near the ground (letters over QPs, rails a few metres up) and only expensive on the wire layer, where a fall costs a climb; the portal caps that cost for the tape.
11. Remake runs are 10:00 rather than 2:00 because the THPS4 maps are large: LP34 cleared every College goal with about 5 minutes left (1:12:30), and the tape took ≈ 18 s of that from the street.
12. Letters in the remake are rearranged around new props (shipping containers, the purple statue, the mascot QP) but keep the original logic of a street half and a campus half (PS34).

## Original vs remake

- Tape: none in the 2002 PS2 career; PS1 hid one on a wire from the white building's top; the remake puts it on the clock-tower wire reached through the tower portal (LP34 1:10:00-1:10:04).
- Timer: original free roam with per-goal clocks; remake 10:00 runs (LP34 1:07:10).
- S stays on the Woodland School front (both); K moves from a power line reached from the garage gate (original) to a bunting wire from car-park deck 2 (remake); A moves from the garbage truck spine to shipping containers with a QP; T moves from the Fine Arts QP to a blue QP by a new purple statue; E moves from the Engineering handrail corner to an upper level reached by spine transfer from a mascot QP (G4m p003 vs PS34).
- Officer Tom becomes Officer Dick; the banner goal is the same 7-banner wall grind (G4m p011 vs PS34).
- The amateur C-O-M-B-O by the bridge becomes a pro goal in the remake with a similar bridge-side rail start (G4m p004 vs PS34).
- Spine transfer over the wall becomes "Boneless Spine Transfer Between the Courts" (G4m p008 vs PS34).
- Remake adds stat points, cash, a hidden deck, an Iron Galaxy logo (second wire from the tape-wire rooftop) and a panda plushie, several of them on the rooftop wire layer (PS34).

## Transfer to this game

1. Landmark-wire crystal: build the level's tallest tower visible down the main axis, run a long descending rope or rail from its top to a lower roof, and put the crystal two-thirds of the way down. Access by a portal door at the back of the tower base (fast) or by grinding up from the roof end with tricks to hold speed (skilled). Our grind snap radius (2.1 m) and grind-ollie pop (≈ 2.4 m) make a ≈ 1-2 m drop from the door exit to the rail forgiving.
2. Gate-arm launch: place a sloped object (gate arm, ramp plank) under an overhead cable so a rail grind up the slope feeds a grind ollie (≈ 2.4 m above the rail) onto the cable; a letter or crystal shard on the cable teaches the wire layer early.
3. Teach then test: put an early collectible on a single cable 4-5 m up, a mid-level one on a roof-to-roof cable, and the crystal on the highest cable; each needs the previous lesson.
4. Rooftop chain alternative: wallride a boundary wall (wallie off it) into a bunting rope, hop to a second rope, then a roof; with no wallplant in our game, use wallride + wallie and a kicker at the wall base.
5. Flythrough tease: show the crystal in the level-intro camera beside a distinctive roof corner so players know where to look, then hide only the entrance (back door), not the crystal.
6. Permeable building: an open window on an upper storey reachable from a bowl kicker (PS1 Central Hall) gives a secret room with its own exit cable; good for an on-foot double jump (≈ 4.6 m) alternative if the board launch fails.
