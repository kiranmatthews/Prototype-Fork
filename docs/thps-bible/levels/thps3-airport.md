Layers, low to high: cargo floor (one storey below the gates, reached by the north and south spiral walkways); terminal and gates floor; counters, couches, carousel and kiosks (≈ 1 m); escalator handrails (sloped); banked walls and narrow doorway QPs (≈ 2.5–3.5 m, estimate); monitors and message boards; overhead light rows (the "ceiling rail" layer, several metres up); window lattice tiers (flag rail → second rail → top rail); roof lattice. Outdoors: the helipad at terminal level. Heights are estimates; no frame gave a measurable reference.

Circulation: the main route is a one-way spine. Spawn → walkways → baggage claim (side room) → security → purple area and restrooms → two escalator banks → fork → north or south arm → spiral down to the cargo, which joins the two arms underneath. The helipad loops back into baggage claim (Through the Pad!). The cargo basement is the only true loop (north spiral ↔ cargo ↔ south spiral).

## Spawn and first view

Spawn is in the ticket hall between the Hawkair counters, facing down the terminal toward the first moving walkway (G3 p093 screenshot; remake: "S straight ahead after level start", PS34). Visible from spawn: the tickets on the left counter (goal object), the payphone kiosk on the left with a light row above it (S for Hawk's group, taken 5–6 s in by TAS3 3:46-3:47), and the first walkway's centre handrail ahead (S for Caballero's group). The X-ray machine is behind the right counter; it does not announce itself.

The opening invites a rail line, not a drop. Kiosk → lights → escalator → walkway rail (G3 p086). Vert features are absent until baggage claim. The tape is not visible: it is at the opposite end of the level, beyond two escalator banks and a fork.

**Route (designed line):**

1. **roll** — from bottom of the last escalators (Z7, the fork) to northern gates (Z8). Turn right at the fork; G3 p089 warns of the rent-a-cop at the escalator foot.
2. **vert-air prep** — from gates floor to narrow QP in a gate doorway left of the French flag. Build speed on the open gates floor; J3 p018 identifies it as the gate C door left of the departures board. Remake: doorway QP with a city label (PS34 15; LP34 0:35:23).
3. **transfer** — from narrow gate QP to uppermost lattice rail on the right. Air up the QP, steer slightly right (J3 p019) and land in a grind on the top rail (G3 p089). Remake run reached it as FS 50-50 on a lower window rail → FS wallride on the glass → wallie → top rail, scoring "1st Class Grind!!" (LP34 0:35:27).
4. **grind (balance)** — from top rail to curve near the end of the north arm. Hold the grind round the curve, keeping balance (J3 p019 "tenez l'équilibre jusqu'à la cassette"); the tape sits on the curve (G3 p089).
5. **drop** — from top rail to gates floor. Drop off, or keep grinding: for Hawk/Koston/Muska/Thomas a stat point lies further along ("hold the grind through the Secret Tape", G3 p092).

Observed vs inferred. The rail tier (uppermost, northern section, around a curve) and the launcher (narrow ramp left of the French flag) are printed in G3 p089 and J3 p018-p019, and observed in LP34 0:35:23-0:35:27 (doorway QP → "1st Class Grind!!" → pickup burst). The rail being the same rail as the cargo "1st Class" gaps is inferred from that gap firing at the pickup in the remake. The heights are estimates.

Alternative lines. (a) TAS3 4:40-4:45.5: north flag rail through five flags → spiral-stair handrail ("Kissed the Rail"; 10 Countries complete at 4:42) → Boneless + BS Overcrook onto the lattice → BS 5-0 → Wallie → top rail → tape. (b) G3 p099: 1st Class Grind!! from the cargo floor, "most likely max stats". (c) J3 p020 (Hawk): keep grinding the whole tape rail to reach a stat point above a QP in front of gate D.

## Guidance and sightlines

- The terminal is a funnel: escalators lead down one level at a time and every sign and corridor points toward the gates. The tape is at the end of the funnel, the farthest point from spawn (G3 p086, p091).
- Each ceiling-light row has its own launcher under it: phone kiosk, centre ramp, narrow monitor, small ramp. Letters, stat points and decks sit on those rows, so the player learns "find the launcher under the rail" (G3 p088, p092-p093).
- The banked wall at the fork faces the bottom of the last escalators and carries a hanging letter for one skater group: a landmark at the decision point (G3 p088).
- The helicopter can be seen through the baggage-claim windows: a tease for the helipad. The way in is the X-ray belt behind the right counter, a "forbidden" passenger path (G3 p087).
- Flags drape over the lowest window rail, so a row of colours marks the rail line, and the Visit 10 Countries goal sends every player along it. The tape is on the same rail system, two tiers higher (G3 p089).
- The doorway QPs are purpose-built launchers. The original guide names them by the flag beside them ("left of the French flag"); the remake gives them city signs (Vancouver, Tokyo, London) so text can point at them (G3 p089; PS34).
- One skater group's last stat point floats above the tape launcher (G3 p092), and the Hawk group's continues past the tape on the same rail: breadcrumbs that confirm the rail is a destination.
- Hidden on purpose: the helipad (behind the counter), the cargo basement (down the spiral walkways), and the restroom interiors (pickpockets; one group's S and K).
- Remake: the tape glints blue on the rail (PS34 screenshot 16); the COMBO letters form a visible ladder up the south lattice (PS34).

## Design logic

1. Linear level, tape at the end: the tape marks the far end of a one-way terminal, so it rewards traversal. TAS3 picks it up last (64 s in); the remake player picks it up after finishing the countries goal at the same window (LP34 0:35:14-0:35:27).
2. Vertical ladder on one wall: the gates' glazed wall carries the flag rail (goal), the Economy, Business and 1st Class rails (gaps from the cargo), and the tape on the top tier. One piece of geometry carries a graded series of challenges (G3 p089, p099).
3. Two ways up, two costs: the top rail can be reached cheaply from the gates floor through the doorway QP, or expensively from the cargo with max stats ("plan for a return visit"). The cheap route gives the tape; the expensive route only gives points (G3 p089 vs p099).
4. The approach is taught first: Visit 10 Countries puts the player on the same rail system with the same balance demand (hold a long curved grind through five flags) before the tape asks for it one tier higher.
5. Mirrored arms: the south arm repeats the north arm (flags, spiral stair, top rail). One group's deck sits on the south top rail "in the same location as the Secret Tape", and the remake hangs its COMBO ladder there (G3 p093; PS34).
6. Letters as a guided tour: for Hawk's group the five letters run from the start-hall lights to the northern monitor bank, so finishing S-K-A-T-E leaves the player under the tape lattice (G3 p088; TAS3 4:35 → tape 4:45).
7. The escort goal teaches the transfer: Get the Tickets forbids the metal detectors, so the player must transfer over the checkpoint (O the S). Clearing an obstacle by air is the skill the gate QPs and the tape launch then use (G3 p090).
8. A secret area through a forbidden door: the helipad is reached by riding the baggage belt through the X-ray machine. The tease (helicopter seen through glass) and the way in are in different rooms, and the way out (Through the Pad!) returns you to the main route (G3 p087, p096).
9. Cheap failure, expensive distance: missing the top rail drops you on the gates floor beside the launcher (a few seconds to retry), but reaching the north arm from spawn costs a large share of a 2:00 run. The tape fits the end of a run, after the other goals.
10. Skater-dependent placement: letters, stat points, decks and the trick goal change with the chosen skater (three letter sets, four stat-point sets), but the tape never moves (G3 p008, p088-p093; J3 p002). The tape is the level's fixed landmark.
11. Moving rails as score engines: carousel (laps add multipliers), walkways and escalators turn travel into scoring (G3 p095).
12. People as obstacles and targets: rent-a-cops and pedestrians obstruct; pickpockets flee and must be chased down. The floor stays busy while the rail layers are empty (G3 p086, p090).

## Original vs remake

THPS 3+4 (Iron Galaxy, 2025) keeps the layout and the tape. The PS1 edition is a different level built for the older hardware.

- Tape: same place and same idea (end of level, go right, high rail against the window, launch from one of the narrow QPs) (PS34). The remake run chains FS 50-50 → FS wallride → wallie → top rail and scores the original "1st Class Grind!!" gap on the way, so the tape rail is the 1st Class rail (LP34 0:35:27).
- Gate QPs are signposted with city names (Vancouver, Tokyo, London), giving text and players a way to name the launchers (PS34 screenshots, text).
- New pro goal COMBO letters climb the south lattice tier by tier (wallride → higher rail → higher rail → jump), an explicit tutorial for the tape climb on the mirror wall (PS34).
- New collectibles: 5 passports (one per district), 5 cash, hidden deck on the first light rows, Iron Galaxy logo on the helicopter rotor, panda plushie on top of the Tokyo QP (needs the level-out button), 4 stat points (PS34).
- Letters: one fixed set (S straight ahead; K on the third light; A above the short QP at the fork; T down the winding south slope; E on a rail fixed to a curved QP in the lower area) instead of three skater-dependent sets (PS34 vs G3 p088).
- Score thresholds unchanged (75k/150k/300k) plus High Combo 20,000; runs in the longplay start at 10:00 instead of 2:00 (PS34; LP34 0:31:20).
- PS1 edition (VG): a different airport. One hall at the end with five flags ("Snag the Flags"), luggage pickups ("Lost Luggage Found!"), "Nosebluntslide the Airport Sign" and "Grind the Plane" goals, gap labels "Plane Bash" and "Hey Get Down". Its tape is a high beam at the threshold of the gates hall, beside the E, not a window lattice.

## Transfer to this game

1. Window-lattice crystal: a curved glass wall with three rail tiers (≈ 1.5 m, ≈ 4 m, ≈ 6.5 m above the floor) and the crystal on the top tier past a bend. Primary launch: a narrow quarter pipe in a doorway (vert air ≈ 3–4 m) into a grind snap on tier 2 or 3. Expert line: grind tier 1, wallride the glass, wallie, grind-ollie (≈ 2.4 m pop) to the top. The rails are inside the room, so a miss lands on the floor beside the launcher.
2. Teach the rail system first: make the level's main objective (e.g. ring five bells or flags) a long curved grind on tier 1 of the same wall, so the balance and the line are known before the crystal asks for them one tier up.
3. Mirror arm as a rehearsal: build the same lattice on the opposite arm with a lesser reward (a gem cluster) on its top tier, so the player can practise the climb.
4. "Forbidden belt" secret: a conveyor (moving platform) through a scanner tunnel behind a counter leads to an outdoor pad with a spinning rotor rail; a speed pad on the belt exit returns the player to the main hall (loop back like Through the Pad!).
5. Light-row grammar: hang ceiling-light rows (grindable) along a long corridor and put a launcher under each (kicker, trampoline or speed pad). Use them for breadcrumb gems that lead to the far end where the crystal is.
6. Two-tier access: let a huge vert air from a basement half pipe below the glass wall also reach the crystal rail (for experts at full speed). The designed route stays the doorway QP.
