- **Loops**: halfpipe ↔ deck-top coping; the roll-in channel; the pool room lap.

Landmarks: central high deck with light-blue floor and white chevrons; floodlights on poles (night); alien on a gurney behind glass; military tank in the pool room; helicopters on pads outside the fence.

## Spawn and first view

You start on the raised roll-in deck at the N wall and drop S into the hall (VG START; LP1 0:27:39–0:27:43). The opening view takes in the light-blue central deck, the halfpipes, floodlights and the rock wall on the right. G1’s line starts at the ramp opposite the roll-in; LP1 lands its first 720 combo 6 s after the start. The tunnel (W/SW) and the pool room (E/SE) are not in the opening sightline. PS12’s remake directions: a quarterpipe and the curved corner rail to the right; the pool room “down and to the left”; a door between two quarterpipes across from the start.

## Collectibles

The original competition has **no collectibles**. The remake adds 2 stat points per style, a Vicarious Visions logo (above the quarterpipe on top of the central deck) and an alien plushie (in the pool room, the original’s secret room). The section closes with the original’s three **signature hard-to-reach spots**.

**VV — Vicarious Visions logo** (remake)

1. `transfer` spawn → hall floor — drop the roll-in

- Difficulty 3: two stages (onto the deck, then build speed on a small structure)
- Why: The central landmark’s top is the vertical reach target, and the logo crowns the object that every major gap uses.
- Evidence: PS12 roswell; G1 p132 (confidence medium).

**POOLROOM — Secret pool room and Pool Grind** (original)

1. `transfer` hall → SE corner of the big halfpipe — follow the halfpipe to its corner
2. `transfer` halfpipe channel → pool room — through the doors; Channel Gap 500 if you air the gap and land on the transition beyond (G1 p134)
3. `vert-air` pool → pool — session the pool; “an easy channel to ollie over” (G1 p132)
4. `grind` pool coping at the channel → all the way round — grind the whole coping from the channel back to the channel, ollying the channel if needed: Pool Grind 2000 (+ Channel Gap)

- Launch from: the channel at the pool-room entrance. Distance from first sighting: unknown.
- Skill gates: finding the room; long balance on a coping grind with an ollie mid-grind. Coupling: none.
- Failure and recovery: Falling off the coping drops you into the pool; restart the lap.
- Difficulty 4: the full lap is a long balance with a channel ollie
- Why: The hidden room holds one of the two highest-value gaps (2000): exploration pays more than the open hall.
- Evidence: G1 p132, p134; VG labels (confidence high).

**ETRAILS — Tunnel to the ET Grind triple rail** (original)

1. `ollie` hall → tunnel — “head into the tunnel on your right”; slow down (G1 p131)
2. `ollie` tunnel → ET operation room — pass the alien behind glass (LP1 0:29:36–0:29:40)
3. `grind` second set of doors → short rail — immediately jump into a grind
4. `rail-transfer` short rail → next rail — pop a kick trick into a different grind
5. `rail-transfer` next rail → final rail (curves right) — kick trick into the last rail; ride it to the end: ET Grind 1000

- Launch from: tunnel exit. Distance from first sighting: immediately outside the doors.
- Skill gates: speed control; rail-to-rail transfers. Coupling: none.
- Failure and recovery: A missed rail ends the combo on the floor; the tunnel can be re-entered.
- Difficulty 3: tight set-up window right after a corridor
- Why: A narrative room works as a speed-control corridor in front of a tight rail chain; G1 quotes 30k–50k (≈ 80k with special grinds) for this line.
- Evidence: G1 p129, p131, p133; LP1 0:29:30–0:29:46 (confidence medium (rail position vs. VG label unresolved)).

**MBE — MB Emerson Grind onto the B-House rail** (original (not in PS12))

1. `transfer` side halfpipe → high deck — get onto the deck
2. `vert-air` deck-top quarterpipe → air — press up at its lip
3. `transfer` air → over the flat and over the lip of the next ramp — clear the flat below and the next ramp’s lip
4. `grind` air → B-House rail — land in a grind: MB Emerson Grind 2000

- Launch from: quarterpipe on top of the high deck. Distance from first sighting: unknown.
- Skill gates: long press-up transfer; landing a grind at the end of a long air. Coupling: none.
- Failure and recovery: Short: you land in the ramp or on the flat; no reset.
- Difficulty 5: G1: “you’re going to have to be an acrobat”
- Why: The landing target of the longest transfer is a rail, not a ramp: precision at the end of maximum distance.
- Evidence: G1 p134; VG label (confidence high).

## Guidance and sightlines

- Night scene with floodlights on poles: the light-blue central deck is the brightest surface and draws the eye from the roll-in (LP1 0:27:39–0:27:43).
- White chevrons on the kickers and halfpipe walls point the direction of travel (LP1 frames; VG).
- “NO STEP” markings on the hatch and decks are a joke that also marks raised surfaces (VG).
- The tunnel is lit green and the alien sits behind glass: a show-room that rewards entering the corridor (LP1 0:29:36–0:29:40).
- Hidden on purpose: the pool room is behind doors in a halfpipe corner, out of the main sightline (G1 calls it a secret room).
- Remake: neon-lit hangar look (LP12 0:36:55–0:38:25); stat points float over ramps near spawn.

## Design logic

1. The final competition summarises vert: a roll-in channel, two halfpipes flanking a deck, a wrap-around halfpipe and a pool room. Street pockets (the deck rail, ET rails, B-House rails) hang off the vert structures rather than standing alone.
2. One central object scores many ways: over it (High Deck 1000, Low Deck 500), off its kickers (Deck Gap 1500), along its rail (Deck Grind 800), out of its top quarterpipe onto a far rail (MB Emerson 2000), and in the remake across its top (Cover Up 1000) and onto its hook rail (Nasty in the Pasty 1000).
3. Tony’s favourite spot is the High Deck Gap: the level’s signature move is clearing its central landmark.
4. The hidden room pays the most: the pool room holds a 2000-point gap, as much as the hardest open-hall gap.
5. A narrative room controls speed. The alien operation room sits in the corridor before the ET rails, and G1 tells you to slow down because the set-up after the doors is “extremely quick”.
6. Rhythm rails with variation recur twice: three rails out of the tunnel (the last curving right) and three rails at staggered heights on the back deck.
7. The longest transfer ends on a rail (MB Emerson). Precision comes at the end of maximum distance, not at the start.
8. The start gives speed: the roll-in feeds the ramp opposite, and G1’s line spends that speed on 720 grabs at once to fill the special meter.
9. Lighting is guidance in a dark level: floodlit deck, green tunnel, dark corners for the secret room.
10. The remake keeps the alien theme as gap anchors (Grey Grind, “Roswell That End’s Well”, a UFO in the pool room) and puts the plushie in the original’s secret room.
11. The remake puts the logo on top of the central deck: the most-used structure is crowned with the collectible.
12. Failure is cheap in each heat (falls land in halfpipes or on the flat), but the stakes rise across the career: this medal completes the set needed for the ending (G1 p135).
13. TAS1 clears the competition with two very short heats (5:32–5:50): judging scores the run’s best tricks, so the level needs a few dense combo spots near spawn.

## Original vs remake

- ET Grind is renamed Grey Grind (same three-rail description).
- MB Emerson Grind is not in PS12’s gap list.
- New gaps: Nasty in the Pasty (hook-shaped rail), Cover Up (manual on the deck-top quarterpipe), Roswell That End’s Well (manual past the alien).
- The pool room gains a UFO and the alien plushie; stat points and a VV logo are added (PS12).
- Look: the remake renders a neon-lit hangar; LP12 shows the “Overtime!” finish (0:37:50).

## Transfer to this game

1. Hidden pool room: put the crystal on the far coping of a pool inside a side room, behind doors at a halfpipe corner. It is taken during a full-lap coping grind that needs one ollie over the entrance channel (the 2.1 m grind snap helps re-catch the coping).
2. Corridor before a rail chain: a short themed room (show-piece behind glass) forces a slow, straight approach. Right outside its doors, three rails in a row, the last curving. The crystal sits at the end of the third rail.
3. Crown the central deck: the crystal floats above a short quarterpipe built on top of the level’s central deck. Getting onto the deck is the first gate (press-up transfer from a side halfpipe), building speed on the small structure the second.
4. Rail at the end of the longest air: a crystal on a rail beyond a ramp lip, reachable only by a long transfer from the deck-top quarterpipe. Tune the distance to a pumped vert air, and make the landing a grind rail, not a ramp.
