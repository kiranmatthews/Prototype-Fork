- **Ground**: plywood floor with low street objects (rails, low walls, funboxes, decks).
- **Raised decks**: the elevated tables area (NE), the shop deck (E), the Element deck.
- **Vert**: the big halfpipe, the pool, low halfpipes, the NW corner quarterpipe and the huge wave wall.
- **Overhead**: the hanging rail off the wave-wall top and the railing over the bridge.
- **Secret areas**: two outdoor yards behind doors that open by grinding key rails (van yard W, rail yard S).
- **Loops**: halfpipe ↔ pool; a manual-linked circuit of the hall; the wave wall as a turnaround.

Landmarks: huge wave-wall quarterpipe; “Skate Street Ventura” sign over the NW corner quarterpipe; Birdhouse sign on the pool; camper van in the outdoor yard; daylight and trees beyond the yard doors.

## Spawn and first view

You start on a raised roll-in at the E-centre of the hall (VG START). PS12 describes the huge quarterpipe (the wave wall) as “ahead from the start position” and the curved yellow rail, which is the van key, as behind you. LP2 heat 2 shows the natural opener: down the roll-in, straight across the floor, a big air on the wave wall and a $100 icon 8 s after the start. The van key rail (K1) is the closest icon, but you must turn around to see it. The NW corner icon ($250) and both yards are out of the opening view.

## Collectibles

THPS2 competitions have no tape or letters; the invite card makes the **cash icons** the completion goal. Skatestreet has 8 icons worth $1000. Its structure is a **lock and key**: two outdoor yards open when you grind specific rails, and the two keys and two yards together hold four of the eight icons. In this competition park, the yards are what a tape would be in a career level. The remake drops cash icons and hides its logo and plushie inside the same two yards.

**K1 — Cash icon $50** (original, value $50)

1. `ollie` spawn → curved yellow rail behind you — turn around at the start (PS12)
2. `grind` curved yellow rail → its far end — grind it all the way: Van Secret Area Key; the door to the van yard (W) opens

- Launch from: the curved rail. Distance from first sighting: ≈ 3–8.
- Skill gates: full-length grind of a curved rail. Coupling: opens the van yard door.
- Failure and recovery: Falling off before the end does not open the door; regrind (seconds).
- Difficulty 2: balance on a curve; next to spawn
- Why: The easy key carries its own icon, so the first lock teaches itself with a reward.
- Evidence: VG; LP2 0:45:10–0:45:11; PS12 (confidence high).

## Guidance and sightlines

- The roll-in points at the wave wall, the hall’s biggest structure, which also holds two icons and the rail key.
- The “Skate Street Ventura” sign sits above the NW corner quarterpipe and marks the High Sticker icon’s height (LP2 0:43:51).
- Keys announce themselves after the fact: “Van Secret Area Key” and “Rail Secret Area Key” appear in the trick string when you grind the right rail (LP2 0:45:11, 0:45:58).
- Opened doors show daylight and trees, which contrast with the dark indoor hall (LP2 0:46:03, 0:46:31).
- Cash pickups print “50 bucks”, “100 bucks!” or “250 bucks!!!”; the last one prints “Cash icons cleared” (LP2).
- Hidden on purpose: both key rails look like ordinary rails, and nothing in the hall says which one is a key.

## Design logic

1. An indoor plywood park packed tight enough for manual linking. LP2’s heats chain wallride → crooked grind → manuals → quarterpipe airs → lipslides into × 11 combos, which teaches THPS2’s new verb.
2. Two locked outdoor yards, each opened by grinding a specific rail. “Grind X to open Y” is the lock grammar, and the key is itself a named gap.
3. Locks are graded and placed at opposite ends: the easy key sits right behind spawn, the hard key on the biggest structure (wave-wall top → hanging rail).
4. Keys persist across heats (LP2 opens the van door in heat 2 and uses it in heat 3), so an unlock is progress you keep. One heat can unlock, another can loot.
5. Each key carries an icon ($50 on the easy key, $100 over the hard key). The lock teaches itself and pays a deposit up front.
6. The two $250 icons show the two routes to value: pure skill (High Sticker, maximum vert under a sign) and exploration (Shoot the Gap, behind the hard lock).
7. The icons tour every zone: two yards, two key routes, and four in the main hall (corner, bridge, wave, east stairs).
8. Reward yards are themed content: the van yard adds a van, benches and a slope quarterpipe, which means three new gaps plus an icon.
9. Completion feedback is explicit: “Cash icons cleared” on the last pickup.
10. Gap values are low and jokey (Over the Wall 10, Rail Secret Area Key 50). In this park, gaps name the lines and score comes from combos.
11. The wave wall is the hub landmark: Surfin U.S.A., Wave Wall Minigap, Ride the Wave, Railing Hop, the rail key and two icons.
12. The remake hides both of its secret collectibles behind the original’s locks (logo in the van yard, plushie on the rail yard’s skinny wall), so the old keys gain new value.
13. Failure is cheap: falls land on plywood, and the yards are dead ends a few seconds from the hall. LP2 still took gold while spending heat 3 on yards and keys.

## Original vs remake

- Cash icons: PS12 lists none; the remake’s collectibles are 2 stat points per style, the VV logo (van yard) and the alien plushie (rail yard); LP12’s panel shows “Stat Points 0/2”.
- Both keys are kept, with the same rails (PS12).
- New gaps: Over The HP (needs near-full stats), Funbox Slap and Over The Barrier (both in the rail yard).
- Two stat points per style are placed behind the rail key (vert and park).
- LP12’s run ends “You win the silver!” (1:04:49): the remake’s judging is not trivial.

## Transfer to this game

1. Key rail behind spawn: a trick rail directly behind the spawn camera opens a side yard. The crystal floats above a parked van in the yard and is reached from a launch ramp. Set the van height so a flat ollie (≈ 1.78 m) cannot clear it, but the ramp can.
2. Wave wall → hanging rail: grind the top of a huge quarterpipe and grind-ollie (≈ 2.4 m pop) onto a hanging rail that opens a back-door yard. The crystal floats over the gap between two quarterpipes on the yard’s back wall and is collected by a transfer.
3. High sticker: the crystal sits at the maximum pumped vert height above a corner quarterpipe, with a sign or banner above as the height marker. A miss costs one circuit.
4. Persistent locks: if this game restarts runs, keep opened trick gates open, so a crystal attempt never has to repeat the unlock.
5. Skinny-wall perch: the crystal on top of a narrow wall in a yard, reached by grinding the wall from a quarterpipe and ollieing out onto its top. Face the crystal away from the approach so the player must turn round (as the remake’s plushie does).
