Circulation: the park is one loop inside a street loop. The Ghetto Pool is a dead end across the street.

## Spawn and first view

Spawn is on the start deck under the red QUIKSILVER banner on the near edge of the park, facing in (TAS3 2:15; LP3 0:21:25). The deck drops into curving ramps with green rails (Over the Break / Over the Bank); a half pipe is to the left and rails lead right into the pool (G3 p060). The opening line goes straight off the start structure; the TAS needs nothing else.

Visible from spawn: the pool, the funboxes and the ring of poles. The Ghetto Pool is not visible: it is behind buildings across the street.

## Guidance and sightlines

- The park is a square inside a street and a ring of power lines: the boundary is visible and grindable, so the lap is the obvious line (G3 p059).
- Quiksilver banners and flags mark the start and two funboxes; a G3 stat point and a gap (Ghetto Extension) are on the flag.
- The spectator platform reads as fragile ("dilapidated", "termites") and invites contact; touching its ladder changes it.
- The secret pool is outside the course, across the street, behind a fence that is already broken: the break is the clue (G3 p063).
- Every stat-point set puts one point on the power lines (High Wire Act): the overhead ring is the competition's hidden layer.
- Remake: cash floats over gaps and slopes; the logo is on a wire above a QP pair, inviting a spine transfer.

## Design logic

1. Competitions have no tape and no letters (G3 p059; PS34 all-tapes list omits Rio). The collectibles are optional (stat points, decks) and sit on or near the scoring lines, so finding them costs time in a judged heat.
2. The park is built for an endless lap: perimeter rails, short walls, corner guardrails, a bus stop and humps let a skater grind and manual round the course "for combos worth hundreds of thousands of points" (G3 p059, p063).
3. One loop per layer: the ground lap (rails and walls), the vert loop (pool, spine, roll-ins), and the overhead ring (power lines and red cables).
4. The only secret area is a deck shrine: all three decks are above the hidden pool across the street. Hiding all the decks in one place rewards one discovery (G3 p064).
5. An event inside a judged heat: the platform collapse changes the lap mid-competition and adds a wreckage ledge (G3 p060).
6. Bails cost more than time: the TAS wins with two 3-second heats of one clean combo each (TAS3 2:15-2:30). Judges score points and bails, not exploration.
7. Gap values grade risk: 500-point gaps are the long airs and high grinds (Over the Platform, Big Tunnel Gap, High Wire Act, Red Line Grind, Take It to da Bridge!), 50-point gaps are small transfers (G3 p065-p068).
8. Stat points are spread so each skater group explores a different part: trees and planters (Hawk), street-side sitting areas (Caballero), spine and flag (Campbell), platform and pool channel (Glifberg) (G3 p062).
9. Street furniture as launchers: brick planters, flower-garden borders and a planter slope pop the skater into trees and onto the wires (G3 p062; J3 p014). The competition teaches "pop off a planter" as the way up.
10. Each stat-point set mixes layers (one or two on the wires, one in a tree or over a planter, one over the pool or a QP, one on the street side), so a single heat can take some without leaving the lap (G3 p062).
11. The PS1 edition keeps the square-in-a-street plan, the ring of poles and wires and the central pool (VG): the perimeter loop is the part of the design that survived the hardware cut.

## Original vs remake

THPS 3+4 keeps Rio as a medal competition. The PS1 edition is a simplified park.

- Still a medal competition; no tape or letters added (PS34 rio; PS34 all-tapes list).
- Platinum pro goal: Christ Air over the bank by the start (the Over the Bank gap) and place first (PS34).
- Added collectibles: cash ×3, hidden deck in the hidden pool (smash rickety boards), Iron Galaxy logo on a wire above a QP pair (spine transfer, catch the wire on the way down), panda in a patio across the road, stat points ×3 (PS34).
- PS1 edition (VG): same idea, simpler park: a square park inside a perimeter street with poles and wires, a central oval pool with a long box, planters, a white spectator tent, curved corner rails; the annex across the street is an alley with laundry lines and a stat point; no hidden pool is drawn clearly.

## Transfer to this game

1. Hidden yard crystal: across the street from a square park, a walled yard behind a broken fence holds an empty pool; the crystal floats above its deep end, reached by pumping the pool and a vert air of ≈ 4–5 m.
2. Ring layer: string power lines on poles round the park perimeter at ≈ 4–5 m; the crystal sits on a span between two poles, reached by a kicker or planter-slope launch into a grind snap and held through a corner.
3. Collapse to reveal: a rickety platform whose ladder breaks when touched drops onto a lower ledge; the wreckage forms a new grindable line that leads to the crystal.
4. Spine-to-wire catch: two quarter pipes back to back under a wire; spine-transfer and snap onto the wire on the way down (2.1 m snap radius) to ride to a crystal at its end.
