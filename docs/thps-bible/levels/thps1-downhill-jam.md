Vertical layers: the main path (ground); rails, pipes and ledges (≈ 1–4 m); the high road (natural rock
bridge, the dam top, the canyon rim and arch, ≈ 5–15 m above the path below it, low confidence); rock
pillars (V4 and the tape podium). Vert: the dam halfpipe, quarter pipes along the path walls, the finish
pool. No roofs except the generator building. Secret areas: none closed; the "secret" is the high road.

Loops: none. The course is one-way; G1 p099 warns "You can't fight the hill": going back uphill is possible
only in short stretches (G1's alternative for valve 3: "turn around and head back up the hill to the
Mountain Dew sign"; valve 5: turn round before the finish).

## Spawn and first view

Spawn: Z1, at the START banner (VG 1258, 108), facing down the first hill (east on VG). LP1 16:56 (timer 1:59)
and TAS1 3:19.5 show the start banner, the first slope, the path walls and the tip "GRIND EVERY LIP AND EDGE".
The hip on the right and the two high rails behind it are the first thing in view (G1 p102: "head down the
first hill, and line yourself up with the hip on the right side of the path").

The tape is not visible from spawn: it is ≈ 150 m away in a straight line and ≈ 300 m along the course,
below the dam and behind terrain. The remake hides its alien plushie behind the spawn ("At the very start,
turn around", PS12).

What the opening invites: the course itself. Gravity provides speed; the first rails are the level's biggest
combo (G1 p102: 12,000–18,000 points from the hip → high rails → valve 1 → curved rail sequence).

Route (designed line; G1 p106–p108 read from the page images; LP1 17:45–18:12):

1. **Reach the dam and take A** (vert-air). Valve 3 is on the rail at the dam's upper end; G1's reliable way
   is back up to the Mountain Dew sign, then the small ramp against the left wall and the long rail over the
   path onto the dam top (G1 p103; LP1 17:45). Then
   "catch air along the quarterpipe on the back side of the dam" for A (G1 p105; LP1 17:49–17:50). Tony's
   version: "hit the rail that goes over the beginning of the halfpipe so that you're set up to get the
   special tape on the triple-rail podium" (G1 p099).
2. **Come back down, turn right, grind the rail across the path** (grind). "At the bottom end of the platform
   you're on, you'll see a rail that extends back across the path, and takes you to another plateau" (G1
   p106). Caption, p107: "Getting the secret tape starts here when you get up on that rail running across
   the top of the halfpipe section."
3. **Hop mid-rail and grind again** (rail-transfer): "hop up and throw an impossible in the middle and grind
   again" (G1 p106). On the other side of the pipe, further down, "take the second rail back across. … Try
   pressing down to slow yourself before you attempt the ollie" (G1 p107).
4. **Air over gap 1** (transfer): "hit the transition, ollie, bust a 360 something, and air over the first
   small gap in the path. As soon as you land, steer left a bit to avoid bouncing off the rail (no big deal if
   you do, just don't go over the cliff)" (G1 p106).
5. **Ollie onto the natural arch and head right** (ollie): a darkened patch of rock marks the line, worn "from
   all the skaters who have passed before you and all the times the development team skated through here"
   (G1 p106). Fastplant impossible through the 500 icon floating over the arch (LP1 18:00 "SWITCH BONUS 500").
6. **Carry over gap 2 off a quarter pipe** (transfer): stay about a third of the way from the left quarter
   pipe, "Angle towards the quarter pipe, and try to hit it right before the second gap in the path. You want
   to hit it at a shallow angle so your speed will carry you across the gap. As soon as you come down, steer
   left" (G1 p106–p107). LP1 18:06–18:08: big air, "720 switch varial + stalefish".
7. **Line up on the plateau** (ground): "angle yourself towards the right end corner of the plateau" (G1
   p107); "Across the rock bridge get lined up for another gap. You will only have seconds to make
   adjustments before the leap" (G1 p108).
8. **Fastplant across gap 3 onto the podium** (boneless, then grind): "fastplant across the gap to the four
   rails atop the rock pillar out in the middle of the path. Hold the grind button, and if you hit any of the
   rails, you're home free" (G1 p107). LP1 18:11: FS boardslide, "HIDDEN TAPE!".
9. **Drop back to the main line and go for valve 4** (ollie): slide off, "Bust a 360 trick as you fall back
   down to the lower level, and continue straight down the middle of the path", launch out of the gully onto
   the large rock pillar (G1 p103). LP1 18:16 "4 OF 5 VALVES".

Remake line (PS12): "Use the left-hand ramp before the dam to grind the high rail. Follow along and grind
another rail to the other side. Use the quarter pipe or wallride and ollie over the gap. Then, get up onto
the bridge and follow it to the right. Use another quarter pipe to cross the next gap. Finally, use the big
ramp to get over to the tape, above some rails." The same high road; the remake names a ramp for the last gap.

TAS1 line (N64, 3:33–3:38): valve 3 on the long rail at 3:33, A plus the pro score on the dam's quarter pipe at
3:35, then a single switch christ air from the dam over and through the round natural arch, landing in a
switch 50-50 on the stepped podium rails at 3:38: the whole high road skipped in one air.

## Guidance and sightlines

- **The course is the guide.** One path, walls on both sides, a start banner and a finish banner. Every goal
  is on or just above that path, in course order.
- **Landmarks.** The dam, the reservoir, the Mountain Dew billboards, the generator building, the yellow/black
  hazard rails. The tape's branch is at the dam, the most visible structure.
- **Breadcrumbs on the high road.** A on the dam's back quarter pipe marks where to leave the main line; a
  500 icon floats over the natural arch; a darkened strip of worn rock marks the arch line (G1 p106); the
  tape glows over yellow rails in the middle of the canyon.
- **Target in a bowl.** From the high road the tape sits in the centre of a round canyon, visible across the
  whole curved approach (thumbsticks: "the centre of the toilet bowl").
- **Hidden on purpose.** The way onto the high road is a rail "that extends back across the path", easy to
  read as decoration of the main line. The tape is invisible from the start and from the main line upstream.
- **The camera and the hill.** At downhill speed the player has "only seconds to make adjustments before the
  leap" (G1 p108): the level shows the target late and briefly, so knowledge of the route matters more than
  reading it in the moment.

## Design logic

1. **Course order is goal order.** On a one-way course every goal is a station: valves V1–V5 and the letters
   S-K-A-T-E appear in course order, the letters even in spelling order. The level teaches its goal list by
   simply running down the hill.
2. **The tape is a parallel high road.** It branches at the dam (marked by A) and rejoins the main line at the
   line for valve 4. The player who takes it misses nothing on the main line below.
3. **On a hill, speed is free and control is the test.** Unlike a park, there is no speed problem to solve;
   the guide's instructions are about braking, steering after landings and angles ("press down to slow",
   "steer left", "hit it at a shallow angle", "only seconds to make adjustments").
4. **No retry within a run.** A miss drops the skater below the branch and the hill cannot be climbed back:
   the tape is lost for the run. That is the main reason it is the hardest tape in THPS1 (G1 p099, p122).
5. **A big catch area at the end.** Four parallel rails on the pillar, "if you hit any of the rails, you're
   home free" (G1 p107): after three precise gaps, the final landing is forgiving.
6. **The tape's exit sets up the next goal.** Sliding off the podium lines the skater up with the middle of
   the path for valve 4 (G1 p103): the tape route is part of the main line's rhythm, not a dead end.
7. **The target is shown across a curve.** Putting the tape in the centre of a round canyon lets the player
   see it for the whole approach along the rim; at downhill speed that is the only way to give a long look.
8. **Wear marks as guidance.** The darkened rock on the arch is a deliberate path cue, presented in the guide
   as the trace of the developers' own runs.
9. **The expert line is one air.** The TAS clears the high road with a single christ air from the dam
   quarter pipe over the arch onto the podium (3 s). The designed route is the long, safe way round a
   shortcut that the geometry permits.
10. **Few named gaps.** The original names only three gap types; the tape's three gaps are unnamed. On this
    level the goals, not the gap list, carry the design.
11. **Risk on the main line too.** The Huge Water Hazard ("the death gap") and the ski jump are big, scored
    risks on the main path; the tape adds a hidden risk above it.
12. **Difficulty peak of the career.** Downhill Jam comes sixth; the next career level, Streets, has a tape
    the guide ranks second only to this one. Earlier tapes were one-trick or one-area routes.

## Transfer to this game

1. **High road on a one-way course.** After a vert section (a dam halfpipe), branch an elevated path (a rail
   across the main path, a second rail back, an arch) that runs ≈ 5–10 m above the main line for ≈ 100 m and
   ends at a crystal over a rail cluster on a pillar; rejoin the main line at the next objective. A miss drops
   the player onto the main line, so nothing else is lost.
2. **Control, not speed.** On downhill stretches near this game's 23 m/s maximum, make the challenge braking
   and steering: rail-to-rail hops within a grind ollie's ≈ 2.4 m pop, a quarter pipe hit at a shallow angle
   to carry across a ≈ 6–8 m gap, landings that require an immediate steer away from a drop.
3. **Generous catch.** Put 3–4 parallel rails on the pillar, ≈ 1.5–2 m apart so this game's 2.1 m grind snap
   catches any of them, and hang the crystal ≈ 0.5–1 m above the rail line so the grind itself collects it.
4. **Target in a bowl.** Place the crystal in the centre of a round canyon and route the approach along the
   rim, so the target stays on screen for the whole curve.
5. **Breadcrumbs.** A floating bonus over the arch line and a worn-texture strip on the correct path; the
   level's third letter (or a shard) marks the branch point.
6. **Expert shortcut.** Allow a spine transfer or big vert air from the dam's quarter pipe straight over the
   arch onto the podium (≈ 12–14 m, near the charged-ollie maximum), as the TAS does.
7. **Cheaper retry.** Unlike THPS's one-way loss, add a rope swing or moving platform from the main line back
   up to the branch for a single retry, or accept the loss on the hardest difficulty.
