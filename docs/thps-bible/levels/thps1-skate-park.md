- **Ground**: concrete floor with the street strip (wall + rail ledge, rail platforms) and the funbox corner.
- **Ledges and walkways**: the NE walkway (spawn side) and the back walkway above the pool. Both are launch decks for gaps.
- **Vert**: the long halfpipe with a spine quarterpipe behind it, a small quarterpipe at its W end, and wall quarterpipes along the N and E walls.
- **Bowl**: the U-shaped kidney pool with a hip.
- **Overhead**: four ceiling girders with hanging lights. This is the only layer above head height and it has no stair or ramp; you reach it from the large spine quarterpipe at full speed.
- **Secret areas**: none in the original. The remake adds a reachable room roof on the ledge behind spawn.
- **Loops**: halfpipe ↔ spine quarterpipe (pumping); rail-between-two-quarterpipes speed loop; walkway → pool → walkway.

Landmarks: NEVERSOFT sign on the W wall; Skate Street sign on the NE wall; four black ceiling girders; kidney pool with yellow-black coping; long wooden halfpipe under orange hanging lights.

## Spawn and first view

You start on the east side near the NE walkway, facing roughly W/SW across the hall (VG START; PS12 directions). Straight ahead is the two-level wall + rail ledge; the halfpipe is ahead-left, the pool ahead-right, the funbox and box to the left, the walkway to the right, and a small red quarterpipe with a ledge behind you (remake). G1 tells you to hold the ollie during the countdown so the first jump reaches the high rail. LP1 does exactly that: 50-50 + 50-50 on the double rail at 0:10:40, the small quarterpipe at 0:10:41, a 540 + Rocket Air transfer into the halfpipe at 0:10:44. The ceiling girders are in view from the first frame; the room roof behind you is not. TAS1 instead does one ≈ 3 s combo off the spawn area (switch fastplant + wallride + kickflip + FS boardslide) and ends the heat.

## Collectibles

The original competition has **no collectibles**: no tape, no letters, no goal objects. The goal is the judged contest. The remake adds three kinds of pickups: stat points (two per style), one Vicarious Visions logo and one alien plushie. The logo and plushie are true hard-to-reach secrets: the logo sits at the far end of the ceiling girder (vert route) and the plushie on a room roof behind spawn (street route). Because the original has no collectible, the section ends with the two **signature hard-to-reach spots** the PS1 design builds toward. These are the routes a crystal would use.

**VV — Vicarious Visions logo** (remake)

1. `vert-air` halfpipe → halfpipe decks — pump the halfpipe for maximum speed (G1 adds a rail between the front-room QP and the large QP as a speed loop)
2. `spine-transfer` quarterpipe on the outside of the halfpipe → rafter girder — ollie off the lip at an angle so the air carries up over the spine onto the beam (LP12 0:13:38 “Spine Transfer”)
3. `grind` girder → girder, heading right — land in a grind; if you land heading left, wallplant against the wall to reverse (PS12)
4. `grind` girder → logo — grind the whole length of the rafter; the logo is at the end of the run (PS12)

- Launch from: quarterpipe on the outside of the halfpipe (the spine). Distance from first sighting: unknown.
- Skill gates: pump a halfpipe to full speed; angled spine transfer; land a grind on a beam out of vert air; wallplant to reverse direction (remake verb). Coupling: none.
- Failure and recovery: A short air drops you into the halfpipe or onto the flat with no reset; you can re-pump at once. Landing the beam the wrong way is recoverable with a wallplant.
- Difficulty 4: maximum speed plus an angled transfer onto a narrow beam; the long beam and the wallplant reverse soften it
- Why: The remake hangs its hidden logo on the original’s hardest scored line (Rafter Rail 1000 / Over The Rafters 2000): the highest layer in the hall, reached by the level’s signature transfer, then a full traverse to the far end.
- Evidence: PS12 skate-park; LP12 0:13:38–0:14:00; G1 p084 (Rafter Rail) (confidence medium).

**RAFTER — Rafter Rail / Over The Rafters (ceiling girders)** (original)

1. `grind` front-room quarterpipe → rail between it and the large quarterpipe — hit this rail on every pass to build speed (G1 p083)
2. `vert-air` large quarterpipe → lip — press up at the lip to transfer toward the halfpipe (G1 p084)
3. `grind` air → girder — fast enough: land on and grind the girders; ollie to other girder sections for multiple grinds (G1 p084)
4. `grind` girder → over the pool — grind the length of the girders over the pool, ollying over the rafters; handplants on the rafters are possible (G1 p084)
5. `ollie` girder → floor — ollie down with several spins to raise the multiplier (G1 p081)

- Launch from: the large quarterpipe butted against the halfpipe. Distance from first sighting: ≈ 15–30 (VG scale).
- Skill gates: speed loop: rail + two quarterpipes; press-up lip transfer; landing a grind from vert air. Coupling: none.
- Failure and recovery: Too slow: you only transfer into the halfpipe (Transfer 200 / Over The Pipe 700); no reset or damage.
- Difficulty 5: the hardest speed requirement in the park; Over The Rafters (2000) needs even more speed
- Why: The top scoring layer of the park is literally the top layer: the further up the overhead structure you get, the more it pays (Light Grind 500 → Rafter Rail 1000 → Over The Rafters 2000).
- Evidence: G1 p081, p083–p084; VG labels (confidence high).

**WALKPOOL — Acid Drop / Pool 2 Walkway loop** (original)

1. `transfer` halfpipe / ramps → back walkway — get up to the walkway (G1 p081)
2. `acid-drop` walkway → pool — ollie with a trick and a spin into the pool: Acid Drop 1000
3. `hip` pool → pool — session the pool, transfer over the hip: Pool Hip 500 (mind the pole)
4. `transfer` pool back wall → walkway — press up at the lip with enough speed to land on the walkway: Pool 2 Walkway 700

- Launch from: the back walkway (reached from the halfpipe or ramps). Distance from first sighting: ≈ 30–40.
- Skill gates: acid drop; press-up transfer out of a bowl. Coupling: none.
- Failure and recovery: Missing the walkway leaves you in the pool; the loop restarts from the pool.
- Difficulty 3: two directed airs between a deck and a bowl
- Why: A two-way gap pair teaches that the same edge is both a launch and a landing.
- Evidence: G1 p081, p083; VG labels (confidence high).

## Guidance and sightlines

- The spawn camera frames the two-level wall + rail ledge: the first scripted line is the first thing you see (LP1 0:10:34).
- Girders and hanging lights cross the whole ceiling and stay in view from the halfpipe: the overhead goal is always visible but out of reach until you have speed.
- Bright orange floors (halfpipe, rail platforms, funbox) separate skate objects from the dark concrete (VG render).
- Gap names describe the target (“Over The Rafters”, “Light Grind”, “Pool 2 Walkway”), so the gap list is also a hint list.
- No HUD markers in the original: the competition’s style/difficulty/variety judging is the only push to visit every element.
- Remake: style-specific stat points float over the ramps near spawn; the rafter stat point sits in the path of the beam grind (LP12 0:13:40).
- Hidden on purpose: the ledge and room behind spawn (remake plushie) are outside the opening view.

## Design logic

1. One room holds the whole skill set: halfpipe with spine (vert), kidney pool with hip (bowl), wall + rail ledge, elevated rails and a funbox (street). Variety judging rewards touching all of them, so the layout spreads one example of each element type around the hall.
2. Spawn faces a designed line. G1 even scripts the input: hold the ollie during the countdown to reach the high rail. The first second of play is a lesson.
3. Height is the scoring ladder: Transfer 200 and HP Transfer 250 at the coping, Over The Pipe 700 across the halfpipe, Light Grind 500 on the lowest overhead object, Rafter Rail 1000 on the girders, Over The Rafters 2000 above them.
4. The hardest place is overhead and reachable from only one launcher (the large quarterpipe on the spine), and only at full speed. A single gate keeps the secret readable.
5. The level supplies its own speed source: a rail between two quarterpipes that you hit every pass (G1 p083). A hard-to-reach spot needs a nearby, repeatable way to build speed.
6. The girders run on over the pool, so the overhead layer is also a route between the vert zone and the bowl zone (G1 p084).
7. Two-way gap pairs (Acid Drop in, Pool 2 Walkway out) teach that a ledge next to a bowl is both a launch and a landing.
8. One object, three verbs: the rail platforms are ground into from the walkway (700), from the pool (1000) or cleared entirely (Whoop Gap 1000). Approach, not object count, makes variety.
9. The remake puts its hardest collectibles (vert stat point and VV logo) on the original’s hardest line, the rafters, and adds wallplant as a recovery for landing the beam the wrong way.
10. The remake hides its other collectible behind the camera (room roof behind spawn, wallride + wallie). This pairs a vert secret with a street secret, and a far one with a near one.
11. Failure is cheap. A fall costs judges’ points, not position, and with three 1:00 heats where the best two count, one heat can be spent on a risky overhead attempt.
12. TAS1 wins gold with one ≈ 3 s combo per heat and only two heats. The judged score rewards a single strong combo, not time on the clock, so a competition park needs a few dense combo spots rather than a long route.
13. No collectibles in the original: the competition itself is the objective, and the named gaps and the overhead layer do the job of a tape (aspiration, a visible but hard target).

## Original vs remake

- Collectibles added to a former contest-only park: two stat points per style (vert, street, park), a Vicarious Visions logo and an alien plushie (PS12).
- Gap list: PS12 adds Across The Park (manual), Around The Bend (pool grind) and Wall Gap Jr.; Pipe 2 Box Grind is not in PS12’s list. Wall Gap is on the PS1 VG map but not in G1.
- The ceiling girders are lattice trusses in the remake and are skated with wallplant reversals (LP12 0:13:44–0:13:56).
- Competition rules restated: a medal for a top-3 finish, the final score is the average of the top two runs; LP12 shows the remake’s “Overtime!” finish on THPS1 parks.
- LP1 vs LP12: the PS1 run opens on the double rail; the LP12 heat includes a long halfpipe → spine transfer → rafter sequence (0:13:36–0:14:00), which is where the remake put its vert pickups. The remake loading card reads “That there bowl’s deeper than a couple …” (LP12 0:13:05; text partly cut off).

## Transfer to this game

1. Rafter crystal: put the crystal at the far end of a ceiling beam above a halfpipe. The only way up is a spine transfer from a quarterpipe backed onto the halfpipe, at pumped maximum speed. A miss lands in the halfpipe and costs seconds. Tune the beam height in-engine so that cruise speed reaches only the lower rung (below).
2. Ladder of rungs: hang a light or a short bar under the beam about 2.4 m below it, so a grind-ollie from the rung (≈ 2.4 m pop) reaches the beam. This gives an intermediate step that teaches the vertical line before the full transfer.
3. Speed loop beside the goal: place a rail between two quarterpipes on the approach (as G1’s Over The Rafters setup does) or a speed pad, so the player can repeat the build-up without crossing the level.
4. Behind-spawn roof: a small quarterpipe up to a ledge behind the spawn camera, then wallride + wallie onto a room roof. Use it as the crystal for an easier level, or as the tease that shows the crystal’s glint from the roof.
5. Two-way bowl edge: a walkway beside a bowl with gaps in both directions. A crystal floating just past the walkway edge can only be taken by the vert air out of the bowl (Pool 2 Walkway), not by walking along the walkway.
