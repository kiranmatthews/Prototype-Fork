- **Ground**: an open flat between the bowls, with the Y-shaped spine ridge running through it.
- **Bowls**: the eye pool (deep, round), the U-shaped quarterpipe bowl, the rectangular 6-foot pool.
- **Vert walls and ledges**: the tall quarterpipe wall (the biggest launcher) and the three tiers of ledges along the back wall.
- **Decks**: the start deck under the bridge, and the barrier deck above the quarterpipe (Triple Rail).
- **Overhead**: the bridge girders between the pillars, with struts. This is the only layer above head height, and it is reached from the funbox with a charged ollie.
- **Secret areas**: none (no hidden rooms in the original or the remake).
- **Loops**: the U-quarterpipe/spine circuit (counter-clockwise), bowl-to-bowl transfers, and the Triple Rail ending back at the start obstacle.

Landmarks: giant eyeball painted in the round pool; Burnside Bridge deck and pillars overhead; yellow-black painted coping on every edge; ZERO skull sign by the start; port-a-potties and the street outside the fence.

## Spawn and first view

You start on a raised deck under the bridge in the SE corner, beside a pillar (VG START). LP1’s first frames look out under the bridge toward the street. The drop then runs past the pillar, the chain-link fence and the ZERO skull sign into the bowls, and reaches the eye pool in about 6 s (0:19:52–0:19:58). The bridge overhead is visible from the first frame; the girder line’s access (big quarterpipe → funbox) is not. PS12’s remake directions place the eyeball pool to the right, the white quarterpipe and bridge supports to the left, and “the pool” straight ahead. G1 adds that the 6-foot pool is the one furthest from you and the twinkie is to your right. Those directions do not all agree with the VG layout (see Open questions).

## Collectibles

The original competition has **no collectibles**. The remake adds 2 stat points per style, a Vicarious Visions logo and an alien plushie. The logo is the true hard-to-reach one: it sits inside a bridge support, where the original’s Bridge Gap threads between struts. The plushie is an easy near-spawn find at the foot of a support. Because the original has no pickups, the section closes with the PS1 design’s two **signature hard-to-reach spots**.

**GIRDER — Bridge Grind / Bridge Gap (the girder under the bridge)** (original)

1. `vert-air` big quarterpipe wall → transition — get speed off the big quarterpipe wall (G1 p113)
2. `kicker` transition → funbox on the right — aim for the funbox and hold the ollie to charge it
3. `grind` funbox → girder — ollie and land in a grind on the girder edge: Bridge Grind 800
4. `ollie` girder → girder past each strut — struts block the grind: ollie before each strut and keep the board straight
5. `ollie` funbox (alternative) → over the girder and through the struts — biggest ollie you can, no wheel contact with the girder: Bridge Gap 1000; also possible in reverse from the quarterpipe on the other side, with no landing transition

- Launch from: the funbox on the right after the big quarterpipe wall. Distance from first sighting: ≈ 10–20.
- Skill gates: charged ollie off a funbox; ollie-hops inside a grind. Coupling: none.
- Failure and recovery: Falling off the girder drops you onto the flat or into the bowl; no reset. The reverse Bridge Gap has no landing transition, so a bail is likely.
- Difficulty 4: charged ollie plus rhythmic hops over struts; the clean gap needs threading
- Why: The architecture of the real place (the bridge) becomes the top layer, and its struts turn a plain grind into a rhythm test.
- Evidence: G1 p113–p114; VG labels (confidence high).

**TIERS — Three-tier ledge descent** (original)

1. `vert-air` tall quarterpipe wall → top ledge — huge air and land in a grind on the top, unmarked ledge
2. `ollie` top ledge → top ledge beyond the break — ollie over the broken-away section
3. `ledge` top ledge → main yellow-black ledge — drop down to the long ledge on the back wall
4. `ledge` main ledge → lower yellow-black ledge on the right — ollie down again and grind the edge round and down the right side of the level
5. `transfer` ledge → bowl or vert wall — finish into the bowl, or reverse the line toward the pool and lip-grind the pool, then transfer out

- Launch from: huge air from the tall quarterpipe wall. Distance from first sighting: unknown.
- Skill gates: landing a grind from huge air; ollies inside a grind line. Coupling: none.
- Failure and recovery: Each break or drop is a place to fall off; you land lower in the same line and can keep grinding.
- Difficulty 4: starts from the biggest air in the park and asks for several precise hops
- Why: A staircase of ledges with deliberate breaks turns height into a long, descending combo; failure only drops you a tier.
- Evidence: G1 p112 (confidence medium (text only)).

## Guidance and sightlines

- The giant eyeball is the level’s compass: you can see it across the park, and three gaps are named after it.
- Every edge is painted yellow-black (VG): grindable coping and ledges read at a distance.
- The bridge deck, pillars and girders are overhead from the first frame (LP1 0:19:49); the girder grind is in view the whole run, while its access (funbox + charged ollie) is not obvious.
- Brand signage marks key objects: “flip” ads on the Triple Rail barriers, AXION banners on the pillars, the ZERO skull at the start (LP1).
- Remake: dark, rainy lighting (LP12 0:27:10 loading card “It’s raining, it’s pouring …”) makes the lit bowls stand out.
- Hidden on purpose: the top ledge tier is unpainted and broken, so it does not read as a grind line until you are up there.

## Design logic

1. A real park, game-ified. The layout follows the famous DIY park (bowls, the hip Tony names as his favourite) and adds game objects: the eyeball paint, the twinkie bump and a girder line under the bridge.
2. Bowls dominate and transfers connect them. Tony’s favourite line is “transfers … in and out of the bowls”; almost every original gap is a transfer or an air over a bowl.
3. The hardest place is the real bridge structure overhead. Bridge Grind 800 and Bridge Gap 1000 make the architecture the challenge, the same idea as Chicago’s rafters.
4. Obstacles on a grind line: the struts force ollie-hops while grinding the girder. A rhythm test on a single beam.
5. Threading as a gap. Bridge Gap pays for passing through the struts without touching the girder. The remake turns that window into the logo’s hiding place.
6. Tiered ledges with deliberate breaks turn the biggest air into the longest descending grind, and a fall only drops you one tier.
7. One big launcher, many outcomes: the tall quarterpipe wall feeds Over Da Pool, the vert wall, the tiered ledges and the girder line (via the funbox).
8. A tiny object makes a big gap: the twinkie bump turns flat ground into a pool transfer (700).
9. Circuits pay for repetition. The U-quarterpipe/spine circuit awards three Transfer bonuses in one loop and is designed to be ridden counter-clockwise (G1 p112).
10. The start obstacle is part of a line. The Triple Rail finishes on the obstacle you started on, so the level loops back to its own spawn.
11. Remake: vertical tiers on one quarterpipe are scored separately (Bottom Shelf Lip 500 → Top Shelf Lip 1000). Height doubles the value.
12. Remake collectibles are paired high/low near the same supports: the plushie on a little white quarterpipe at a support’s foot, the logo inside the support above.
13. Failure is cheap everywhere: falls land in bowls or on the flat, never out of bounds, so a 1:00 heat can absorb a missed girder attempt.

## Original vs remake

- Setting: the remake’s Burnside is dark and rainy (LP12 0:27:05–0:29:20); the loading card reads “It’s raining, it’s pouring, but my skat[eboard] …”, and its PROTIP warns that the more times you use a trick in a run, the less it is worth (LP12 0:27:10; text partly cut off). That makes the variety judging explicit.
- New gaps: Lippn’ The Bridge, Top Shelf Lip, Bottom Shelf Lip and Something in da Eye (lip tricks), Rollin’ The Hill (manual), Over da Pool Landing (a longer Over Da Pool).
- Collectibles added: 2 stat points per style, the VV logo inside a bridge support and the alien plushie on a white quarterpipe by a support (PS12).
- VG labels a “Wall Gap” that neither G1 nor PS12 lists.
- LP12 shows the remake’s “Overtime!” finish during Burnside heats (0:28:15–0:28:25).

## Transfer to this game

1. Through the struts: put the crystal inside the opening of a bridge support, between two struts. It is reachable by one aimed vert air from a nearby quarterpipe, or by a charged ollie (≈ 13.8 m reach at maximum charge) off a funbox. Make the window about 1.5–2 times the player’s jump width so it reads as a threading test.
2. Strut rhythm: a long girder grind with posts every few metres forces grind-ollie hops (≈ 2.4 m pop). Place the crystal on the girder after the last strut, so the reward follows the rhythm.
3. Tiered descent: three ledges stepping down from the top of a tall quarterpipe wall, with breaks shorter than a cruise ollie (≤ 9 m). Put the crystal at the top tier’s first break, so only players who land the huge air reach it; a fall drops them to a lower tier.
4. Landmark bowl as compass: give the level one unmistakable bowl (the eyeball) that is visible from everywhere, and orient all secrets relative to it in the level’s hints.
5. High/low pair: tease the crystal from a low, easy perch at the foot of the support (as the remake’s plushie does) and put the real crystal in the window above.
