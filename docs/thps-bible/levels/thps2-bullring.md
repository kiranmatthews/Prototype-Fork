- **Arena floor**: the halfpipe and its roll-ins, the loop, humps, funboxes, rails, the banana wall and the gate.
- **Barrier signs**: banners and signs along the top of the arena barrier, with flag sets; a middle layer reached from quarterpipes below.
- **Overhead wires**: two lighting wires spanning the arena with lamps at their middles; the top layer, reached from the halfpipe rail or the stands’ launch ramp.
- **Stands**: the tiered spectator ring, reached by side ramps; a second skateable level with launchers.
- **Hidden lower ring**: the bull channel behind the barrier, where the bull runs.
- **Loops**: the halfpipe; the circular sign ring; the channel lap; floor ↔ stands via the side ramps.

Landmarks: loop-de-loop at the halfpipe’s W end; lighting wires with lamps over the arena; red-and-white arena barrier with bunting; tiered stands under a roof ring; the bull.

## Spawn and first view

You start on the roll-in at the middle of the halfpipe’s S side (VG START) and drop N into the halfpipe. The loop is down-left (W), the gate to the right, and a second roll-in and the stands behind you (PS12 directions). The lighting wires hang overhead in the first view, and LP2 shows a $250 note on the wire 7 s later. LP2’s first heat goes drop-in → Nailin da Rail → wire → first $250 at about 10 s. The bull channel is below the eye line behind the barrier and cannot be seen from the halfpipe.

## Collectibles

THPS2 competitions have no tape or letters; the invite card makes the **cash icons** the completion goal. The Bullring has the biggest pot: 9 icons at $250 each ($2250). They are spread over three perimeter layers (the overhead wires, the barrier signs and a hidden channel behind the barrier), and the channel holds the bull. In this competition park, the channel is what a tape would be in a career level: a hidden space you have to find. The remake drops cash icons, puts its logo above a wire and adds a third stat point per style, one of them in the channel.

## Guidance and sightlines

- Wires and lamps hang over the halfpipe in plain view from the start, and LP2 shows a $250 note on the wire above the skater (0:57:54). The top layer is a visible tease.
- Bunting and flag sets mark the signs that can be ground (Enjoyin the View: “from one set of flags to the next”).
- The red-and-white barrier hides the channel. Nothing shows the way in except the barrier’s top edge and the bull’s presence (PS12, LP2).
- The loop is the arena’s centrepiece silhouette, and its 5000-point gap is the obvious aspiration.
- Each pickup prints “250 bucks!!!”; the last prints “Cash icons cleared” (LP2 1:01:25).
- Hidden on purpose: the channel and its icons are below the arena’s eye line.

## Design logic

1. A single dominant structure (the long halfpipe with a mid-length roll-in and a loop at one end) sits inside a ring of layered perimeter systems: lighting wires overhead, banner signs on the barrier, a hidden channel behind it and stands above.
2. All nine icons are worth the same ($250), so value does not rank them; the layer they sit on does. Each layer has its own access verb: rail → wire pop, quarterpipe → sign grind, barrier → drop into the channel.
3. Height runs both ways from the floor: the wires are the visible top layer (≈ 8–10 m), and the channel is the hidden bottom layer behind the barrier. The level’s secret is below you, not above.
4. The channel holds a moving hazard that is also a trick object (“Bull plant 2000”). LP2’s last icon is taken just as the bull arrives, so the climax of the collection is the hazard.
5. Opening line to the top: from the start, Nailin da Rail leads onto the wire and the first icon in ≈ 10 s. The level shows its top layer first.
6. Two entries to the same layer (halfpipe rail, stands launch ramp) make the wires reachable from both the floor and the stands.
7. The roll-in is scored as a precision ladder: 1, 50, 300, 1000, 2000 points for increasingly tight lines over the same obstacle.
8. Triads teach approach variety: Box / Launch / Ramp Rail to Rail and to Banana hit the same two targets from three launch types.
9. The loop is the big commitment set-piece (Way to Go Gringo!!! 5000, the most valuable gap in all three THPS2 competition parks studied).
10. The stands are a second skateable level (Up to the Stands, Finesse Test, launch ramps) that turns spectator space into a launch deck.
11. One heat, one layer. LP2 spends heat 1 on wires, heat 2 on signs and heat 3 on the channel: the three-heat format maps onto the three icon layers.
12. The remake keeps the layering: its logo sits above a wire (one step higher than the original’s top icons), a street stat point sits in the bull channel, and the plushie is on the stands.
13. Failure is cheap on the floor and costly at height: a wire fall drops ≈ 8–10 m to the arena and costs the climb back, but no time penalty beyond that.

## Original vs remake

- Cash icons: PS12 lists none; the remake’s collectibles are 3 stat points per style (one more than other parks), the VV logo above a lighting wire and the alien plushie in the stands.
- The bull channel keeps a role: a street stat point is “in the channel where the bull is running” (PS12).
- Gap list: PS12 has 30 gaps, all also labelled on VG; VG adds “Jumpin da Humps”, which PS12 does not list.
- LP12 shows the remake’s arena with branded stands and the channel lined with red-and-white walls (1:11:00–1:11:15).

## Transfer to this game

1. Wire crystal: string a cable from the stands to the arena centre over a lamp cluster. The crystal is at mid-span, reached by grinding a halfpipe rail and grind-ollieing (≈ 2.4 m pop) up to the cable, or by a launch ramp in the stands. A fall drops to the floor and costs the climb back.
2. Channel crystal with a runner: a narrow perimeter corridor behind the arena barrier, entered by grinding the barrier top and dropping in. A moving platform or patrolling enemy runs laps in it (the bull), and the crystal sits where the runner’s lap brings it back to the player about 40 s after entry.
3. Layer = verb: give each height layer one access verb (rail → cable, quarterpipe → sign, barrier → corridor) and put the crystal on the least obvious one (below the eye line).
4. Precision ladder on one obstacle: score or gate the crystal behind the tightest version of a line over the start roll-in (through the supports). Earlier, wider versions teach the line first.
5. Loop commitment: a loop at the end of a halfpipe with a speed pad before it. The crystal at the loop’s top is collected only by riding the full loop.

## Open questions

- Icon systems: VG draws four icons on the two wire lines, three in the N/W/S perimeter band and two on the E/NE signs, but LP2 collects 3 on wires (heat 1), 2 in sign combos (heat 2) and 4 in the channel (heat 3). The mapping used here (4 wire, 1 sign, 4 channel, with the 1:00:13 pickup on a wire) is the only consistent split, but it is inferred.
- What happens on contact with the bull? Not observed. “Bull plant” shows the bull can be planted on.
- The footprint is an upper bound; the true arena diameter needs a better scale object.
- “Jumpin da Humps” (VG) is not in PS12: a PS1-only gap, or omitted from the guide?
- In which direction does the bull run, and at what speed? Its lap time sets the channel’s timing window.
