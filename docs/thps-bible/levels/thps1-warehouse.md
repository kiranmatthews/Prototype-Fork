Route (designed line, G1 p045 and p050; LP1 3:40–3:44):

1. **Leave the start room through the right window** (break-glass). Tony's line: "Break the right window and go
   down the ramp" (G1 p045). Optional first: turn round, ride the quarter pipe behind the spawn and hit box
   pile 1 on the way out (G1 p046).
2. **Drop down the right entry ramp** (ramp). This is the run-up: the start room is the highest floor surface
   in the level, so this is maximum speed without pumping.
3. **Veer right towards the sky box** while still rolling (G1 p050: veer "before you go off the ramp over the
   halfpipe"). The aim is the room, not the centre of the pipe.
4. **Ride up the halfpipe's outer bank and ollie at its coping** (transfer). LP1 3:42: the skater reaches the
   hazard-striped coping with the far "FIRM" wall and the room above it in view.
5. **Fly over the halfpipe flat** (≈ 9–12 m across). This earns Over the Pipe (300); T is on the same arc over
   the centre (G1 p049), and TAS1 appears to take T and the tape in one air (inferred, see TAS notes).
6. **Crash through the glass of the room** (break-glass). The tape is collected inside; Secret Room (300)
   is awarded. LP1 3:43: "HIDDEN TAPE!" with "SWITCH OVER THE PIPE + SECRET ROOM 1200".
7. **Land on the far deck**, or, "if you're really daring", in a grind on the rail or ledge on the far side of
   the halfpipe (G1 p050), which is the pipe ledge of Z7 with box pile 2.

Alternative lines observed or described:

- **From inside the halfpipe** (PS12; WEB thumbsticks): pump the halfpipe and vert-air up into the room from
  its near wall. Slower but always available.
- **From the pipe ledge / far deck** (TAS1 0:35–0:37): after box pile 2 on the pipe ledge, TAS1 rides along the
  halfpipe's far deck and drops a short air into the side of the room; the trick text shows "SWITCH CHRIST AIR
  210" with no gap names, so it did not clear the pipe. TAS1 then quits the run (all goals done).
- **Taxi Gap** (G1 p052): from the taxi, a long jump lands in the centre of the halfpipe; with this speed the
  next wall air can reach the room (inferred).

## Guidance and sightlines

- **The feature is the sign.** The tape is in a lit, hanging box over the biggest ramp in the hall; the
  halfpipe and the room are visible from most of the floor. No arrow or icon is needed.
- **The spawn aims you.** The right window and the right entry ramp point at the halfpipe; G1 p053 says the
  jump is "hard not to clear" from the start ramp. Speed and direction are given before the player decides
  anything.
- **Glass is taught first.** The start-room window breaks (G1 p045), so the glass of the sky box reads as an
  entrance, not a wall.
- **T rehearses the arc.** T hovers over the centre of the same halfpipe; a player chasing letters flies the
  Over the Pipe arc before or during the tape attempt.
- **Bonus icons point at features**, not at the tape: three 100s mark the transition-grind coping, the channel
  quarter pipes and the back quarter pipe (G1 p020: icons are "sign posts").
- **Remake:** the level intro shows a camera shot of each goal; the "GET THE SECRET TAPE" shot is from inside
  the room (LP12 0:03:09). The remake tells you where the tape is before the run starts.
- **Hidden on purpose:** nothing important. The only obscured spots are the High Rail on the billboard and
  the deck behind the front quarter pipe (E, box 5), which you see only after going up.

## Design logic

1. **The first secret is on the main line.** The career's first hidden tape sits on the level's signature jump,
   not in a corner. It teaches what a tape is (a reward for flying somewhere unusual) at almost no search cost.
2. **Spawn → speed → target.** The start room is the highest floor in the level and its right exit points at
   the room. Height is converted into the one speed the tape needs, without any pumping skill.
3. **Same arc, three rewards.** Over the Pipe (300), Secret Room (300) and T all sit on one trajectory. The
   tape attempt is never wasted: a short jump still earns a gap or a letter.
4. **Glass at both ends of the line.** Breaking the start window and breaking into the sky box frame the line;
   the player learns that a glass pane is a door at the moment it costs nothing.
5. **Visible-but-hard, softened.** The room is in plain view but out of reach from the floor; the only skill is
   speed plus aim. The "hard" part is ≈ 5–8 m of height, supplied by the drop-in.
6. **Cheap failure.** A miss lands you in the halfpipe, which is itself a second route to the room (vert air
   from inside), so a retry costs seconds and teaches pumping.
7. **One letter per feature.** S, K, A, T, E each sit on one landmark and ask for that landmark's basic move.
   Collecting them is a tour of the hall and a list of the verbs.
8. **Boxes teach ledges.** Every box pile is on a different ledge or deck reached by a different move (quarter
   pipe out of the start room, ollie onto a ledge, transition over a hump, kicker to ledge, press Up at a
   quarter-pipe top). The goal is a curriculum of "getting up onto things".
9. **Graded options from one prop.** The taxi launches to three destinations of rising difficulty and value
   (Taxi Gap 600, Taxi 2 Ledge 500, Taxi 2 Rail 1,000), so one obstacle serves beginners and experts.
10. **A loop, not a corridor.** The hall circulates; the TAS proves every goal lies on one 15-second lap. A
    player who misses something only has to go round again.
11. **The expert line ends where the tape line lands.** Monster Grind finishes on the pipe ledge across the
    halfpipe, and G1 suggests landing the tape jump in a grind there. The beginner and expert lines share a
    destination.
12. **The guide says "grab it first".** G1 p050 treats the tape as a free opening move. The first level makes the
    secret the easiest goal, which tells new players that secrets are worth looking up and flying into.
13. **The remake makes the hint explicit.** THPS 1+2 adds a goal-preview camera inside the room. The 1999 design
    relied on the room's silhouette; the remake's change suggests that the silhouette alone was considered too
    subtle for new players (inference).

## Transfer to this game

1. **Sky-box crystal.** Hang a small box on chains over the far end of a halfpipe, with an open or breakable
   front (a crate panel the skater smashes) facing the run-up. Put a long drop-in ramp at spawn aimed at it so
   a straight run reaches the 23 m/s charge cap; at that speed a full ollie carries ≈ 13.8 m, enough to clear a
   9–12 m halfpipe. Box floor ≈ 2 m above the coping.
2. **Keep it off foot routes.** Place the box floor at least ≈ 7.5 m above any walkable surface (double jump
   ≈ 4.6 m plus a 2.4 m ledge grab) and give it no grabbable edge, so the crystal needs the board.
3. **Second chance inside the pipe.** Size the halfpipe so a pumped vert air from its near wall can also reach
   the box (the PS12 alternative). A miss then becomes a pumping lesson, not a walk back.
4. **Put a letter-equivalent on the arc.** Place a trick gate or bonus pickup over the halfpipe centre on the same
   arc (the T role), so a short attempt still pays and shows the trajectory.
5. **Teach the breakable at spawn.** If the box front is a smashable panel, make the spawn exit the same panel
   type, so the player learns "panels break" in the first second.
6. **Graded launches from one prop.** Use a parked vehicle as a kicker with three marked destinations of rising
   difficulty (into the pipe, onto the far ledge, onto the long rail), mirroring the taxi.
7. **Preview camera.** If the level intro has camera nodes, end one inside the crystal box, as the 2020 remake
   does for the tape.
