## Spawn and first view

- **2002 PS2:** you drop in at the start. A flatbed train runs along the tracks ahead, and a QP leads on toward the buoy basin (THG Manual combo the train; Slap the buoy). Goal-givers stand near the start: Andrew Reynolds offers C-O-M-B-O at the first "New Goal" (LP4 1:26:02) and the Delivery Guy is close by (1:27:02). The S-K-A-T-E goal begins in front of a ramp painted "CLEAR OF THE TRACKS" (LP4 1:29:02). The first letters are not visible from the goal start in LP4.
- **2025 remake:** you spawn on the dock road with rail tracks set into it, a stair or ramp with a yellow rail on the left, and cranes and warehouses ahead (PS34 #37). The S is up the ramp to the left and K, A and T follow in a row (PS34 S-K-A-T-E). **The tape is not visible from the spawn.** It floats behind the warehouse block, in an alley at roof height (PS34 #37–#40). The "Rooftops" door is one left turn and one right turn away (≈ 10–15 s; the PS34 timer reads 59:55 at the spawn and 59:45 at the door).
- **PS1 edition:** you start on top of the square building on one side of the water gap (CC1 S-K-A-T-E Hard), next to the START label at the right of the yard (VG). The opening invites the S-K-A-T-E Hard first move, a grind along the line to the square building across the water.

**Route (from spawn, PS34 and WEB):**
1. From the spawn, ride **left along the rail tracks** in the dock road (PS34 #37; WEB pcgamesn "head left, following the rail tracks on the ground").
2. Take the **first right into the narrow alley** between warehouses. It has cones and yellow hatching and a lit doorway further on (PS34 #38).
3. Ride through the **open doorway** with the green arrow sign. It "leads you to 'Rooftops'" (PS34 #39). In LP34 the doorway is a 2–3 s dark corridor that comes out on the roof (1:50:56–1:50:58.5), and WEB pcgamesn says you are "teleported to the rooftops". WEB hardcoregamer describes it as a ramp up to cargo "in-between the two main buildings", marked by "a green square with an arrow on it", that "will teleport you to the roof".
4. On the roof, **turn right** to the ventilation duct that runs off the roof edge across the alley (PS34 #40).
5. **Roll slowly onto the duct and off its end** (WEB finalboss "Roll slowly onto the vent and then gently ride off the edge"). The tape is just past the end and slightly below it; you fall through it.
6. You land in the alley or on a lower roof, depending on speed. LP34 landed on a roof at 1:50:58.8 to the banner "COMPLETE! Get the secret tape".

**Alternative (WEB hardcoregamer):** "You can drop to collect it, or maneuver through the ventilation". The vent ducts hold rails (pro goal "Gap all of the Vent Rails"), so the tape can also be reached along the duct line.

**LP34 variant (at speed, 1:50:26–1:50:59):** spine transfers and wallrides in the container yard → BS wallride on a teal container → wallie → "Kissed the Rail" → pop shove-it → BS 50-50 along the dark wall with the panda graffiti beside the door → into the door → comes out airborne off the vent box ("Impossible") and through the tape. A fast arrival still works when it sets the skater on the duct's line. The roll-off advice is for players who walk up to the edge and jump with full speed.

## Guidance and sightlines

- **Goal-givers and arrows (2002):** goals are handed out by people standing in the level, such as Andrew Reynolds, the Delivery Guy, a Security Guard, Bucky, a Dockworker, Rune and a Mechanic (LP4 1:26–1:44). A cyan arrow marks each giver, and race or chase goals use a red on-screen arrow (LP4 1:27:50, red arrow over the dog chase). The View Goals menu can **jump straight to a goal's start** ("Jump to Goal (if unlocked)", LP4 1:39:50). This is the 2002 answer to a large map.
- **Letters as waypoints (2002):** S-K-A-T-E strings its letters along one climbing grind line from the waterline to the roofs and back. Each letter sits at a transition (rail→wire, roof→roof, roof→kicker, QP→roof, scaffold bend) rather than in a dead end (THG).
- **Glowing pickups:** the 2002 rivets "glow a bright orange you can't really miss them" (THG), so the rivet chain draws its own line.
- **2025 tape is hidden on purpose:** you can't see it from the spawn, the alley or the door. It appears only when you turn right on the roof (PS34 #40). The door is marked by a green arrow sign on the wall (PS34 #39) and a lit opening at the end of an alley with cones and hatching (#38). Both point you at the door, but not at the tape. WEB hardcoregamer calls the sign "a green square with an arrow on it", which says the cue is designed.
- **The remake's pause screen** tells stuck players that other goals may open the way to a tape (LP34 4:14:40). That tip is generic and does not apply to Shipyard.
- **PS1:** VG shows the tape icon on top of the central building, which is ringed by blue S-K-A-T-E Hard letters (T, E, A). The hard-letter line therefore frames the tape's building (VG; inferred sightline).

## Design logic

1. **2002: no tape, and the secrets are entrances.** The PS2 original has no tape. Its "hidden" content is a **QP-into-a-floor-hatch** entrance to the shack (THG), loose cash icons, and goals hung on moving machinery (containers, crane, train). The hard-to-reach items are tied to goals, not hidden.
2. **2025 tape: a portal answers the big map.** Iron Galaxy places the tape ≈ 15–25 s from the spawn and lets a **doorway do the climb** (PS34 #37–#40; LP34). On a large, cluttered map the route is short. The hard part is noticing the door, then turning the less obvious way on arrival.
3. **Restraint instead of air.** The tape sits just past and below the end of a duct, so a slow roll-off catches it and a fast launch flies over it (WEB finalboss). That is the inverse of most tapes and a cheap, memorable twist.
4. **Secrets clustered on one corridor.** The Rooftops door serves the tape, the panda plushie and two of the three vent-rail gaps (PS34). One discovery pays three times, and the pro goal later sends you back through the tape's spot (the vent rails).
5. **The remake keeps the original's vocabulary.** The 2002 gap list already has "Ventilation Claustrophobia" and "Warehouse Ledge Madness" (THG gaps). The remake turns the vents into the tape's landmark and a pro goal, building on existing geometry instead of adding new.
6. **Moving machinery is the level's theme.** 2002 goals ride the crane (held lip), spine into swinging or spinning containers, and manual a moving train; 2025 adds a moving walkway and a moving container for the logo. Collectibles on moving pieces test timing more than air.
7. **Letters teach the vertical loop.** S-K-A-T-E (2002) goes waterline → wire → roofs → cabins → ground → QP → roof → scaffold. It is a guided tour of every layer the later goals use (THG; LP4 1:29:02–1:30:50).
8. **Combo sets graduate.** The amateur C-O-M-B-O teaches wallride-into-grind and a kicker on cabin roofs; the High C-O-M-B-O adds boat bowls, spine transfer and revert-manual; the Pro C-O-M-B-O ends on a raised half-pipe platform reached by a spine onto a building and an air up from its roof (THG). Each set adds one layer and one verb.
9. **Water is the failure line.** Water separates the buoy, the shack gap, the barges and the cables. A miss costs a bail ("You big drip!") and a respawn, not a long climb. In 2025 the alley drop under the tape is the only long fall, and the door puts you back on the roof quickly.
10. **PS1 parallels the PS2 idea in miniature.** The VV port has one big locked room (the secret area, 5 keys) and puts its tape on the highest open-air rooftop block. That block's edges are scored gaps, so the climb and the pickup overlap (CC1).
11. **Collectibles on moving pieces in both lineages.** The PS1 deck rides the crane's lifted beam (CC1) and the 2025 logo rides a shuttling container (PS34). Each version has one collectible you can only reach by timing a moving object.
12. **Cash as micro-tapes in 2025.** One bundle is hidden inside a stacked container and others are strung on wires near the spawn (PS34). The small pickups teach the layers (wire, container interior) that the bigger secrets use.

## Original vs remake

- **Tape:** 2002 PS2 has none. The **PS1 edition** hides an unlisted tape on top of a rooftop brick structure (CC1; VG). **2025** puts a listed goal "Get the Secret Tape" mid-air under a duct between the central warehouse roofs, reached through a new **Rooftops door** (PS34). Both later versions put the tape at **the top of the central roofs**. The PS1 tape is a landing target on a pedestal; the 2025 tape is a fall-through target over an alley.
- **The door is new in 2025 (inferred).** No 2002 source mentions a door or transition to the roofs. In 2002 you reach the roofs by the waterside wire (THG S-K-A-T-E) or a QP (THG welders). LP4 shows no such door, so this is low-confidence negative evidence.
- **Goals:** 2025 keeps S-K-A-T-E, welders, rivets (21 → 7), the train manual and the buoy (5 bell grinds → one Hardflip grind). It drops Dog Chase, Inventory the containers, the shack water gap and the competition, and adds the tape, Ollie North across the roofs, Judo across the containers and the vent rails (PS34; THG).
- **Letters:** the 2025 S-K-A-T-E is a short line starting next to the spawn (PS34). The 2002 S-K-A-T-E is a 1:48 tour of the whole vertical loop (LP4). The remake trades letter-tour length for a shorter, readable opening.
- **Clock:** 2002 is free roam with per-goal clocks; 2025 has 10:00 runs (LP34). The remake reintroduces a run clock but keeps it long enough to explore a large map.
- **What the change suggests:** when the remake added classic collectibles to a free-roam map, it placed them **near the spawn and on new shortcut geometry** (a door). It did not add them to the old long lines, which keeps the tape's cost in proportion to a 10-minute run.

## Transfer to this game

1. **Slow drop-off crystal.** End a grindable duct or platform edge over a 6–10 m wide alley. Place the crystal ≈ 1.5–2.5 m beyond the edge and ≈ 1 m below the edge top. A player rolling off at ≈ 4 m/s falls 1 m in ≈ 0.45 s and travels ≈ 1.8 m, so they pass through it. A cruise-speed launch (12 m/s) travels ≈ 5.4 m before dropping 1 m and overshoots. The challenge is braking, not air. Add a respawn ledge or a lift back up so a miss costs ≈ 10 s.
2. **Shortcut door to the roof layer.** A ramp or lift near the spawn that delivers the hero onto a roof that is otherwise reached only by a long wire line. Hide the crystal in the direction opposite the arrival view. With no portals in this engine, use a moving platform or a lift with a visible entrance.
