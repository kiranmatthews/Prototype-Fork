## Method

- **Loading.** Each level is constructed with `new Level(scene, entry)` inside the headless `withSkateRuntime` harness (the `tools/audit-crate-layout.mjs` pattern). The published pack is restored into the in-memory store first, as a fresh browser does, so `findLevel()` returns what a new player gets. A failed build is recorded and the run continues; nothing asserts.
- **Crates.** Measured from `level.crates` (after the ×1.2 cluster enlargement, so every crate is a 1.152 m cube) and `level.checkpoints`. Race item boxes are excluded. The derived end-of-course green ! is included and flagged `systemic`. "Counted" follows `Level.totalCrates`: every crate except !, green !, steel and steel-arrow crates. Checkpoint crates, which the gem tally also includes, are reported separately. Outline crates are reported under their underlying kind. Component indices (`cid`) index the level's built data (`builtFromData.components`). Native builders (`beachfront`, `jungle`, `dark`) have none, so those crates are named `c<n>` in `level.crates` order.
- **Route and sections.** The route line is the level's camnode lane (`lanePts`), used when spawn and gate lie within 40 m of it and it spans at least half its length. Otherwise it is spawn → checkpoints → gate, as in `Level.bonusCourseRoute`. Each crate is projected in 3D onto the line, like `Level.laneDirAt`: *progress* is arc length from the spawn's projection toward the gate's, and *lateral* is the signed horizontal offset. Checkpoint progress values split the route into sections. Density is counted crates per 100 m of spawn→gate route.
- **Clusters.** Crates (checkpoints included) are linked when their centres are within 1.2 crate widths on every axis. That covers touching, stacked and diagonal neighbours. Arrangement is classified on the crate grid: single, pair, row, stack (one column), pyramid (each layer smaller and resting on the one below; includes staircases), wall (one crate thick, ≥ 2 layers), grid (one layer, full rectangle), block (full box) or irregular. Rows, walls and pairs are oriented along, across or diagonal to the route. A *collectible* cluster contains at least one counted crate. *Floating* means no member rests on ground.
- **Patterns.** Evenly spaced singles: ≥ 4 consecutive single crates 1.6–24 m apart, lateral change ≤ 3 m and spacing CV ≤ 0.25. Floating line or group: ≥ 3 unsupported crates chained within 4 m, a line if the first principal axis holds ≥ 90% of the variance. Spaced line or scattered: ≥ 3 clusters chained within 4 m.
- **Support and elevation.** Vertical raycasts against `level.groundMeshes` (centre plus four footprint corners) decide whether a crate rests on ground, on another crate, on an inactive outline surface or on a spin-bridge deck that appears once spun, or on nothing. *Void* means no ground above `killY` below, lethal water first, or a pit first. A pit counts only when it is not underneath other ground, so level-wide safety pits are ignored. *Near drop* means a ≥ 1.5 m drop, pit or void, found in 8 directions, that persists outward and starts within 1.5 m of the crate face.
- **Reach.** A crate is *above jump reach* when its base is more than 3 m above the ground under it (single jump peak 2.97 m). Tools are tested in this order: grounded stack; arrow below within 2 m (plain 3.88 m, perfect 6.06 m, plus the double jump refreshed by every bounce and the 0.92 m head-bump hitbox); trampoline within 15 m; crate stomp below (2.97 m + double jump + head); enemy stomp; a standing surface within 3 m; an authored rail; a mover; a vert ramp within 12 m; own double jump (4.8 m + head); a surface that appears after a switch. Last comes *crate-chain*: a reachable crate or arrow within 6 m whose bounce carries to the crate base, spread outward from reachable crates. Anything left is *none-found*. Crates within reach are tagged *walk* (resting on ground or on another crate, base no more than 3 m up) or *jump* (unsupported but within 3 m of a standing surface).
- **Explosives and switches.** The blast reach is radius + 0.6 m centre-to-centre (TNT 2.75 m, Nitro 2.75 m), as in `Level.update`. Arrow and outline crates are immune. ! wiring follows `Level.activateOutlines`: shared group ids, or both ungrouped.
- **Arrows and multi-hit crates.** For each one, the report lists what is above it: crates within its bounce reach (2 m horizontally), fruit, or a surface out of double-jump reach from its floor within 5 m. *gapAnchor* marks arrows over a void or at a drop edge.
- **Skating.** Crate rails are `level.crateRails` grouped by run, as derived by "CRATE RUNS ARE RAILS". A second derivation is also counted with every outline materialised. *On rail* means a crate centre within 1 m of an authored rail line (within 2.5 m: near). *Downhill* means route grade ≥ 10% over ±10 m. *After pad* means within 40 m after a speed pad. Plain crates on such stretches are where fast skating (≥ 12.5 m/s) plows through boxes.
- **Enemies and hazards.** Horizontal distance to an enemy patrol segment ≤ 4 m (vertically within 3 m), and box-to-box distance ≤ 4 m to a crusher, pendulum (swing envelope), mover (travel envelope), crumble, phase pad, boulder lane, fire tiki or angry ball body.

## Limitations

- The route line is the camera lane, not the walked path. Lateral offsets and the on-path / detour bands are therefore approximate, especially where the lane runs to one side of a wide area. The per-level JSON gives `summary.routeOffset`, the median 3D distance of crates from the lane.
- Reach is a geometric budget (peak heights from `src/tuning.ts`, 2 m / 6 m / 15 m search radii), not a simulation. It ignores run-up speed, air control, charged skate ollies and moving geometry. *none-found* means "nothing detected", not "proven unreachable".
- The state is the level at build time: switches unpressed, spin bridges closed, movers at phase 0, phase pads in their initial state. Outline crates and surfaces are reported separately, and crate rails are also counted with outlines materialised.
- Hidden-from-camera crates were not estimated. Fast sections use route grade and speed pads only; actual skating speed is not simulated.
- Bonus rooms, races, competitions and free-skate parks are outside the scope (see Skipped). Add any level id with `--levels id1,id2`.
