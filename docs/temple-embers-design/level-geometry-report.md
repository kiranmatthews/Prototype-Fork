## Method

- **Loading.** Each level is constructed with `new Level(scene, entry)` inside the headless `withSkateRuntime` harness (as `tools/crate-placement-report.mjs`). The state measured is the level at build time: switches unpressed, outline crates and surfaces inactive, spin bridges closed, movers at their base positions, phase pads solid, crumbles intact.
- **Route line and sections.** Identical to the crate audit: the camnode lane (`lanePts`) when spawn and gate lie within 40 m of it and it spans at least half its length, otherwise spawn → checkpoints → gate. *Progress* is arc length from the spawn's projection toward the gate's; checkpoint progress values split the route into sections. A lane segment longer than 120 m that crosses a stretch with no ground is a portal or teleport link; it is excluded from the *measured length*, which is the denominator of every per-100 m figure.
- **The walk.** Camnode heights are camera data, not floor heights, so the floor is tracked from the ground itself. From the spawn, a walker steps along the route line every 0.25 m and probes standable faces of `Level.groundMeshes` (the set the player's own ground probe uses; faces steeper than about 63° are walls) in 13 vertical columns 0.4 m apart across a ±2.4 m corridor centred on its own line, which may drift up to 12 m from the route line and eases back toward it whenever the floor allows. Moving platforms are excluded from the walk (they are reported as rides); crumbling pads and phase pads count as platforms in their initial state. A surface is not standable if it lies in a pit volume, under lethal water, below `killY`, or is a lethal or out-of-bounds mesh. Continuous ground may fall 1.5 m or rise 1 m between samples; a rise of 1–5.2 m is a *climb* (step-up). Anything else ends the floor.
- **Gaps.** Where the floor ends, the edge is bisected to ±1 cm and the walker looks ahead (up to 60 m) for a landing between 1.5 m below and 5.2 m above the take-off, first inside its corridor, then up to 10 m either side. Each column on the way records what a fall there ends in: ground, pit, lethal water, lethal mesh, out-of-bounds mesh, or void (nothing above `killY`). Classification: a landing within 15 m makes a **gap** (*level*, or *offset* when beside the corridor); safe ground directly under the edge with no near landing is a **drop** (step down, not a gap); if the floor simply continues beside the corridor within 1 m it is a lateral **shift**, not a gap; otherwise the first safe floor below (*lower*), a farther level landing (*level-long*), or any surface (*void*) ends the gap. A gap that rises into something taller than 5.2 m with no open space beneath is a **wall**. With no surface for 60 m the stretch is a **void** (rides, rail sections, vehicles; aids are listed). Breaks narrower than 0.6 m are seams. A gap is *lethal* when at least half of its sampled columns end in a lethal outcome; *safe* gaps record the depth of the floor below. *Width* is measured along the route line; *jump* is the edge-to-edge horizontal distance on the walked line (they differ for offset landings and curved lanes) and is what the reach check uses.
- **Crossing reach.** The reach thresholds are the measured flat ranges (take-off to touchdown on flat ground) from real-input Player runs in [`data/skate-crate-probe.json`](data/skate-crate-probe.json), scenario `range`: foot jump at 9 m/s 5.85 m (apex 2.85 m); full jump + double jump at the apex 9.15 m (apex 4.59 m); full charged ollie from 12 m/s cruise 9.36 m (charging accelerates the board to 15.6 m/s at take-off); full ollie at 23 m/s 13.83 m (apex 1.78 m); for reference run-tap-jump 4.95 m, board-tap-ollie-12 4.66 m, board-full-ollie-18 12.55 m. Only the height difference is modelled: a two-gravity arc through the measured apex with `src/tuning.ts` gravities (foot rise 33, fall 119; board rise 33, fall 70 m/s²) gives the airtime ratio t(dh) / t(0), which scales the measured flat range; a landing above the apex is out of reach. For example a landing 1 m lower stretches the foot jump to 6.18 m and the 23 m/s ollie to 15.24 m; 1 m higher shortens them to 5.46 m and 11.93 m. Ledge grabs, kicker and slope launches, trampolines, crate and enemy bounces and speed lost before take-off are not included, so "beyond the measured reach" often means "needs a launch or a ride", which the aid list records.
- **Aids** listed per gap or void: a mover travel envelope, deployed spin-bridge deck, grind rail / sagging rope / moving rail running across it (within the corridor, at −2…+4 m of take-off height), a rope swing within reach, a trampoline within 8 m before take-off, a speed pad within 40 m before it, a *kicker* (take-off from a ramp component, a ramp- or kicker-named surface up to 60 m long or a vert ramp up to 40 m long, or a floor rising ≥ 0.5 m over the last 3 m), solid / steel / arrow / explosive / outline crates or an enemy patrol inside the gap, or an inactive outline surface. *Side* gives the lateral distance to ground at take-off height beside the gap at its 25/50/75 % points (≤ 6 m counts as avoidable).
- **Platforms.** Every walker sample records the surface stood on, typed from its component (`platform`, `mesh`, `terrain`, `woodpath`, `ramp`, `vertramp`, `crumble`, `phasepad`, `trampoline`, `decor` …) or, for the native builders, from its runtime tags and name. Footprints come from the component size where authored, otherwise the world bounding box. Every 2 m the supported width across travel is probed outward to 6 m each side; *narrow* is < 2 m, *very narrow* < 1.2 m. *Landing depth* runs from a landing edge to the next break of any kind (gap, drop, wall, void, or step-up ≥ 1 m).
- **Hazards and timed platforms.** From the runtime arrays: crushers, pendulums (swing envelope), rolling boulders (patrol lane; chase boulders separately), fire tikis (breath sector, firing 23% of each cycle), grindosauri, angry balls, minefields, movers, phase pads, crumbles, rope swings, spin bridges, snapping ropes, moving rails, thorn clusters and tumble zones. *Placement* compares the swept area with the walked line: within 1.5 m (or straddling it within 8 m) is *across*, within 8 m *flank*, beyond — or more than 5 m behind the spawn or past the gate — *off*. *Orientation* compares swing, roll, travel or patrol direction with the route direction (along > 30° … across < 60° off perpendicular). Relations: *at a gap* means within 4 m before a take-off edge, after a landing edge, or over the gap; *near crates* means a solid crate within 4 m (box to box); checkpoint distances are along the route.
- **Enemies.** Patrol segments from `Level.enemies` (sentries and spinners are stationary). *On route* means the patrol comes within 3 m of the walked line, *flank* within 8 m (patrols more than 5 m behind the spawn or past the gate are off-route). *Edge* means a pit, lethal water, void or a fall of more than 3 m within 2 m of the patrol line. A kind's *first appearance* in a course is its on-route enemy with the lowest progress; it is *alone* with no other enemy within 12 m along the route, no danger hazard within 8 m and no gap edge within 4 m, and on *safe ground* when supported, not at a lethal edge and not over a gap. Variants are the runtime appearance (tribal, nightworks) and ghost skins.
- **Rhythm.** A *beat* is a gap (G, at its take-off), an enemy (E), a hazard or timed platform (H), a crate cluster with any non-steel crate (C, clusters linked within 1.2 crate widths as in the crate audit), a rail, rope or moving rail (R, at its first point near the route) or a ramp, kicker, short vert ramp, trampoline or speed pad (K), within 10 m of the route. Beats of the same kind closer than 4 m merge. *Quiet* is the longest distance between consecutive beats (route start and gate included).
- **Route quality.** *low* when ≥ 15% of the walk is on solid decor scenery, there are ≥ 5 walls, or ≥ 10% of the measured route is void with no detected aid; *medium* with any unexplained void, ≥ 15 lateral shifts or ≥ 5% decor; otherwise *high*. Read gap and coverage numbers of *low* courses with care.

## Limitations

- The route line is the camera lane (or a checkpoint chain), not a recorded player path. The walker follows the ground within ±12 m of it, so gaps are measured where that line crosses them: widths are along the line (edge-to-edge *jump* distances are also given), a lane drawn beside the path produces lateral shifts, and a route that splits is followed along one branch only (for example a lower ledge path rather than a rope or rail above it; drops record a grind line continuing overhead). Check the route-quality column before quoting a course.
- Reach thresholds are measured flat ranges; only height differences are modelled (two-gravity arc ratio). Run-up length, ledge grabs, kicker and slope launches, trampolines, crate or enemy bounces and board double jumps are not included; "beyond the measured reach" therefore often means "needs a launch or a ride", which the aid list records.
- Everything is measured in the build-time state. Switch-activated outline crates and surfaces, deployed spin bridges and moving platforms are not walked; gaps they bridge appear as gaps or voids with the aid listed. Crumbling and phase pads count as solid.
- Standability uses face normals (≈ 63° limit) and ignores ceilings, so a corridor column inside a closed building or under an overhang can see the floor beneath it; vert transitions, loops and tubes are walked only where they are flat enough.
- Hazard sweeps are approximate envelopes (pendulum arc endpoints, fire-tiki breath sector at 65% of its turn, mover bounding boxes); enemy "edge" checks sample 16 points around each metre of patrol; danger/timing categories are by kind, not by measured lethality.
- Bonus rooms, races, competitions and free-skate parks are outside the scope (see Skipped). Add any level id with `--levels id1,id2`.
