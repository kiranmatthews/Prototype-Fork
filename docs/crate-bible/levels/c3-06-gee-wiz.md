## Puzzle rooms and dependency chains

**P1 — ? on TNT, mask first (S3-d → S3-e).** Setup: an Aku on the left, then a ? sitting on a TNT.
Correct: take the Aku; stomp the ?, then stomp the TNT and get clear (or, masked, just break through).
Tempting wrong: spinning the stack from the side — the spin hits the TNT at ground level and it blows
at once. Loss: a life (or the mask); for the all-box gem the TNT itself must be broken (mask hint 6).
Why it works: the safety net is placed one beat before the test.

**P2 — Iron column on a timer (S2-a → S2-b).** Setup: 1UP on three iron crates with a Nitro beside
them; a slot crate in the row ahead. Correct: super body slam the column without touching the Nitro,
then move on promptly and break the slot before it turns to steel. Wrong: lingering at the column.
Loss: the slot's reward (a steel slot crate cannot be broken); a careless slam next to the Nitro costs
a life. Why it works: two clusters coupled by a timer, so mastery of the new move is measured in time.

**P3 — Nitro gauntlet and the green ! (S4-b, S4-c, S4-e, S4-f).** Setup: 10 Nitros in S4 (beside a
frog, beside the bonus lift, with the green !, and a 2×3 block at the gate). Correct: avoid all,
spin the green ! after the power stone. Wrong: skipping the ! (it sits behind two berserkers and in
front of a third). Loss: all 15 Nitros of the level. Why it works: the biggest single count trap of
the level is placed at the end, where the player is most tempted to just run.

**P4 — Bonus pit before the switch (B-d → B-e).** Setup: a ? low in the pit under the outline
staircase; the ! that materialises the outlines is reachable first. Correct: drop for the ?, return,
then hit the !. Wrong: hitting the ! first — the materialised blocks close over the pit. Loss: 1 box
(all-box). Why it works: the order is the puzzle; the room teaches it without lives at stake.

**P5 — Goat and the three stacks (S1-j).** Normal play: spin the three bases (the stacks are not
explosive). TT: the goat is the tool — slide it into the stacks or stand on it to spin the tops.

## Design logic of this level

1. **Mechanic gate right after the unlock.** Boss 1 gives the super body slam and stage 6 opens with
   a 2×2×2 iron cube beside the path, no enemy or explosive in reach (TEACH); S2 re-uses it as a
   column under a 1UP with a Nitro beside it and a timer ahead (TEST).
2. **"Explosives hidden among normal crates" is the stage's crate theme** (F3 intro): 15 Nitros and
   1 TNT, almost all touching or next to ordinary crates (W+N, ?+W+W+N, N beside the iron column,
   ? on TNT) rather than standing alone in walls.
3. **Enemy–explosive adjacency.** Nitros sit next to frogs (S1-e, S4-b) and next to a berserker
   (S3-b): frogs move toward Crash and berserkers swing wide, so the fight itself pushes the player
   toward the Nitro.
4. **Mask before hazard.** S1-d precedes the first Nitro mixes; S3-d precedes the ? on TNT.
5. **Slot crates as timers.** Three slots (S1-f, S2-b, S3-f); mask hint 4 explicitly links the iron
   column to the slot ahead, so the slot's countdown spans two clusters.
6. **Density curve.** S1 31 crates (incl. 8 iron + 9 stacked), S2 17, S3 8 (enemies dominate), S4
   opens with the 10-crate reward block after C3 and then becomes a Nitro gauntlet (10 Nitros).
7. **The count trap is at the exit.** 8 of the 15 Nitros sit in the last two clusters; without the
   green ! the player loses 15 boxes at the goal. TT deliberately skips it (TA7).
8. **Stacks are uniform and readable.** Most structures are 2-stacks or 3-stacks in rows; the only
   large block is the reward at S4-a. Complexity comes from neighbours (Nitro, enemies, slot timers),
   not from the stack shapes.
9. **Enemies as tools in Time Trial.** The goat breaks 1-s boxes when slid into (TT 08–09); one frog
   is left alive at the start as a timing cue (TT 01).
10. **Bonus grammar.** Three striped sandwiches, then arrow-to-floating-crate twice (single, then a
    2×3 block), then an outline exit with a pit crate that must be taken before the switch.

## Transfer to the skate game

1. **Unlock sandbox.** When a new crate-breaking move appears (our body slam, shock radius 3.4 m),
   put a 2×2×2 cluster (2.3 m cube) of slam-only/multi-hit crates beside the path with nothing
   dangerous within ~6 m: one slam from above covers the cluster, teaching the area effect.
2. **Life on a reinforced column with a Nitro beside it.** Life crate on a 3-stack (top 3.46 m):
   double jump (4.6 m) reaches it on foot; put one Nitro 1 crate to the side so a sloppy slam or a
   board plow (Nitro kills on touch) is punished. Board riders must dismount: the column top is a
   single crate, not a grind row.
3. **? on TNT (2-stack).** Stomp the ? (14 m/s bounce, ≈ 3 m above the lid), land on the TNT, stomp it,

### Camera and route shape

The main course is one continuous into-screen path, drawn bottom-left → top-right on every strip, that winds in S-bends between low stone walls (石塀) on both edges. It is never cut by a bottomless chasm across its full width: the gaps are shallow purple water bands (frogs live in them), holes beside a wall, steps, two castle arches and one hole-plus-bridge near the goal. The walls are walkable (F3 p012L; TT frames 02, 04, 13, 24, 27), so the path is really three lanes: left wall, floor, right wall. Only the bonus is side-on.

- **S1** — into-screen: walled path ≈4–6 u wide from the castle start gate; water band, a fallen log, a hole at a right-hand bend, a second water band; ends at C1 after the goat and the three 3-stacks
- **S2** — into-screen: steps down through a castle arch, S-bend, long water band, then a narrowing stepped passage up to a raised end where a wizard stands; C2 after a goat
- **S3** — into-screen: hole by C2, two water bands, a lane squeeze (water left, Nitro right, berserker centre), a castle arch tunnel, wizard beyond it; C3
- **S4** — into-screen: slope with the reward block, a pond, the bonus pad, two water bands, a two-berserker blockade, a hole and a bridge, then a narrow gate passage to the goal
- **B** — side-scroll: left → right over three plank walkways and an outline staircase above a pit (P3 shots 10–12)

### Geometry and enemy design logic

1. A walking stage: the main course has no full-width chasm until the hole near the goal (G-S4-d). Its gaps are shallow water bands (G-S1-a, G-S1-c, G-S2-b, G-S3-b, G-S4-a/b/c) and holes that take only part of the width (G-S1-b, G-S3-a, G-S3-c), so difficulty comes from enemies and hidden explosives, not from jumping.
2. Three lanes by construction: the low stone walls on both edges are walkable (F3 p012L), and the guide uses them as the answer to almost every threat — wizard beams (TA4), the frog beside a Nitro (mask hint 7), the berserker blockade (mask hint 8 uses the right side) — while big rocks and holes end the wall lanes at chosen points (O-S1-a, G-S1-b, G-S2-a).
3. The new enemy (wizard) debuts right after a hole (G-S1-b → E-S1-c), and each later wizard is paired with a different space: above a narrow passage behind a berserker (E-S2-c/d), and beyond a tunnel with a slot crate timing out (E-S3-d).
4. Known enemies from stage 1 (frogs, goats, berserkers) are recombined with explosives: frog + Nitro twice (E-S1-b, E-S4-a), berserker + Nitro lane (E-S3-a + O-S3-a), berserker pair before the green ! and a berserker in front of the Nitro gate (E-S4-c, E-S4-f).
5. Water is the frog habitat: four of five frog records stand in or at a water band, so a purple band on screen reads as "a hopper will cut across here" before the frog is visible.
6. Chokes hold exactly one enemy each — the S2 stepped passage (berserker), the S3 castle arch (goat), the goal gate (berserker) — so a choke always means a forced engagement or a timed pass, never a free walk.
7. The S3 lane squeeze (P-S3-b) is a three-option test: water (safe but where the frogs come from), sword (timed), Nitro (lethal); the guide's solution is a timed jump past the enemy rather than a lane.
8. Enemy density stays level (5, 5, 4, 7 enemies per section) while crate density falls (31, 17, 8, 11 boxes): the late sections are combat-led, and the reward block sits on the calm slope right after C3 (P-S4-a) as the last rest.
9. The final stretch stacks one blocker per beat: blockade → hole → bridge → green ! with Nitros → berserker in the gate → Nitro columns around the portal. Escalation at the exit is done with blockers, not with longer gaps.
10. The only long jump (G-S4-d) comes last and is set up by a run-up from a rock; everything else is a hop (log, wall, step).
11. The time-trial line is the walls plus diagonal cuts (TT 02, 15–17, 26–27): the course is built so the shortest-looking line is not the fastest (F3 p103R intro) — useful geometry for a skate line, which also wants raised edges.
12. The bonus is the stage's jump room: three walkways with 1–2 u gaps, steel arrows as the vertical tool, and a pit under an outline staircase whose crate must be taken before the switch fills the space (G-B-c).

### Geometry transfer to the skate game

1. Walkable curbs as skate lanes: line a 5–7 m path with 1-crate-high (≈1.15 m), ≈1 m-wide walls; ordinary platform edges already grind, so a rider at 12 m/s cruise gets two grind lanes that bypass floor enemies. End each wall run with a `rock` or a `pit` notch every 15–25 m so the player has to drop to the floor where you want them (the Gee Wiz rock/hole rhythm).
2. Projectile enemy after a hole: a `sentry` 6–10 m past a 2–2.5 m `pit` notch (well inside the 5.6 m running jump) so the player lands into its fire; give the projectile a slow speed and let a spin destroy it (wizard beam), and keep the wall lane beside it as the safe answer.
3. Three-lane squeeze: on a 5 m path put soft ground (`groundSpeedLimit`, e.g. 2.4 m/s) on the left 1.7 m, a `spinner` in the centre, a Nitro on the right; the intended counter is a timed full jump over the centre enemy (2.85 m apex) or a charged ollie (1.7 m) — check the enemy hitbox height against the ollie before relying on it.
4. Blockade choke: two `charger`s or `spinner`s side by side across a 5 m path, leaving one 1.2–1.5 m gap on one side; on the board the gap is a line choice at cruise speed, on foot a timed run past.
5. End-of-level long jump: a 3–4 m `pit` across the full width with a rock as the run-up marker and a 2.3 m-wide bridge as the landing, placed after the last checkpoint; on foot it is a real jump (5.6 m reach), on the board an easy ollie (≈6.6 m at cruise), so it rewards skaters without blocking walkers.
6. Bonus jump room: three 6–8 m walkways with 1.2–2.3 m gaps, a steel arrow under a floating block on each, and a pit under an outline staircase with one crate low in the pit that must be taken before the `bang` materialises the stairs.
