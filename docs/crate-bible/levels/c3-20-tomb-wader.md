# Crash 3: Warped — Stage 20 Tomb Wader  (きょうふ! ナイルがわはんらん)

Sources: F3 p074–p076 (book pp.140–145: stage map, Aku callouts 1–8 and A–E, time-attack tips 1–6, checkpoint counts,
skull course); F3 p080L (book p.152: Egypt bonus strip, stage 20 "boxes ×25, 1UP ×1"); F3 p110R (book p.213:
time-trial route "スゴイの20", frames 01–32, staff best 1:05:56); F3 p006 (stage table), F3 p005R (how-to-read
legend), F3 p011 (box legend); P3 p037L–p039R (book pp.70–75, shots 85–117 + Time Trial note). Web corroboration
(summary only): gamepressure N. Sane Trilogy "Tomb Wader" page ("smash all 88 crates").

Evidence level:
- Read directly: all crate icons on F3 p074–p076 and the bonus strip on p080L (zoomed; the striped crates are
  identified by their vertical-slat texture against the X-braced wood texture); the three checkpoint counts
  **C1 8 (9), C2 44 (48), C3 52 (56)**; P3 captions and the bonus HUD (13/25, 14/25, 20/25).
- Counting rule (F3 p005R legend, confirmed by the arithmetic): a checkpoint count includes the C box; the bracketed
  number adds Nitro boxes. The bonus room's HUD total (25, which itself contains 4 Nitros and 1 TNT that appear from
  outlines) is added whole to both numbers; the bracket adds only the main-course Nitros that the green ! clears.
- **Total 88 reconciles exactly:** main course 41 boxes + 12 main-course Nitros + bonus 25 + 10 Nitros on the skull
  (death) course = 88 = gamepressure's figure. So the in-game counter includes every Nitro, and the death route's
  Nitros are counted although the route holds nothing else (the green ! detonates them remotely).
- Badge convention (F3 p005R): red 1 / blue 2 / green 3 = 1/2/3-s time crate in time trial; orange A = Aku box in
  time trial. Drawn icon = normal-mode type.
- Uncertain: exact float heights; whether the two TNTs of S1-d stand in the channel or on its edge; the left/right
  position of a few pairs in S4; F3 labels the skull-course gem "紫ダイヤ" on p076R but its own table (p006) and P3
  say blue — treated as the blue gem.

## Puzzle rooms and dependency chains
- **P1 TNT stepping stones (S1-d).** Setup: two TNTs in a channel. Correct: at low water, stomp one, use the bounce, retreat
  to dry ground before 3 s. Wrong: standing on a lit TNT while waiting for the water. Loss: a life (and the blue-gem run,
  which forbids any death before the skull pad). Why: two clocks (fuse, flood) teach that both can be read.
- **P2 Mask on the refuge (S2-a) → life on the refuge (S2-f).** The two safe islands carry the two protective items.
  Taking them is the same action as surviving, so the "reward" and the "route" coincide.
- **P3 The bonus explosive lid (B-e → B-g → B-h).** Setup: four reward crates under four outlines, a TNT outline at the end.
  Correct: hit the high ! (B-e), jump over the newly solid Nitros with double jump + tornado spin, stomp the TNT, wait.
  Wrong: trying to break the reward crates first (the Nitros sit on top of them). Loss: a life and the bonus count.
  Why it works: the switch you need to progress also turns the reward into a trap; the TNT is the key that converts it
  back into a chain reward.
- **P4 Backtrack after the refuge (S4-a → S4-c).** Reach the steel refuge first, then return for the W[1].
- **P5 Count trap at the exit (S4-i, S4-l).** A TNT pair beside the last bend and the green ! by the goal stairs; P3
  warns about the TNT explicitly.
- **P6 Blue gem vs. clear gem.** Blue: no deaths from the start to the skull pad, then survive a route with no crates.
  Clear: all 88, which does **not** require the skull course (its Nitros are detonated by the main route's green !).
  The two gems are earned on separate runs (gamepressure advises two playthroughs).

## Design logic of this level
1. **The flood is the level's metronome.** Crates are sorted by time window: dry crates (stairs, landings, steel tops) are
   free; wet crates are taken only in the low window. Every wet cluster is ≤ 2 crates, so one window buys one cluster.
2. **Refuges carry rewards.** Aku on a steel block, 1UP above a steel stack, a W[1] beside a later refuge: the designers
   put protection and lives exactly where the player must stop anyway.
3. **TNT appears as footing, not as a wall.** The first TNTs are stepping stones in water (S1-d); the last is a count
   trap (S4-i). No TNT blocks the path.
4. **Nitros are walls and enemy partners.** Column (S2-g), diagonal line with a mummy (S4-e), pair with a shield
   researcher (S4-b) — they shape where you can stand during a water window.
5. **The death route has no crates to collect.** It is a pure skill test with 10 Nitros that still count; the all-box
   goal never forces the hard route. Separating "skill route" from "completion route" keeps the blue gem honest.
6. **Checkpoint spacing:** C1 after only 8 boxes (a short teaching section), C2 after the 25-box bonus (44), C3 close
   behind (52), then the longest and densest section (14 boxes, 8 Nitros) with no checkpoint — the climax.
7. **The bonus holds 28 % of the counted boxes** (25 of 88) and teaches its own outline grammar: a footing bridge from
   switch 1 and an explosive lid from switch 2.
8. **Time-trial masks freeze the hazard.** Three masks (one real, two A-badged) chain to invincibility exactly when the
   water is lowest, and invincibility stops the rise (TT fr. 24). The TT route turns crates into springboards (TA2, TA3,
   fr. 12–14).
9. **The bazooka changes the level on a return visit:** the crystal must be taken without it (F3 header), and the
   Nitro-heavy skull course is easy with it (P3 note). The level is built for two passes.

## Transfer to the skate game
1. **Window-sized clusters.** If a level has a periodic hazard (sweeping laser, crusher), size the crate clusters inside
   it to one window: at 12 m/s cruise a 3 s window covers ≈ 36 m; place ≤ 2 crates per window and a steel "refuge" pad
   (unbreakable footing) every 30–40 m.
2. **TNT stepping stones.** Two TNTs spaced 1 crate apart across a 3–4 m pit. On foot: stomp, bounce, keep moving (3 s).
   On the board: contact at 3–12 m/s lights the fuse and is survivable, ≥ 13 m/s kills — so the crossing forces a slow,
   deliberate line.
3. **Rewards on refuges.** Put the Aku crate on top of a steel block standing in a pit (top ≈ 1.15 m: tap-jump height)
   and a life crate floating 1 crate above a 2-high steel stack (needs the double jump).
4. **Explosive lid from a switch.** A row of 4 crates (W, W, life, W) is a grindable rail until a `bang` materialises
   4 Nitros on top; then it is no longer a rail (something on top) and touching it kills. A TNT at the row's end
   (blast 2.75 m) chain-detonates the Nitros and pays out the row. Order lesson: grind the row before pressing the
   switch, or use the TNT after.
5. **Optional hard line with only Nitros.** Build the death-route analogue as a skate line containing Nitros only;
   the level's `nitrobang` detonates them remotely so the all-box goal never forces it.
6. **Exit count trap.** A ?+TNT pair one lane off the final approach and the green ! beside the gate stairs.

### Camera and route shape
- **Into-screen throughout.** A zig-zag corridor of walled water channels about 3–4 u wide (P3 s86–s99) alternating with short tile stairs and raised dry paths, turning at right angles (F3 p074–p075). There are no side falls: the walls hold you in, so the hazard is vertical — the water.
- **One global clock.** The water rises and falls at fixed intervals; above Crash's head it kills, low it can be waded (F3 p074L header). The cycle is fixed from the start (TT intro, TA1), and the scarab at the start is the readable gauge: it crawls at low water and flies up when the water rises (F3 p067).
- **Branch:** the skull (death) course leaves right after C2 only if you reached it without dying (F3 p074L; P3 s93) and ends at its own gem pad. Bonus: side view, entered from the shield-researcher landing before C1.

### Geometry and enemy design logic
1. **The water is the level's metronome and its gap.** Each channel is a lethal gap one low-water window long; the cycle is fixed from the start (TT intro, TA1), so the whole route is one rhythm.
2. **A living gauge.** The scarab crawls at low water and flies when it rises (F3 p067) and stands at the start and beside the key channels: the telegraph is an enemy you can read before you are in danger.
3. **Channel → stairs → dry landing.** Walled channels about 3–4 u wide alternate with three-step stairs (TT 06) and dry landings; crates on dry ground cost no window, wet crates cost one. With no side falls, everything is vertical timing.
4. **Three kinds of safe island:** floating red discs that ride the water (callout 2), steel blocks and stacks (callout 3), and blocks the player builds with a ! (callout 5; skull callout A). Rewards sit on them: Aku on the block, a 1UP over the stack, a crate just past the switch-built refuge.
5. **Switch-built refuges scale the difficulty.** In S3 the ! is optional (TA5 skips it for time); on the skull course the switches must be pressed in order to build the safe zones ahead (callout A).
6. **Coupled clocks.** The sliding wall footholds run their own cycle and are usable only in some water phases (P3 s95: move when the water starts receding and sits at the top edge of the block); the guide's lane (left wall, callout C; right wall in TT 21–23) turns the double timing into a line choice.
7. **The shield researcher is a gatekeeper with a knock-back.** Slide only; an upper-body hit throws Crash back toward the camera and he side-steps to block (F3 p067). He is taught alone at the top of dry stairs, then placed beyond a hole (callout 4) and beside Nitros (callout 7), where the knock-back or his charge is the real danger.
8. **The spiked mummy is a jump-only hurdle** (spiked lower body): double-jump onto its head or fake it out (P3 s88). It is met alone on dry ground first, later patrols a Nitro line, and on the skull course should simply be ignored (callout B).
9. **Few true gaps.** One hole guarded by a researcher (S2), one hole at the S4 bend (TT 30) and the holes under the skull footholds (callout C); everything else is water, which kills only at its peak.
10. **Explosives shape standing room in the water:** TNT stepping stones (S1), a Nitro column (S2), a Nitro pair beside the researcher and a diagonal Nitro fence with a mummy (S4), and skull groups with gaps at both walls (callout D).
11. **Escalation:** S1 teaches (one disc, two TNT steps, each enemy alone), S2 adds refuges and the guarded hole, S3 adds built blocks and sliding footholds, S4 is the longest flood with discs and no checkpoint after C3, and the skull course combines everything with no deaths allowed.
12. **Patience is the skill.** Callout 8 ("don't force it") and the TT intro ("accuracy over speed") say the same thing: the level punishes impatience more than imprecision, because waiting on dry ground is always possible.
13. **The hazard has a reward-based off switch.** Three masks give invincibility timed for the lowest water, and while invincible the water does not rise (TT 24).
14. **Bonus without water:** an outline bridge from a high ! (crouch high jump), floating crates used as stepping stones that vanish when broken, and an explosive lid over the rewards.

### Geometry transfer to the skate game
1. **A global flood with phasepads.** Model a flood channel as a 3.5–4.6 m (3–4 u) wide walled `pit` floored with `phasepad`s on one global cycle (solid = low water), 7–9 m long, with three-step dry stairs (≈1.7 m rise) between channels; put a visible gauge (a slow `hopper` or a bobbing marker) at each channel head.
2. **Islands every window.** Inside each channel place one unbreakable island (a 1.15–2.3 m steel block or a floating pad) so a window can be split in two halves; put the Aku or life crate on top of it.
3. **Gatekeeper beyond a hole.** A `charger` (frontal attacks fail, telegraphed dash) on the far side of a 1.2–2.3 m hole: approaching high gets you knocked back into the hole; the counter is a short hop then a slide or ram.
4. **Coupled-timer footholds.** A 5–7 m deep gap crossed on three `mover` blocks sliding out of one wall on their own cycle, usable only when the global cycle is in its safe phase; keep one wall-hugging line where the timing is forgiving (the left-wall rule of callout C).
5. **Explosive fence with a guard.** A diagonal line of four Nitros across a channel with a patrolling `spiker` (no stomp — it must be jumped), and on the optional hard line a five-Nitro quincunx with 1.2 m gaps along both walls.
6. **TNT stepping stones in a timed channel.** Two TNTs 2.3 m apart in a 5.8–6.9 m channel: a double jump (+1.74 m) from a TNT top reaches the far stairs, so the chain works even in the unsafe phase, while the safe line waits for the window.
