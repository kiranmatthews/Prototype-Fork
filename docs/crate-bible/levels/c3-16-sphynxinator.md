  a base number with a second number in brackets: upper C **6 (8)**, lower C **17 (19)** ("if the right-route reverse
  run is also done"), C3 **25 (30)**, C4 **47 (54)**; the bonus strip (28 drawn = header 28); P3 captions.

  the C box included; the number in brackets also counts Nitro. Applied to the map, all eight numbers reproduce
  exactly — base 6, 17, 25, 47 and brackets +2 (left-route Nitro), +2, +5 (3 more Nitro), +7 (2 more) — **but only
  if the two plaza crates of POINT 2 are left out**. Carried on to the goal, the bracketed count is 54 + 22 (S4
  non-Nitro) + 28 (bonus) + 1 (last Nitro) = **105**, exactly gamepressure's total.
- **Count conflicts:** F3's icons sum to **107**; F3's own checkpoint numbers and gamepressure imply **105** — the
  difference is exactly the plaza pair, which F3 draws (two clear wood icons) but never counts. Separately, F3 draws
  **3** crates behind the start while P3 ("four boxes stashed behind him") and gamepressure ("four crates, one with
  an extra life") say **4**.
- The blue-gem course (F3 p073) has no boxes, only steel C-blocks (not counted) — P3 shot 12 agrees.

## Puzzles and dependency chains
1. **Fork order (S1-a → L → R reversed).** Setup: a fork whose left branch rejoins by a one-way drop; the right
   branch starts with a hard hole beyond the plaza pair. Correct: break the plaza pair, take the left route, drop to
   the merge, run the right route backwards to the hole, come forward again, then take the lower C (POINT 4).
   Wrong: going right first (the left route's 2-stack and Nitro are then unreachable without a restart), or
   leaving the plaza pair (two extra crossings of the hard hole). Loss: all-box gem, or extra risk.
2. **Behind-the-start crates (S0-a).** Turn round at the start; missing them is the classic all-box failure.
3. **Pillar-crushed Nitro (M-e).** Wait at the pit edge for the pillars to set off both Nitro.
4. **Three TNT piles (S3-a/b/c).** Same 2×3 silhouette, TNT moved from none → middle row → bottom row; slide low,
   jump high, never spin into a TNT row. Loss: a death, or the 1UP in pile C blown away.
5. **Cube with a TNT core (S4-f).** Slide the side columns crate by crate; the core TNT pair is surrounded so a
   spin from the front reaches it.
6. **Bonus: iron onto TNT (B-d), TNT steps (B-b, B-c).** Explosives are tools for height and for breaking the iron.

## Design logic of this level
1. **Count trap at second zero.** The first crates of the level are behind the camera; the game hints by turning
   Crash round.
2. **The fork is a planning puzzle.** The left branch is short on crates (a 2-stack, two Nitro) but rich in lives;
   the right branch carries the crates. Because the left branch rejoins by a one-way drop, the all-box route is
   left → reverse right → forward; the plaza pair is placed exactly where it saves two crossings of the hard hole.
3. **Nitro forbid the default move.** In spotlight corridors you slide under the beams; a Nitro in the beam
   forces a double jump through instead. The explosive is a rule changer, not a crate to collect.
4. **Hazards clear explosives for you.** Stone pillars crush the POINT 5 Nitro; the lesson is patience.
5. **Same silhouette, changing TNT position.** POINT 6 repeats one 2×3 pile three times with the TNT in a new row
   each time: teach (no TNT), test (middle row), twist (bottom row with a life above).
6. **The densest cluster comes last.** The 16-crate cube with a TNT core sits in the final third, where a mistake
   costs the most progress.
7. **Checkpoints after each hard beat.** Lower C right after the reverse run; C3 right after the pillar pit; C4
   right after the moving blocks.
8. **Optional skill route without crates.** The blue-gem course has no boxes, so the all-box gem never depends on
   a colour gem here (contrast Tomb Time's purple course).
9. **Time trial re-reads the layout.** Masks line up so invincibility arrives before the stone-dropping workers;
   four 1-s crates around C3 merge into one spin once the C crate is gone.

## Transfer to the skate game
1. **Behind-spawn alcove.** 3–4 crates incl. a life 4–6 m behind spawn (+Z); add a camera hint (rider glances back)
   — on the board the alcove is a short kick-turn away.
2. **Fork with a one-way rejoin.** Left branch drops 2–3 m into the merge (one-way); right branch starts with a
   gap a full-charge ollie (1.7 m height, ~5–6 m at speed) clears. Put a crate pair on the plaza edge before the gap
   so riders who think ahead save two gap crossings. Needs camnodes for the reverse run.
3. **Nitro in the slide lane.** Under a low obstacle (dart beam / low ceiling) put a Nitro so sliding or a low grind
   is fatal and the rider must ollie over; any touch kills, so keep 1 crate width of clearance for the ollie.
4. **Crusher clears the explosive.** A timed crusher (pillar) that lands on 2 Nitro on a slippery strip; the blast
   (2.75 m radius) teaches waiting at the edge. Slippery traction must not change edge-grinding policy.
5. **TNT-row piles.** Three 2-wide × 3-high piles (3.46 m) in a row with TNT in no row / middle / bottom. On the

### Geometry and enemy design logic
1. **The floor is the main variable.** Plain tile, tar (ice-like), red-scarab plates (collapse), spear floors and bottomless holes; the skill route (BLUE) removes plain tile entirely. The corridor shape barely changes.
2. **One movement vocabulary, drilled.** Almost every crossing asks for slide → long jump → (double jump) → tornado spin: POINT 3, TT 09–10, TA3, POINT B/D/E/F. The tornado spin is taught as a distance controller, not only an extender (POINT D: land short; POINT E: steer out of the light).
3. **Fork asymmetry.** L opens with the level's longest jump (≈4–6 u) and ends one-way; R opens with two short jumps and a rest island. Lives sit on the hard branch, crates on the easy one, and POINT 2's crate pair is placed so the all-box route never re-crosses R's hole.
4. **One clock for everything.** Doors, flames, pillars and blocks all run from the start (F3 p067, p108R). Stairs before each door (6, 12, 7 steps) are the metronome that brings you in phase (TA4, TT 11).
5. **Hazards that collect for you.** The sliding pillars smash the POINT 5 Nitros; the wait is placed before a one-step drop onto tar, where stopping is no longer possible.
6. **Lane splitting instead of a single line.** M's corridor offers a dart lane and a pit lane; a Nitro in the dart lane forbids the slide, and the expert line runs the pit edge.
7. **Flame on the landing.** Each flamethrower worker stands just beyond a gap or at a hazard exit (R hole, trench exit, far end of the moving blocks, last corridors): the counter is to wait on the take-off side until the flame points away. Only one (after C4) is met alone on flat floor.
8. **Ambushers at short sight lines.** The five coffins stand at bends and stair heads; Penkies hide in pots until Crash is close. Both punish arriving fast, and both are two-step enemies (coffin then mummy; pot then Penky).
9. **Escalating enemy groups.** Penky row ×4 (L) → row ×4 (S4) → a room of ≈8 (POINT 9); the blue course puts its groups on a long-jump landing (POINT D) and at the final door.
10. **Checkpoints right after the hardest timing beat:** upper and lower C behind auto-doors, C3 at the trench exit, C4 after the moving blocks.
11. **Crates as walls.** POINT 6's piles fill a 2-wide walkway and the cube fills its corridor: the crate cluster is the obstacle, so explosive placement inside it is the hazard.
12. **Little recovery, generous timing.** Holes are bottomless and the tar does not forgive, but the timers are slack: an island in the split hole, blocks that pause, a door that stays open if you are already inside.
13. **Bonus geometry from explosives.** TNT lids, a TNT staircase and an iron crate dropped onto TNT are the stairs and keys; falling only ends the bonus.

### Geometry transfer to the skate game
1. **Slick trench with a clearing mover.** A low-traction strip ≈3 crates wide × 6–7 m (5–6 u), stepped 1.15 m below the approach, crossed on a cycle by a full-width `mover` block; two Nitros in the mover's path are cleared on its first pass (blast 2.75 m), so the rider waits on the top edge. Slippery traction must not change the strip's edge-grinding policy.
2. **Hard opener for an optional branch.** A 4–6 u (4.6–6.9 m) gap right after a plaza: past the on-foot full jump (≈5.6 m at 9 m/s) at its long end, so it needs the double jump (+1.74 m) or skate speed. Put lives, not crates, behind it and make the branch one-way (2–3 m drop into the merge, with ordered camnodes for the reverse run).
3. **Split hole with a rest island.** On the other branch, two 1.7–2.3 m holes around a 1.7 m island: two ollies on the board, two hops on foot; a crate pair on the plaza edge before it.
4. **Stair metronome into a door.** 6–12 steps (with a grindable stair rail) feeding a `crusher` door on a fixed cycle, sized so steady hops or one rail grind arrive in phase; put the checkpoint just past the door.
5. **Flame on the landing.** A `firetiki` whose sweep covers the landing of a 1.2–2.3 m gap; the safe line is one tile (≈1.2 m) to the side, which a skater can hold on approach.
6. **Ambushers in groups.** Penky → a pop-up `grunt` (spin on approach); coffin → a two-stage enemy (break the shell, then a `grunt`). Escalate row of 4 → row of 4 → an arena of ≈8 at the gate, and seed `crumble` plates (1.15 m square, 1.15 m apart) right before a checkpoint.
