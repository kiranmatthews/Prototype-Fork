## Puzzle rooms and dependency chains
**P1 — Death route + backtrack (perfect order).** Setup: the death route bypasses the normal path between the fork and the junction, and both carry boxes. Correct (F2 p098R): no deaths until the fork → skull platform → clear the death route (D) → at the junction break C4 → walk the normal path backwards (S4 → S3b) breaking everything, burrowing where possible and spinning bees that now come from the camera side → turn round at the fork → back to the junction → S5. D2 gives the opposite C4 advice (leave it, rely on C3). Wrong: dying before the fork (no death route, so no perfect), or going straight to the goal after the junction. Loss: the box gem. Why it works: one level asks for a no-death run *and* a full reverse traversal, and the camera direction flips the bee threat.

**P2 — Mask in front of Nitro (S2-a).** Jump on the mask crate; a spin can clip the Nitro behind it. The mask is the key to the trap-dense middle, so the level makes you earn it with the careful verb.

**P3 — Burrow economics (S4).** Burrowing breaks crates from below and makes you immune to seeds, but plants killed from underground pay 1 fruit instead of 9 (F2 POINT 15). Crates sit in plant clusters so the player chooses between safety and fruit.

**P4 — Bonus towers and the 1UP (B-d, B-e).** Arrow super-jump onto tower 1, hold jump to tower 2, break the base so the TNT drops, and high-jump from the TNT box to the 1UP crate on the left.

**P5 — Steel arrow + striped crate (S3-c).** The arrow cannot break; the striped crate above pays fruit on every bounce until it breaks.

**P6 — Secret warp (S5).** Body-slam the lone plant on its round platform after C4 to reach the secret warp room and the Road to Ruin alternate entrance. It is the solution to the outline-crate promise in stage 14.

## Design logic of this level
1. **The death route carries boxes** (12, including a Nitro field and its own checkpoint), unlike Road to Ruin's empty one, so the box gem requires a no-death run up to the fork.
2. **The perfect run is a loop.** The death route skips half the normal course, so the all-box player must ride that half backwards; F2 even tells you which way the bees come from on the return.
3. **Checkpoints are placed for the loop**: C3 in the middle of the skipped half ("very welcome"), C4 at the junction, and a death-route C so a slip there does not cost the whole branch.
4. **Speed pads aimed at crate rows** (S1-e, S3-a, S3-d): the boost does the breaking and then points you at the next hazard (stone head, fork, fence).
5. **Both masks come right before the trap-dense stretches**, and the second is guarded by Nitro, so the method of collection matters.
6. **Crates sit next to hives**, so stopping to break them lets a chasing bee arrive: kill first, collect second.
7. **The new verb (burrow) is taught with crates.** The first red-ground groups break when you pass under them, so the mechanic pays before it is tested.
8. **The bonus is a vertical tool puzzle** (arrow, iron-bound pile, TNT towers) and holds the level's only 1UP crate.
9. **One green switch closes three explosive groups** (S2, death route, bonus), and it sits after the junction so it is always reached last.
10. **The level hosts a second cross-level link**: the lone plant after the junction is the entrance to Road to Ruin's hidden section.

## Transfer to the skate game
1. **Speed-pad crate rows**: a boost pad that lifts the board above the 13 m/s plow threshold, aimed at a 3-crate row 10–15 m ahead, with the next hazard 10 m beyond. The pad turns collecting into a line choice.
2. **Mask behind explosives**: an Aku crate in front of two Nitro in a 2.5 m wide lane. Plowing at speed would clip the Nitro (death); the correct take is an ollie-stomp from above or a slow bump.
3. **Reverse-rideable branch**: a hard branch (no-death gate) that bypasses ~40 % of the main line, with crates on both; make the bypassed half rideable backwards (no one-way drops, rails usable both ways) and put a checkpoint mid-way and at the junction.
4. **Arrow + multi-hit crate sandwich**: a steel arrow (16 m/s bounce, ≈3.9 m) with a multi-hit crate floating 3–4 m above it; each bounce pays out until the fifth.
5. **TNT tower with a life on top**: 4–6 crate towers (4.6–6.9 m) with TNT in the upper third; the life crate is reachable only by a double jump from the TNT top during its 3 s fuse.
