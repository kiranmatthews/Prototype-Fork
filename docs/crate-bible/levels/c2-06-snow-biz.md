## Puzzle rooms and dependency chains
- **P1 — TNT row with a life (S1-b).** Setup: TNT, TNT, 1UP in a row on a ledge. Correct: jump at the 1UP (no spin, no standing on TNT), then hit a TNT and step away; the pair chain. Wrong: spinning the nearest crate (blast at your feet) or stomping a TNT while standing beside the 1UP (the blast takes the life crate: count kept, life lost). Why: first explosive in the level; the order of contact matters more than the target.
- **P2 — Bonus switch behind its outlines (B-c).** Setup: outlines first, steel ! further on, under a rock. Correct: hit the !, turn back left, break the now-solid crates. Wrong: moving on after the switch. Loss: ≈ 5 crates and a life. Why: makes the bonus round a two-way room.
- **P3 — Self-clearing TNT wall (B-f).** Setup: 7-crate wall with two TNT inside. Correct: spin a lower crate while moving left, wait at the left edge until the blast. Wrong: standing under the wall (a loose TNT falls on you) or stomping from the top. Loss: death → the bonus tally. Why: one action clears seven crates, but only from the right place.
- **P4 — TNT key for the gem switch (G-e/G-f).** Setup: a TNT perched on a steel arrow, the ! hidden above. Correct: bounce, hit the TNT once, drop and wait; after the blast the ! comes down; hit it, cross the bouncing blocks to the materialised crates. Wrong: leaving before the blast or skipping the ! (F2: "don't miss it"). Loss: the outline crates (all-box only).
- **P5 — Checkpoint last (G-k).** Setup: a checkpoint crate amid four crates. Correct: break the crates first, then the C. Wrong: hitting the C first — after a death those crates reappear. Why: Crash 2's checkpoint stores broken-crate state, so the order protects progress.
- **P6 — Nitro gauntlet + green ! (G-d … G-r).** Setup: ≥ 11 Nitro along the gem course, green ! at its end. Correct: avoid all of them, then hit the green ! to detonate them for the count. Wrong: touching any (death). Why: Nitros are hazards on the way and count only once the run is over.

## Design logic of this level
1. **The all-box goal lives off the main road.** Of 124 crates, 37 are on the normal course (≈ 30 %), 18 in the bonus and ≈ 69 behind the red gem. F2 even says the normal course is easy and has no hidden crates; the perfect is the long gem course.
2. **One stage, two keys from other stages.** The gem course needs Snow Go's red gem, which is reached through Air Crash's secret warp: crate completion becomes a world-level route puzzle.
3. **Every camera mode gets its own crate trick.** 3D: stair pile and TNT row on the line of travel. Side-scroll: an exit into the screen (S2-b) and crates behind foreground rock (S3-a). Ice slope: a float line you overshoot (S2-c). Finale: crates in the lanes of rolling discs.
4. **Masks precede new hazards.** The first Aku crate sits right before the hedgehogs; the second inside the first finale cluster before the disc gauntlet.

## Transfer to the skate game
1. **Into-the-screen pocket.** In a side-scroll zone, put 3 crates (2 + 1 on top) one corridor-width behind a rock column, reachable by stepping off the scroll plane. Tag it as a count trap; it rewards curiosity, not skill.
2. **Downhill float line.** Over a 15–25 m icy or downhill ramp, float 4 crates in a descending diagonal ~1.5–2.5 m above the slope, spaced 3–4 m. On the board at 20–30 m/s an ollie (full charge ≈ 1.7 m) hits one or two per pass; walking back up is the penalty. Never put a pit under it.
3. **TNT row with a life.** On a ledge before a gap: TNT, TNT, life crate in a touching row (it is also a grind rail — riding it at ≥ 7 m/s outruns the blast, which then clears the rest; ≤ 5.5 m/s kills). On foot the lesson is "take the life first".
4. **Self-clearing wall.** A 2-wide, 3–4-high wall with two TNT inside on a bonus platform: one side-spin lights it, the 2.75 m blast clears it. Leave ≥ 3.5 m of floor on one side so a player who walks away is safe.
5. **Switch after outlines.** Place 4–6 outline crates ~10–15 m before their ! switch so the player must turn back; on the board a grind through a ! row is a natural trigger.
6. **Nitro on a collapsing bridge.** Nitro on the deck, a 2-high hanging Nitro (bottom at ~1.2 m: slide or duck under), Nitro on the deck again, every 4–5 m, on a bridge that drops after ~2 s of standing — a jump/slide rhythm. On the board any touch kills, so keep this an on-foot section or leave a clean grind line beside it.
7. **Checkpoint-last cluster.** Ring a checkpoint with 4 crates and tell the player (or teach through a sign) to break them first; supports the target game's own checkpoint-state rules.
