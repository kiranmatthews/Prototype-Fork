## Puzzle rooms and dependency chains
**P1 — Tower A: ignore the springboard (S2-a).** Setup: a worker holds a springboard in mid-road at the tunnel
mouth; a loose life is drawn on the roof. Correct: ride under/through on the road and break the two stacks and the
single inside (5). Tempting: take the springboard to the roof (the life). Loss: 5 boxes → all-box run over (no
backtracking). Why it works: it is the first springboard of the level and the instinct is to use every tool.

**P2 — Tower B: use the springboard (S3-b).** Setup: hole, then springboard. Correct: small jump over the hole,
land on the springboard, bounce to the roof, steer right for the three crates. Wrong: avoid the board. Loss: 3.
Why: inverts P1 — the player who learned "don't bounce" must now bounce.

**P3 — Tower C: jump over the springboard (S4-c).** Setup: hole + springboard, loose life on the roof again.
Correct: big jump clearing both, land in the tunnel, break ?, W, ?. Wrong: bounce (lose 3) or jump late (hit the
stone wall). Why: the third answer for the same prop; the life on the roof is the bait, as at tower A.

**P4 — Floating life crates (S1-f, S2-d).** Setup: crates hanging above the road, shadow on the paving. Correct:
jump as you pass under. Wrong: keep eyes on the road. Loss: 1 + 2 boxes and two lives. Why: lives are the price of
looking up; both sit on straight stretches where a jump is safe.

**P5 — Checkpoint inside a crate rhythm (S1-g, C2 + S3-a, S3-f).** Correct: carve through the group. Wrong: steer
around the "obstacle" crates. Loss: boxes and the respawn point. Why: on a forced ride every pickup must sit on a
line the player is already steering.

## Speed-line analysis (time trial)
- **Time crates outweigh the run.** 22 crates freeze the clock for 29 s; the staff record is 16.9 s on the
  clock (F3 p102L), so the real ride lasts roughly 45 s and two thirds of it is frozen. In this stage the time
  crates are the race, which is why the TT advice is entirely about catching them (P3 s40: hold dash and sweep
  side to side for the time boxes; s41: the first tunnel holds a couple of 1-s boxes).
- **Ride height vs stacks (the key rule).** F3 TT frame 10: Pura crouches just before a jump and on landing. To
  take a time crate on the second tier or higher (tower A stack tops, S2-e top row, tower B roof), jump so that you
  land just before the stack; otherwise you pass through in the low posture and only break the bottom crate. The
  designers put the 1-s crates on the **tops** of the tower-A stacks and the top-left of the S2-e block, so the
  all-box line (anything that breaks the stack) and the time-trial line (exactly timed jump) differ.
- **Slopes:** fr.22 — on an uphill a crate can be missed by jumping just before it; jump at the instant of contact
  to take it and the next one.
- **Speed dissolves hazards, not crates.** At full dash: barrels shatter (advice 2, P3 TT), low dragons need no
  jump (TA2), small crevices need no jump (P3 TT), the hole before tower B is safe (TA3). What remains is steering
  onto crates. F3 (p102L intro) still advises jumping as often as possible and using the wall edges to dodge;
  P3 says jumping can slow you down — the two guides disagree.
- **Lane geometry:** crate groups alternate sides (S1-a L, S1-b R, S1-c L) and the record route uses the parapet
  edges (fr.03, fr.11, fr.26: big jump onto the wall, jump again on landing). Crates on the line are 1–4 per group,
  about one group per screen, never more than a pair across the full road width.
- **Masks in time trial:** the two A badges (tower A, S2-f) turn into Aku crates before the dragon-dense S3, so a
  record run can ignore barrels (fr.25: with Aku just jump the rolling barrels).

## Design logic of this level
1. **One verb set, one direction.** Steer, jump, dash; no attacks, no backtracking. Crate placement is therefore
   pure lane choice and jump timing, and every miss is permanent (gamepressure: expect replays).
2. **Beat structure:** groups of 1–5 crates roughly every screen, separated by hazard beats (holes, barrels,
   dragons, workers). Per-section counts 12 / 15 / 17 / 7: density rises until the crystal and then drops for the
   short run-out.
3. **Teaching by lane alternation:** S1 places three crates left–right–left before the first hole/barrel combo
   asks for the same tempo.
4. **Same prop, three answers.** Towers A, B, C each have a worker-held springboard; the correct use is ignore /
   bounce / jump over, and the crates sit in the tunnel / on the roof / under the tower accordingly. Loose lives on
   the A and C roofs reward the wrong choice with a life while costing crates — a deliberate decoy.
5. **Lives are hung in the air.** Two of three 1UP crates float above the road (S1-f, S2-d); they are the
   reward for looking up and the stage's count traps.
6. **Checkpoints ride with crates.** C1 sits between two wood crates, C2 heads a five-crate slalom, C3 follows a
   crate: the checkpoint is collected by the same carve as the crates.
7. **Explosives and switches are absent.** The difficulty comes from speed and sight-reading, which suits stage 3
   (the first vehicle stage of the game).
8. **Time-trial overlay is the real puzzle:** time crates on stack tops and roofs (height), on slopes (contact
   timing), and in tunnels behind springboard decisions; the 3-s crates sit on tower B's roof and under tower C —
   the two hardest decisions pay the most.
9. **The crystal is on the crate line** (S3-d), so collecting the stage's objective and the block happen together.

## Transfer to the skate game
1. **Three-kicker inversion on a speed line (P1–P3).** Kicker A: crates (2-high stacks are fine) in the lane
   beside/past the kicker — rolling past collects them, launching skips them; a decoy pickup sits on the high
   ledge the kicker reaches. Kicker B: 3 crates on a ledge 3–4 m up that only the kicker reaches. Kicker C: a gap
   plus kicker that a full-charge ollie must clear in one go, with crates on the landing line under the ledge.
   Space them so each lesson is visible from far enough at cruise speed (12 m/s).
2. **Checkpoint as the head of a slalom (C2 + S3-a).** On a downhill line place the checkpoint as gate 1 of a
   5-element slalom, crates ~2–3 m either side of centre, one every 4–6 m, all single height so they are plowed at
   ≥12.5 m/s (8 % tax each, ~33 % over four) — the carve is the collection.
3. **Floating reward above the ollie arc (S1-f, S2-d).** Float a life/mask crate 1.2–1.6 m above the deck on a
   straight: inside a full-charge ollie peak (1.7 m), above a tap ollie (0.56 m). It rewards charging and
   looking up, like the levitating lives.
4. **Stack tops vs ride height (TT fr.10).** On the board at smash speed only the bottom crate of a 2-stack is
   plowed; the top crate settles after the rider has passed and is lost. Put the "extra" crate on top only where
   the line slows down or an ollie into it is natural; on fast lines prefer single-height rows (which are also
   grindable rails in our game).
5. **Speed dissolves hazards — but not TNT in our game.** OE lets dash break barrels; our board detonates TNT
   instantly at ≥12.5 m/s. Keep TNT off plow lines, or precede it with a mask crate as OE's time trial does with its
   two A crates.
6. **Lane-alternating teaching run.** Before the first two-sided hazard on a skate line, place three single crates
   alternating left/right ~3 m off centre, 8–10 m apart, to set the carve tempo.
7. **If a time-trial mode is added:** put 1-s crates on the natural fastest line, and the 2/3-s crates behind the
   hardest decisions (high ledge, under a kicker landing), as OE does.
