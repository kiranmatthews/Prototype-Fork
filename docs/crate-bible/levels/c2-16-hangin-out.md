## Puzzle rooms and dependency chains
**P1 — TNT wall (S2-c).** Setup: three 4-high piles with TNT in their upper halves block the tube. Correct: spin the bottom wood crate of the middle (or right) pile; the stack drops onto/over a TNT, which starts its fuse; run back toward the camera; the chain clears all 12. Tempting wrong: spinning a TNT directly (immediate blast, death) or stomping on the top. Loss: a life (the mask from S2-b absorbs it). Why it works: one input clears a dozen crates, but only if the player reads the stack as a machine.

**P2 — Remote Nitro (S5-a, S5-c, S5-d).** The first Nitro row must be jumped, and the switch that clears it sits further on in front of a second, impassable Nitro wall. The wall explains the switch; the switch pays for both groups (and, by the switch rule, the bonus Nitro).

**P3 — Bonus finale (B-g → B-i).** Land on the TNT ledge (fuse starts), leave it at once, then cross on striped crates that break after their bounces, breaking the lower row left to right. Wrong order or lost count = fall and the bonus is lost (no life lost in a bonus).

**P4 — Secret exit (S4-b).** Going backwards (toward the camera) across the pool after C4 leads to the Totally Fly vortex. D2 advises finishing the level first so the crystal and box gem are banked.

## Design logic of this level
1. **Mode-switch structure**: walking tube → drop → hanging mesh → floor → hanging mesh → drop → pool. Every mode change is marked by a crate cluster (pyramid at drop #1, ?+mask before the floor hazards, drop pile at drop #2).
2. **"Dead end with a hole" is signposted by crates twice** (S1-b, S4-a): the crates make the player stop, and the hole is under them.
3. **Hanging crates teach and test the hanging spin**: 3 under slow patrols, then 2 under faster ones; never more than one crate per patrol window.
4. **Density is low on the long hanging runs** (only 5 crates across three meshes) — the challenge there is timing, and crates are sparing rewards.
5. **The single mask is placed exactly before the two lethal crate structures** (bobbing mines + TNT wall).
6. **Explosive crates as walls**: TNT wall (break by support order) and Nitro wall (break by switch) — the same "wall" read two ways.
7. **The switch is shown before the wall it clears**, and an earlier Nitro row has already made the player want it.
8. **The bonus holds 40 % of the boxes** and is where the dense crate puzzles live (iron-bound tower, Nitro carpet, TNT ledge, striped bridge); the normal course is a timing level.
9. **Four checkpoints at mode changes**, the third and fourth after the hardest mesh runs; C4 is paired with the crystal and the secret fork.
10. **The secret exit carries no boxes**, so the box gem and the secret level never compete within one visit.

## Transfer to the skate game
1. **Signposted drop**: put a 2-3-2-1 crate pyramid (8 crates, ≈ 4.6 m wide) on the lip of a drop-in so the rider stops, breaks, and discovers the drop under it. On the board the pyramid's top layers form short grind rows.
2. **Support-order TNT wall**: three 4-high piles (4.6 m tall — above the 4-stack limit, so they cannot be jumped) with TNT in the upper half; one bottom crate knocked out at ≥ 13 m/s (plowing) drops a TNT and starts the fuse; 3 s is enough to roll 25–35 m back. Put a mask crate 15–20 m before it.
3. **Switch before the wall**: a Nitro row (4 crates, 4.6 m) the rider must ollie (full charge 1.7 m clears one crate in height, so make it a gap jump), then later a `nitrobang` in front of a Nitro wall that blocks the line completely.
4. **Sparse rail-run pickups**: on long grind lines with moving hazards, place single crates on the rail line only where the patrol window allows; at ≥ 12.5 m/s they smash without bailing — one per window.
5. **Striped bridge**: a floating row of multi-hit crates over a pit as a one-way bridge, with a TNT row right before it that must be crossed fast (≥ 7 m/s outruns the blast).
