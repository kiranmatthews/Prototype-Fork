## Puzzle rooms and dependency chains
1. **Arrow-gap column (S1-d).** Setup: two wooden arrows, a floating striped crate, and a 3-crate column floating above the right arrow with a gap. Sequence (F2): left arrow → break the floating striped crate → jump into the gap above the right arrow and bounce up through the column. Wrong: break an arrow first (spin), which loses the tool and strands the column. Loss: up to 4 boxes. Design: arrow as an elevator; the column refills the gap each time a crate breaks.
2. **Bonus reverse trip (B-a…B-g).** Setup: outline lives at the entry, ! at the far end, Nitro mountain between, striped steps as the only easy footing. Correct: break towers → cross on the steps without breaking them → hit ! → come back on the steps → collect the 2 lives + ? → return → break the steps → piston long-jump out. Wrong: smash the steps on the way out, then you must thread the Nitros twice with no footing. Design: a crate is both a reward and a tool, and its value as a tool expires at a known point.
3. **Whole-level reverse run for the box gem.** Setup: the only green ! is at the end of the death route, the death-route pad needs a deathless arrival, and the pad is behind you once you finish the main route. Correct: play the main route and bonus without dying, skip the pad, break the last box (S4-a), walk back, use the surviving ISGN to climb onto the beam turret, take the pad, finish the route, hit the green !. Wrong: kill the ISGN on the way out (the return climb becomes nearly impossible), or die anywhere (the pad stays shut). Design: the level is authored to be played forwards and backwards, and enemy survival becomes part of the puzzle.
4. **Beam-switch plates.** Each plate fires a shrink ray from behind. The ? on its pedestal (S3-b) asks you to stop exactly where stopping is dangerous. The four-plate row before C3 rewards a slide-jump that touches only one plate.

## Design logic of this level
1. **Few, deliberate main-route crates (33)** in a level about enemy patterns and machine timing. Most crates sit where a robot or a beam makes collecting them a timing decision.
2. **Each robot has one correct attack** (YTYT slide-only; ISGN stomp/spin/slide by arm state; researchers spin while the shield is still), and crates are placed to make you read the pattern from a fixed spot (S1-c, S2-a).
3. **Hidden Nitros punish overshoot** right behind a crate stack (S2-b/c). The 8-Nitro columns make a life item a test of nerve.
4. **One authored crate puzzle on the route** (the arrow column), with the rest of the puzzle budget in the bonus (reverse trip) and the level structure (reverse run).
5. **The green ! is hidden in the hardest optional route,** which ties the box gem to the death-route gem. The perfect order (p100R) is forced: normal → bonus → normal → back to the death route.
6. **Lives are locked behind switches** (both 1UPs are bonus outlines), so even the bonus's lives require the reverse trip.
7. **Checkpoint spacing tracks difficulty:** C1 after the arrow puzzle, C2 before the death-route gate, C3 after the plate gauntlet, and a fourth checkpoint inside the death route.
8. **Masks come before gauntlets:** one at the start before the slide-only robots, one after C2 before the plates, the skull pad and the bonus.

## Transfer to the skate game
1. **Arrow-gap column:** a wooden arrow on a step with a 3-crate column floating one crate-gap above it. Our arrow bounce (16 m/s, about 3.9 m) breaks the column bottom-up, and each crate settles onto the column below (our settle rule), so the column keeps feeding the arrow. Put a second arrow and a floating striped crate before it to teach the move.
2. **"Keep the tool" striped bridge:** in a bonus pocket, place 3 striped/multi-hit crates as a step line over a Nitro field, with outline life crates behind the player and a ! at the far end. On the board a row of ≥ 2 equal-height crates is a grind rail that consumes crates as you ride. So grinding the steps on the way out destroys the bridge home, which is a natural skate version of "don't break it yet". Offer the alternative line beside the Nitros at full-charge ollie height (1.7 m).
3. **Enemy-pattern crate posts:** one ? at the exact spot where the player must wait for an enemy's vulnerable state (like ISGN's flat arms) teaches "stop here and read". On the board, put it where cruise speed (12 m/s) carries you into the enemy if you don't stop. Our smash speed (12.5 m/s) means a charged run-up smashes the crate but then hits the enemy.
4. **Overshoot Nitros:** hide 2 Nitros directly behind a staggered 3-crate column on the landing side, within about 1 crate width. On the board the charged ollie that clears the column lands in them, so the safe approach is to stop and break the column on foot.
5. **Nitro-column dare:** two 4-high Nitro columns (4.6 m) with a life floating between them at about 3 m. Reachable with a straight full jump (2.8 m) plus a short double jump. Any sideways drift kills.
6. **Reverse-run level:** let a skate level's optional green ! sit at the end of a hard branch behind the main line, and make the return line a reverse grind (rails authored to be ridden both ways). Gate the branch on a deathless run, as Crash does, only if the return line is fun in itself.
