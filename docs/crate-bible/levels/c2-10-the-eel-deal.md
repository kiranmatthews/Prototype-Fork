## Puzzle rooms and dependency chains
- **P1 Fork order 1 (Nitro room before the bonus).** Setup: fork 1 right is a dead end with one ? among Nitros; fork 1 left leads to C2 and the bonus pad. Correct: right first (? by a careful jump; the Aku from S2-a forgives one touch), back, then left → C2 → bonus. Tempting wrong move: going left first and saving at C2, which makes the walk back to the Nitro room longer (p095R gives the order right → left → bonus). Loss: route time, or a life without the mask.
- **P2 The ? between Nitros (S2-b).** Correct: jump straight up/forward between the first Nitro pair to break the ?. A spin or a sideways drift touches a Nitro. Why: a single collectible framed by explosives turns the all-box goal into a precision test without making the clear harder.
- **P3 Fork order 2 (crystal block before the drop).** Setup: fork 2 left = 9-box block + crystal (dead end); fork 2 right ends in the box room with a one-way hole. Correct: left first, body-slam the block, back, then right. Wrong move: right first, which drops you into the finale with the crystal and 9 boxes behind you (a full replay). Loss: all-box and the crystal.
- **P4 Box room before the hole (S3-e).** Correct: break all 13 (body slam the wall) and only then drop. Wrong move: stepping onto the hole while lining up a slam. Loss: up to 13 boxes, unrecoverable. Why: the "last chance" payout is placed right before the point of no return, and the hole sits in front of the wall.
- **P5 Bonus column (B-b).** Correct: body-slam from the top of the steel-hemmed column; the slam travels down to the 1UP. Wrong move: trying to spin from the side (steel blocks it). Teaches body slam as a vertical tool.
- **P6 Nitro count without visiting the secret.** The green ! at the start of S4 detonates all 22 Nitros, including the secret course's 3. The all-box run never needs the secret, but must reach the finale.

## Design logic of this level
1. **Order is the all-box puzzle.** Two forks, each with a dead end that must be done first (fork-1 Nitro room, fork-2 crystal block), make sequence the main all-box skill. p095R prints the order.
2. **Body-slam walls as count banks.** 22 of the 62 main boxes (35%) are in two hazard-free walls (9 + 13), both slammable at once. They teach the body slam's area effect and give the most boxes for the least risk. The risk lies in the routing around them (dead end, one-way hole), not in the boxes.
3. **Explosives frame single rewards.** The Nitro room has exactly one real box, the ? between the first Nitro pair, and the S3-c ? stack stands beside a Nitro stack. Nitro is used to make one pickup precise, never to guard a big group.
4. **The mask comes before the Nitros.** Both Aku boxes come early. The second sits right at fork 1, just before the Nitro room.
5. **Secret content does not tax the all-box goal.** The secret course holds only 3 Nitros, counted but remotely detonated, so 100% boxes never requires finding the fake wall. The green gem is a separate reward.
6. **The Nitro switch sits at the start of the finale, not the goal.** It sits beside C3 and a ?, so the Nitro count is settled before the last segment and is never lost by dying at the end.
7. **Teaching beats sit at checkpoints.** The striped sandwich (S1-d) is at C1. The ! switch, steel-hemmed body slam and arrow-to-ceiling bounce are taught in the bonus.
8. **Density curve.** 11 / 16 / 31 / 4 (main): low, then a spike from the two walls, then a navigation-only finale. Box density falls as the finale's movement difficulty (hanging, mines) rises.
9. **Pools and fans separate box rooms.** Every box group sits in a calm room or ledge between two hazard tunnels, so the level alternates "hazard corridor → payout room".

## Transfer to the skate game
1. **Dead-end payout wall.** A 2-high × 6-wide crate wall (≈ 6.9 m × 2.3 m) at the end of a short side bay. It can be cleared by a body slam at the wall's midpoint (shock radius 3.4 m reaches about three crates either side, i.e. nearly the whole wall) or by grinding the top row, since the top layer is a rail. Put the bay on the far side of a fork whose other branch is one-way (S3-a/S3-e).
2. **Last-chance wall before a one-way drop.** Crates in front of a drop-off or launch that cannot be ridden back up. Keep the drop edge 2–3 m in front of the wall so a rider lining up a grind or slam can fall in by mistake (S3-e). Mark it with a ? or mask crate at the near end.
3. **One reward between two Nitros.** A single ? floating between two Nitros on a 3–4 m wide walkway. A straight ollie of the right length collects it, while drifting even 0.6 m sideways touches a Nitro (S2-b). Put a mask crate 1–2 crates before the room (S2-a).
4. **Secret route holding only explosives.** If a hidden path holds Nitros counted in the total, place a `nitrobang` later on the main route (at the start of the final segment, beside a checkpoint) so the all-box goal never depends on the secret (S4-f, X-a).
5. **Floating-hazard pool crossing.** Three Nitros floating across a water gap act as the obstacle. The rider ollies between them on a diagonal line (S3-d). Keep ≥1.2 m between Nitro faces along the jump line, because a full ollie only clears one crate height.
6. **Teaching sandwich at a checkpoint.** A striped crate floating above a floor striped beside C1, for repeated bounce fruit on foot. On the board it is a plow-proof pair (multi-hit) that rewards stopping.

### Geometry and enemy design logic
1. **Two room types alternate like a drum beat.** Pipes are slide tests (fans, rolling enemies); pool rooms are timing tests (eel discharges). Crates sit in the calm walkways between them.
2. **The pool is one gap re-dressed five times.** Wide walkways to wait on (pool 1) → sinking pads that forbid waiting (S1) → a mid-pool platform where waiting is sanctioned (fork 1 left) → no footing and three floating Nitro (POINT 8) → five floating platforms with dash-jumps (secret). Same clock, new footing each time.
3. **The eel is the pool's clock, and the telegraph is the water itself** (yellow flashing = live). The counter is precise: enter as the flash ends, leave fast (POINT 8; D2 s31).
4. **Fans are gates with an enemy key.** Sliding a scrubber or knocking a rat into a fan breaks a blade (POINT 1, D2 s26): the first enemy taught is also the first tool.
5. **Counters are taught in contradiction.** Scrubbers want spin or slide; spiky rats forbid the spin. POINT 9 then sends them "one after another", so the test is switching counters, not executing one.
6. **Barrels use the pipe's curvature as a pendulum.** They roll back and forth up the walls; you pass while one is high. The reprise puts a rat between two barrels (POINT 10), so the safe waiting spot is occupied — the Aku tip "wait one beat" is the lesson.
7. **Nitro is architecture.** Edge stacks narrow walkways (S1, S3); the Nitro room builds a 1-u lane (the ? between two Nitro), a 2-high hurdle and the cover for a secret door; floating Nitro replace stepping stones (S3).
8. **Forks pair a dead end with a through route**, and the second fork's through route ends in a one-way hole — geometry, not text, enforces "dead end first".
9. **The point of no return is placed in front of the last payout**: the box room's hole lies between the player and the wall, so lining up a slam carelessly drops you first.
10. **The finale swaps the movement mode.** Hand-over-hand on the mesh is safe from falls but slow (POINT 11), so the threats are indestructible mines you must let pass and then outpace (Aku tip).
11. **Mines escalate by behaviour**: patrolling under the mesh → a sentry that passes on one side → hovering beside a landing → three static (an announced easy beat, "余裕だぜ") → two bobbing under a low ceiling where a dash-jump kills.
12. **The low ceiling punishes the over-jump.** The last gate is passed with a regular jump; the strongest jump is the wrong one (D2 s44).

### Geometry transfer to the skate game
1. **Timed pool as a phasepad crossing.** A 5–6 m `pit` crossed on `phasepad`s (solid on a 3–4 s cycle with the engine's 0.9 s warning strobe): first with a wide waiting ledge, then two pads that must be taken in a one-two, then one pad mid-crossing where waiting is safe, then a crossing threaded between floating Nitro.
2. **Rocking-barrel tube.** A `pendulum` swinging across a 5–6 m-wide tube (bob high at each side) so the player passes while it is up on one side; the reprise puts a `grunt` in the waiting spot between two pendulums. (`stone` rolls only along the corridor's Z axis, so it suits a barrel rolling *toward* the player, not this side-to-side rocker.)
3. **Counter-switch corridor.** Alternate `grunt` (spin) and `spiker` (stomp only) every 4–6 m in a 4 m corridor, after first teaching each alone.
4. **Nitro room with a 1-crate lane.** A ? crate between two Nitro (one 1.152 m lane, approached straight) and a 2-high Nitro hurdle (2.3 m: inside a full jump on foot, beyond a board ollie) — an on-foot room only; mask crate just before it.
5. **One-way drop in front of the last payout.** A 2–3 m floor hole between the player and a 2 × 6 crate wall; the drop leads to the next checkpoint, so breaking the wall first is the player's choice.
6. **Height-capped final gate.** A ceiling at ≈ 4.2–4.6 m (above a full jump's head reach of ≈ 3.8 m, below the double jump's ≈ 5.5 m) over a hazard whose safe crossing is one full jump: the single jump passes, the reflexive double jump bumps the ceiling and drops the player onto the hazard.
