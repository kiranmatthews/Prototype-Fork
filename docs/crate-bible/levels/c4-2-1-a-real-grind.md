## Rail anatomy (the core finding)
How crates relate to the rails, read from the 0.5 s frames:

| Relation | Rail 1 | Rail 2 | How the player collects | Design reading |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Crate **floating above** the rail (≈ one jump above the rider's head) | R1-a, R1-c, R1-e, R1-f, R1-g (5) | R2-c | jump off the rail; you land back on it | the default rail crate: a pure timing test, because you can't fall off |
| Crate **below the rail at hang height** | R1-b (row of 3), R1-d | R2-g (2 at a tower), R2-h | hang under the rail | placed *exactly* under the obstacle that forces you to hang, so **the dodge input is also the collect input** |
| Crate **sitting on the rail line** | — | R2-d | none: grinding into it breaks it | a "free" crate that teaches that the rail line itself collects |
| Crate **beside the rail at rail height** on a prop | R1-i (metal platform at the rail's end) | R2-a (`?` on a car hood at the rail start), R2-e | contact or a spin as you pass | marks the rail entrance/exit; no input needed |
| Crate **at the side, broken by swinging** around the rail | — | R2-f (2) | SWING (hang and rotate) | each new rail verb is paired with crates that only that verb reaches |
| Crate next to **the safe rail** of a twin pair | — | R2-b (3) | SWITCH RAIL to the rail without spikes | the collectible lives on the safe line, so dodging and collecting point the same way |
| TNT / Nitro **on a rail** | none | none | — | rail lethality comes only from authored obstacles (spiked boards/posts, a rock, a spiked cart, a lamp, striped barriers), never from explosive crates |
| **Checkpoint** relative to the rail | C2 on the ledge just before the rail entrance; C3 at the landing after the rail's launch | C4 at the dismount, beside the bonus pad | — | rails are bracketed by checkpoints and never contain one |
| Crates on **platforms between rails** | the slam pyramid + `!` bridge (S5–S6) | — | on foot | puzzle-heavy, slow rooms between the two speed sections |

The obstacle → crate grammar on rail 1 (0:16:17–0:16:35):
1. **Teach:** a crate floats above the open rail (R1-a), so you jump.
2. **Teach hang:** prompt, then a spiked board and spiked post sit *on* the rail with a row of crates at hang height *under* them (R1-b). You hang to survive and break all three.
3. **Mix:** above (R1-c), below (R1-d), above (R1-e).
4. **Test:** a spiked board forces the hang, and the crate floats *above* the rail right after it (R1-f, R1-g). You must hang, unhang and jump in quick succession: the crate now sits on the opposite side of the obstacle.

## Puzzle rooms and dependency chains
- **P1 Phase-Nitro pyramids (S1-k, S1-l, N-1, N-2).** Setup: wood crates rest on Nitros that exist in only one
  phase. Correct (inferred): switch to the phase where the Nitro is absent; the top crate loses its support and
  drops; then break the side crates. Wrong: spinning a side crate while the Nitro is solid risks touching it,
  which kills you. Lost: a life, which matters for the ≤ 3-death gem; the Nitro itself counts later anyway. Design:
  the mask is taught as a way to *remove an explosive*, not only to make platforms.
- **P2 Bounce-on-arrow tool (S3-c).** Correct: bounce the B until it breaks (Wumpa per bounce), then ride the
  arrow to the high crate, then break the arrow. Wrong: a spin at the stack takes both and strands the high crate.
  Lost: 1 crate (all-box) unless you die and reload C2. Design: a tool sits between a reward pile (the B's Wumpa)
  and a goal (the high W).
- **P3 Rail-1 posture chain (R1-a → R1-b → R1-f/g).** Teach jump, teach hang with a crate row under the
  obstacle, then put the crate on the far side of the obstacle. Wrong: staying in hang after the
  board loses R1-f/R1-g. Lost: only crates (you cannot fall), so the rail can be dense and strict at the same time.
- **P4 Slam pyramid (S5-a → S5-b/c).** Correct: (1) take the high crate first by holding jump on the `?`
  (which breaks it); (2) break the middle W row; (3) stomp the TNT and back away, since its blast clears the left W
  but not the R; (4) body slam the R (the LP slams at 0:16:53.5). Wrong: breaking the `?` some other way first
  makes the high crate unreachable; slamming beside the lit TNT costs a life. Design: the lone R on the platform
  before it teaches the slam in isolation.
- **P5 `!` outline bridge (S6-a).** The switch beyond the pyramid creates the only way forward, and the way
  forward is half crates. Design: switch → bridge → crate row.
- **P6 Twin rails and swing (R2-b, R2-f).** Rail choice is forced by the boards and rewarded by the crates.
  A swing prompt is followed by two crates that only the swing reaches.
- **P7 The bonus-pad orbit (T-a, R2-k, T-b).** A hidden crate in the left tyres before the pad, the
  checkpoint beside it and a hidden crate in the right tyres after: three count traps orbit one bonus pad.
- **P8 TNT on iron-bound (S10-d).** The TNT has to go first, and it cannot finish the job.
- **P9 Gem ladder (F-d).** Crates are the steps up to the gem.

## Design logic of this level
1. **Rails are the densest crate terrain.** Rail 1: 12 crates in ~25 s; rail 2: 14 in ~24 s, about one every
   2 s. On foot, the level averages about one every 4–5 s.
2. **On a rail, crates live at three heights: above, at and below the rail.** The obstacle on the rail decides
   which posture you are in, and the first crate of each new posture sits exactly where the dodge puts you
   (R1-b, R2-b, R2-g). Tests then put the crate on the far side of the obstacle (R1-f, R1-g).
3. **No explosive crate ever sits on a rail.** Rail deaths come only from obstacles. Crates on rails are pure
   reward, so missing one costs count, never a life. The rails can therefore be strict without being cruel.
4. **Each rail verb is introduced by a prompt and immediately paid with crates** (HANG → row under the board;
   SWITCH → crates by the safe rail; SWING → two side crates).
5. **Checkpoints bracket the rails and never sit on them:** C2 on the entrance ledge, C3 at rail 1's landing,
   C4 at rail 2's dismount.
6. **Slow puzzle rooms sit between the speed sections.** After rail 1 come the slam lesson, the slam pyramid
   and the `!` bridge, and the bridge leads straight onto rail 2.
7. **Explosives teach immunity.** TNT clears wood but not iron-bound (S5-b, S10-d). TNT is counted, so the
   bonus uses TNT stacks as crates.
8. **Mask pacing.** Phase is taught at the start, crates and Nitros are tied to it, it is taken away for the
   rails (focus on rail verbs) and returned for the bonus and the finale.
9. **Count traps orbit the bonus pad** (left tyres, the checkpoint, right tyres). The high crates (S2-a,
   S3-c, S5-c, F-a) are the other trap family, each guarded by a tool you must not waste.
10. **Nitros count only at the end.** All 6 sit in the phase sections; the detonator at the exit turns "avoid
    them" into "collect them by reaching the end".
11. **Signposting by crate.** The Aku crate marks the hidden left path (S9-a); the `?` on the car hood marks
    rail 2's entrance (R2-a); a W on a metal platform marks rail 1's exit (R1-i).
12. **The all-box goal adds three detours** (left path 8, tyres 2, high crates 4); the rails add none, but they
    demand clean posture changes.

## Transfer to the skate game
1. **"Dodge = collect" lane under an obstacle (R1-b).** Our rider cannot hang, but our crate rows are grindable.
   Put a steel crate on the main rail line (a bail) and, 1 crate (1.15 m) lower and 0.8–1.2 m to the side, a row
   of 3–4 touching wood crates parallel to the rail as a second grind line. Dropping onto the crate row dodges the
   steel, and riding it breaks each crate as you leave it. Start the row 2–3 m before the steel crate and end it
   4–6 m after, so the player must hop back up to the rail before the last crate breaks under them.
2. **Decouple after teaching (R1-f/g).** Once (1) has been shown, keep the steel obstacle but float the wood
   crate 1.2–1.5 m above the main rail top, about 4–5 m beyond the obstacle at 15 m/s (the apex of a full ollie,
   peak ≈ 1.7 m, ≈ 0.64 s air). Player sequence: hop down to dodge, hop up, ollie at once. Allow ≥ 0.8 s between
   inputs (12–18 m at grind speed).
3. **Twin rails with alternating hazards (R2-b).** Two parallel rails 2.3 m apart (2 crate widths), 40–60 m long.
   Alternate steel crates (bail) between the rails every 15–20 m. Place plain wood crates on the *safe* rail's line
   (smashed at ≥ 12.5 m/s with an 8 % speed tax) and a Wumpa trail on the safe rail. Reserve TNT/Nitro on a rail
   for later levels: ARG never puts an explosive on a rail.
4. **One free crate on the rail line (R2-d).** On a downhill single rail, where the rider is reliably above
   12.5 m/s, put one wood crate on the line. It teaches "crates on the line break as you ride through". Never put it
   right after a slow entry, or it becomes a bail.
5. **Tool stack at the rail approach (S3-c).** Before an elevated rail start, a multi-hit crate on top of a wooden
   arrow under a column with a wood crate on top 3.5–3.9 m above the arrow's lid (plain arrow bounce ≈ 3.9 m).
   The board plows wooden arrows at ≥ 12.5 m/s, so the player must arrive slowly: teaches braking for tools.
6. **Checkpoint placement.** One checkpoint on the approach ledge before every rail, one at the landing after
   the rail's launch, none on rails. Give each rail 12–14 crates over 20–30 s.
7. **Switch-made crate bridge into the next rail (S6-a).** A `!` materialises an outline bridge of steel and
   wood crates across the gap to rail 2. Under our rules a mixed row is one grind ledge: the wood breaks as it is
   ridden and the steel survives, so the bridge is one-way, and a second pass leaves only the steel stepping stones.
8. **Slam pyramid with a reinforced base (S5-b).** For our multi-hit crate (5 stomps) and body slam (≈ 3.4 m shock):
   `?` on 3 wood on [wood, multi-hit, TNT, multi-hit], with a wood crate floating about 6 m above ground (the `?`
   lid is at 3.46 m, plus a ≈ 3 m stomp bounce). Order: stomp the `?` for height first, then the TNT
   (on foot it is a wall: stomp it and retreat), then the slams.
