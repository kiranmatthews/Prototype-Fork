## Puzzle rooms and dependency chains
- **P1 Ice pair 1 (S2-a).** Setup: W left, ? right on ice, right after a fallen totem you must jump. Correct: come round the totem on the left, jump, skid right through both. Wrong move: arriving centred, or trying to steer on the ice. You get one of the two. Loss: all-box, and a replay from C1. Why it works: the line-up happens before a mandatory jump, so the player must plan two inputs ahead.
- **P2 Ice pair 2 (S4-b).** Same idea, harder: the first box is striped and the take-off is from the middle. You must hold right the instant it breaks. Teaches the "hold toward the next box" rule (p097L text).
- **P3 Big-hole landing field (S2-c/S2-d).** Correct sequence: burst, then jump angled left → land on W (left line) → ?, ? → cross right behind the TNT → ?, W, ? → jump the seals → land on the right W → jump the TNT → striped. Wrong move: landing centred or right, which runs into the TNT or skips the left line. Loss: up to six boxes, or a life if you hit the TNT. Why: the TNT at the head of the right line dictates the sweep order, so the explosive is the puzzle key.
- **P4 Hidden ? and C3 in the totem forest (S3-d).** You must know the box is behind a totem and weave to it while threading the forest. A blind run loses it. Occlusion as a count trap.
- **P5 Green ! at the goal (S4-f).** Required for the two Nitros (S4-d).

## Design logic of this level
1. **A sequel to Bear It: same rules, longer jumps, a new timed obstacle.** The worker slab (lowered = jump, raised = pass under) is the new element. Boxes are placed as rewards right after it (S2-b, S4-a) or to set the approach lane (S3-b).
2. **Landing-marker boxes grow into landing fields.** Bear It uses single landing boxes. Here the big hole lands into a 10-box, two-line field whose first box is exactly where an angled-left jump lands (POINT 9).
3. **Explosives as keys, not fields.** Only 4 explosives in 42 boxes. Each one dictates order or timing: the right-line TNT forces left-first (S2-c), the second TNT forces a jump (S2-d), the Nitro pair is a hurdle across a strip with no way round (S4-d).
4. **Ice pairs come back with escalation.** Pair 1 is W + ? entered from the left. Pair 2 is striped + ? entered from the middle with an instant hold-right. The perfect page names both as the top misses.
5. **Occlusion is a count trap even on rails.** A ? and the C3 hide behind totems (POINT 15). This is the only hidden placement in the bear stages and is signposted on the perfect page.
6. **The opening block teaches by abundance.** Four boxes in a 2×2 right at the mount point bank 10% of the level before any hazard and show the ram mechanic.
7. **The density curve peaks in the middle.** 10 / 15 / 8 / 9: the big-hole field makes S2 the heaviest section. S3 is deliberately calm (POINT 11 "relax") before the hidden-box forest.
8. **Fruit trails can lie.** At S3-c the fruit leads right while the box asks for a cut back left. The all-box line and the fruit line are not the same.
9. **Checkpoint spacing tightens toward the end.** C1 after 10 boxes, C2 after 25, C3 after 33 of 42, so the last segment (9 boxes, two ice/Nitro tests) is short to retry.
10. **Secret without boxes.** The live warp icon is a hidden exit (the round foothold at the end), not a box route. The header superscript stays 0.

## Transfer to the skate game
1. **Timed barrier gate + reward.** A moving gate that alternates between low (ollie it: 1.7 m full charge clears one crate height, 1.15 m) and high (ride under it). Place 2–3 plow-able crates in one lane 5–10 m after the gate as the reward (S2-b, S4-a), and one crate before it to fix the approach lane (S3-b).
2. **Landing field after a charged gap.** After a gap that needs charged speed (23 m/s), put a two-line field of 6–7 wood/? crates. The first crate sits at the landing point on one side, and a TNT heads the other line, so the order is left first, then cross (S2-c). The TNT must sit at least 3.3 m from the left line. Remember board contact with TNT at ≥13 m/s detonates it and kills.
3. **Diagonal ice pair.** On a low-traction pad, two crates one lane apart laterally and ~2 crate lengths apart along the route, entered from the opposite edge (S2-a). Repeat it later with a mask or ? second and a stricter entry (S4-b). Edge grinding stays at its default.
4. **Nitro hurdle across a narrow strip.** Two Nitros side by side spanning a ~2.5 m strip between water. With a full-charge ollie (1.7 m) the rider clears one crate height (1.15 m) with ~0.5 m to spare. Make it the only obstacle in that stretch and put a landing crate 6–8 m after it (S4-d → S4-e).
5. **Occluded crate in a pillar forest.** In a dense prop cluster on a fast lane, put one crate behind a pillar, 1 lane off the obvious line, plus the checkpoint nearby (S3-d + C3). Signpost it with a fruit/coin trail that briefly bends toward it.
6. **Opening crate block.** A 2×2 wood/? block at the board pickup that a charged rider plows (S1-a). It is 2 crates tall: plowing the bottom row lets the top crates settle onto the column below (crate support rule), so a second pass or a grind along the settled row collects them.

### Geometry and enemy design logic
1. **The title is the escalation axis, but tuned to the plain jump.** Wide ponds (S1 POINT 2, S2 POINT 9) ask for the speed burst, yet F2 p097L says a normal jump from the very edge reaches. A 'big jump' sits at the plain jump's maximum range from the lip; the burst only widens the take-off window.
2. **Almost every gap lands on something that matters**: a ? (S1-b), a crate pair in one lane (S1-d), a crate field whose first box sits on the angled-left landing (S2-c), an island (POINT 13), the crystal strip, or the next slab gate (POINT 10, 14). The landing, not the width, is the design.
3. **The new obstacle is a two-state gate read at speed.** The worker slab is lowered (≈ 1 u, jump) or raised (≈ 2-2.5 u, ride under). Its seven appearances each add one coupling: alone and lowered (P7) → raised with a hole behind (P8) → raised on a pond's landing (P10) → lowered then an orca hole (P12) → orca hole then lowered (P14) → a double gate with a fake-out hold (P16) → raised as the final gate (P19).
4. **Jump height becomes the advanced skill.** Bear It always wanted full jumps; here POINT 4 (double narrow pond), POINT 10 (land early) and POINT 14 (small hops or 'bang your shins on the slab') make the SHORT jump correct. The gaps are shortened on purpose so a full jump overshoots into the next hazard.
5. **Orca holes are deterministic side gates whose side is named and then flipped**: left at holes 1-2 (teach by repetition), right at POINT 12 (flip), left again in S4. F2 states the side each time.
6. **Ice patches return three times with rising stakes**: with a W + ? pair entered from the left (S2), empty (S3, rehearsal with nothing to lose), and with a striped + ? pair entered at an angle with an instant hold (S4). The empty middle patch teaches without punishing.
7. **Rest is declared, and density alternates.** S1 opens with abundance (the 2×2 block) before any gap; POINT 11 tells the player to relax after the S2 peak; S2 and S4 are dense, S1 and S3 calmer.
8. **Width is a difficulty dial.** The road is ≈ 5-7 u through S3 and becomes a ≈ 2-3 u strip between ponds in S4, where two Nitro fill the whole width: lateral escape is removed exactly when the final jump chain starts.
9. **The finale recombines every verb on one strip**: plain break → Nitro hurdle (early jump) → wider break (dash-jump) → raised slab (no jump). Four different answers in a row (D2 s26-s28).
10. **No enemy can be attacked on the bear.** Workers, orcas and seals are gates and hurdles placed where a jump is already needed: in pits (orcas), across the road on a take-off or landing (workers), inside a crate field (seals).
11. **Fruit lines time and mislead.** D2 s12: take off as you pick up the last fruit before the big hole. At S3-c the fruit trail bends right round totems beside a water channel while the crate asks for a cut back left.
12. **Totems change role four times**: slalom posts (S1), a gate round a crate (S1-e), lying hurdles (S2), and an occluding forest that hides a crate and the checkpoint (S3).
13. **Checkpoints sit on safe banks right before a new combination**: C1 after the S1 pond, C2 beside the raised gate that ends S2, C3 inside the forest just before the double gate. The last section is short (9 crates) but holds the hardest chain.

### Geometry transfer to the skate game
1. **Two-state bar gate.** A horizontal `mover` bar that alternates between low (top ≈ 1.0 m: a full-charge ollie, apex ≈ 1.7 m, clears it) and high (underside ≥ 2.3 m: ride under). Teach it alone in the low state, then raised with a 3-4 m pit right behind it (duck, then ollie), then raised on the landing of a short pit (land short), then a low-high double with a long first hold.
2. **'Big jump' tuned to lip range.** At cruise (12 m/s) a full ollie carries ≈ 6.6 m; make the wide pit ≈ 6-6.5 m lip to lip so a lip ollie at cruise just makes it, and put a `speedpad` or a ≥ 20 m charge run-up before it for margin. Put the first crate of a two-line field 1-2 m past the landing lip, with a TNT heading the other line so the sweep order is fixed.
3. **Short-jump traps.** Two pits ≈ 2.3-3 m long with a ≈ 3 m strip between: a charged ollie (≥ 6.6 m) overshoots into the second pit, so a tap ollie (apex ≈ 0.56 m) or an uncharged hop is the answer. Measure the tap-ollie distance in the probe before relying on it.
4. **Narrow-strip finale.** A 2.3-3.5 m strip between pits holding: pit 1 (plain ollie), a Nitro pair spanning the strip (full-charge ollie clears one crate, 1.15 m, by ≈ 0.5 m; touch = death), pit 2 (needs charge), then a raised `mover` bar to ride under. Space the beats ≥ 6-7 m apart at cruise so each touchdown lasts ≈ 0.5 s.
5. **Alternating side gate.** A pit split into left and right halves with two `crusher` or `firetiki` hazards firing in alternation. Repeat the same safe side twice in the first section, flip it in the third, and put a crate on the take-off of the wrong side once the rule is known.
