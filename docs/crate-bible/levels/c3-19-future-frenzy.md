S1 rhythm: three movement problems, each paired with crates. Belts (S1-b/c/d) teach that a crate over a moving floor
needs a lead; fences (S1-g, S1-h) put crates where you would stop anyway, so the cost of a crate is one fence cycle;
explosives escalate from a TNT lid (S1-e) to a Nitro corner (S1-f) to a Nitro ring (S1-h). The section ends with the
iron cube, a slow reward on safe ground. Every cluster is on a stationary pad or directly on the belt the player
must ride — no crate here needs a detour, but almost every one needs a stop.

## Puzzle rooms and dependency chains
- **P1 Nitro ring (S1-h).** Setup: one ? closed on three sides by Nitros, just past a fence. Correct: stop before the
  ring, approach from the open side, spin (or bounce the top). Tempting wrong move: arriving at fence-dodging speed and
  spinning into the cluster. Loss: a life; the Nitros themselves still count once the green ! fires. Why it works: the
  fence before it makes the player hurry exactly where precision is needed.
- **P2 TNT lid (S1-e).** Setup: TNT on ? on W. Correct: stomp the TNT and step off (the blast takes all three) or spin
  the base. Wrong: bounce-chain down from the top as on any other stack. Loss: none for the count, a life if you stay.
  Why: the first TNT is taught as a lid; in Future Tense the same stack is met from the far side (P3 s57).
- **P3 Iron cube (S1-i).** Setup: 8 iron-bound boxes. Correct: body slams from above, layer by layer, on a stationary
  pad. Wrong: spinning/jumping (nothing happens) and moving on. Loss: 8 boxes — the largest single loss in the stage.
- **P4 TNT wall + spring (S3-f).** Setup: a stepped wall of 6 TNTs with a spin-jump stand in front. Clear goal: ride the
  stand over the wall. All-box goal: light one TNT and retreat; the chain removes the wall and counts all six. Same

## Design logic of this level
1. **No gem, full count.** The stage gives no diamond, yet carries 87 boxes and a hidden green !. The crates are built
   for their second life in Future Tense, whose checkpoint counts include them pass by pass.
2. **Crates price the timing hazard.** Beam fences fire on a cycle; crates sit where a stop already happens (before a
   fence, on a stationary pad). Each cluster costs one cycle, so collecting everything roughly doubles the fence
   encounters — the all-box run is a timing test, not a search.
3. **Moving floors demand a lead.** S1-b/c/d are placed over or at the edge of a treadmill, so a straight-up jump misses;
   the edge crate (S1-c) is bait because the belt keeps pushing.
4. **Explosive escalation within one section:** TNT lid (S1-e) → Nitro corner in a 2×2 (S1-f) → Nitro ring (S1-h) →
   later a Nitro foundation (S4-a). Each changes which approach direction is safe.
5. **TNT as tool, wall and weapon:** TNT kills a robot (S3-b) and forms a wall that a spring clears (S3-f); only one TNT
   in eight is a pure hazard (S1-e's lid).
6. **Vertical hiding in the side-scroll:** six clusters sit below platform lips (S2-b, S2-c, S3-b, S3-d, S3-g, S4-b)
   plus the green ! (S4-e). With nothing to hide behind in a side view, "below the ledge" is the count trap.
7. **One big reinforced deposit:** 8 of S1's 25 boxes are a single iron cube on a safe pad — a deliberate slow beat
   after the belt-and-fence rush.
8. **Masks as rewards for flawless time trial:** two crates become Aku boxes only in TT; collecting all three masks by
   C2's pad gives invincibility through the three-fence belt (TA6). Crates double as timing beacons.
9. **Density curve:** S1 25 + 4N in 9 clusters (dense, varied), S2 10 (sparse, platforming), S3 20 + bonus 19 (the
   reward peak), S4 8 + 1N (short run-out with the green ! trap).
10. **Checkpoint spacing follows camera changes:** C1 right after the pod's camera switch, C2 at the stage-29 merge, C3
    after the hardest fence trio.

## Transfer to the skate game
1. **Fence-cycle pricing.** Put a 2-crate pair (wood + ?) 3–4 m before a timed gate on a straight. A cruising rider
   (12 m/s) who charges above 12.5 m/s smashes through but reaches the gate early; a slower rider must brake. Repeat
   with the pair *after* the gate so the reward moves from "before" to "after" the timing.
2. **Nitro ring as a precision stop.** A single ? with Nitros on three sides on a flat pad, open side facing the
   approach. On foot it is a spin target; on the board it forbids charged runs (Nitro = death on any touch). Leave
   ≥ 2 crate widths (≈2.3 m) of clear run-off beyond the ring.
3. **Reinforced deposit.** An 8-crate iron-bound cube (2×2×2, top at ≈2.3 m) on a stationary pad off the skate line:
   the board cannot break it (steel-like at speed = wall crash), so it marks a dismount-and-slam stop. Do not place it
   in a rail line.
4. **TNT wall + launcher.** Six TNTs in a stepped wall (1-2-2-1, tops 1.15/2.30 m) right after a wooden arrow or
   spring. Clear goal: arrow bounce (≈3.9 m, 6.1 m held) over it. All-box goal: stomp one TNT and retreat (3 s fuse
   ≈ 27 m on foot). On the board a 3–12 m/s bump lights the fuse safely; ≥ 13 m/s kills — so the wall also teaches
   "slow down near TNT".
5. **Nitro-foundation stack.** ? on wood on Nitro. Our crates settle when support breaks but Nitro is exempt, so the
   top two stay where authored; the player must break them from above or mid-air. Do not let a crate row run across the
   Nitro (a grind onto Nitro below 6.5 m/s kills).
6. **Under-ledge count traps in side-scroll zones.** Hang 1–2 crates one crate height below a platform lip
   (≈1.15 m drop) every 15–20 m in a side-scroll zone; the skater drops, collects and ollies/double-jumps back.
7. **Green ! under the final platform.** Hide the level's `nitrobang` one drop below the last platform before the gate
   so the all-box goal costs one deliberate detour at the end.

### Geometry and enemy design logic
1. **Two halves, two problem types.** The into-screen S1 has no forced jump (TA1): its content is timing (fences, belts, BKBKs) on open-edged ≈3 u walkways. The side-scroll S2–S4 is platforming: flip discs, holes with metalnics, saucer bridges, launchers. The pod and C1 mark the switch.
2. **The beam fence is the metronome.** About eight fences in S1 fire on one schedule from the start (TT intro: the first fence's timing carries to the rest). They escalate from a single fence to a diagonal pair to two in a row, then return in side view on pads, and end as three in a row over a moving belt, where the answer changes from waiting to crouching (callout 6).
3. **Open edges replace gaps in S1.** With no gap on the line, the only fall is sideways off a walkway or belt, which is exactly where dodging a fence or a BKBK pushes you; the edge crate S1-c sits on that line.
4. **Belts make depth unreadable.** F3 warns that belt-ways running both ways confuse depth (p087L) and carry you into monsters or off the end (p086). Crates float over the first belt so that every pickup needs a lead.
5. **Periodic enemies, not chasers.** BKBKs are invulnerable while lit (pass beside, or wait for the light — callout 2, P3 s67), metalnics are dangerous while their spikes are out, saucers drift. Every enemy has a cycle to read, matching the fence rhythm; none chases.
6. **Before the bazooka, explosives kill the invulnerable.** On the first visit the player has no bazooka (it comes from N. Gin after stage 20), so a lit BKBK can only be removed by a blast: the S3-b TNT sits under its pad (P3 s68).
7. **Metalnics on hole edges and landings.** The disc lands you beside one (TT 16), another stands at a hole edge (TT 19–20: run to the edge and high-jump both), another on the last landing (callout 7). One high jump clears hole and enemy together.
8. **Enemies as footing.** The side-scroll's saucers are platforms: TT bounces from the Aku box onto one (13–14), and callout 7 crosses the last hole by stomping a saucer and double-jumping over a metalnic. Hanging spikes over the treadmill cap the height of that bounce.
9. **A telegraphed phase platform, introduced alone.** The first flip disc (callout 4: board only when horizontal; green safe, red flashing danger) sits over a plain pit; it returns later as a chain of three small discs.
10. **One device, three meanings.** The spin-jump stand is first an optional detour that the time trial must refuse (TA5), then the tool that clears a TNT wall (callout 5), then the required launcher over a gap (P3 s72, TT 25).
11. **Ceilings shape the jump.** A steel-block ceiling forces the slide at the start of S2 (TT 10), hanging spikes cap bounces over the treadmill, and the last fence trio is passed crouching.
12. **Checkpoint placement follows the camera and the hardest test:** C1 right after the pod, C2 at the Future-Tense merge pad, and C3 (by F3's pointer) at the start of the three-fence belt.
13. **Bonus as a signposted long jump.** Signs show the double jump + tornado spin (P3 s73), and the room ends with the widest gap of the stage (≈6 u) crossed through a floating row with a 1UP at the peak.

### Geometry transfer to the skate game
1. **Fence corridor without gaps.** An into-screen run of open-edged walkways ≈3.5 m (3 u) wide with a timed gate every 8–12 m (a `crusher` or `firetiki` across the lane plays the beam fence), all on one global clock and no jump needed; place crate pairs where riders stop for a cycle.
2. **Telegraphed phase platform.** A `phasepad` over a 4.6–5.8 m (4–5 u) pit, first alone, then as a chain of three 1.2 m pads; give it a colour telegraph (green = solid, flashing red = about to drop) like callout 4's disc.
3. **Spike robot at the hole edge.** A `spinner` on the take-off or landing edge of a 1.7–2.3 m gap, so one full jump (≈2.85 m high, ≈5.6 m long at 9 m/s) or one ollie clears both; a second `spinner` on the landing of the final gap.
4. **Enemy bridge.** Over a 3.5–4.6 m hole, a stompable hover enemy on a `mover` (a `grunt` on a moving pad — not a `floater`, which cannot be stomped) as the footing, with a `spinner` on the far pad; cap the bounce with an overhead hazard so only a double jump (+1.74 m), not an extra-high bounce, is safe.
5. **Launcher with three meanings.** Use a `trampoline` three times: an optional detour early (a slower line), a tool that clears a 1-2-2-1 TNT wall (1.15/2.3 m tops), and finally the only way over a 6–8 m gap.
6. **Fence trio over a speed strip.** End the fence half with three gates over a `speedpad` strip whose answer is the slide or a low tuck rather than timing — the posture twist of callout 6.
