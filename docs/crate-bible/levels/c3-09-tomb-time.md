## Puzzle rooms and dependency chains
1. **Bonus 4-stack (B-e).** Setup: ?/W/TNT/W column below a high ledge with a 1UP. Correct: spin the bottom two;
   the TNT drops (fuse lit by the fall), ride down, jump from the top wood to the ledge before the blast. Wrong:
   stomping from the top lights the TNT with no way up, or breaking the top first leaves no step to the ledge →
   the life is lost (route intact). Why it works: one object is both the tool and the timer.
2. **Purple outline bridge (P-f → P-g → P-h → P-i).** Correct: ! #1, cross the materialised bridge, break the
   3-stack, break the bridge cells from the walkway/platform edge, then either walk back (P3) or hit ! #2 and run
   the right course backwards (F3 POINT E). Wrong: hitting ! #2 and riding out before breaking the bridge or the
   stack → those boxes can only be recovered by replaying the course (all-box loss).
3. **Floating crates over the tar slide (S4-a/b).** The slope's momentum carries you past two high ? crates; you
   must deal with the switch operator and come back (P3 s63). Missing them is the most likely all-box miss on the
   normal route.
4. **Reverse run (F3 perfect route).** Purple course → pads → junction → right course backwards to the branch
   (POINT 6: go slowly; the moving platform is visible from the hole edge), then forward again. The level's
   mechanisms (doors, pads) are built to work in both directions.

## Design logic of this level
1. **Front-loaded count.** 12 of S1's 17 boxes are one wall at the start; the all-box total is built from safe
   deposits early and scattered singles later.
2. **Crates mark safe ground.** On the scarab floor every crate sits on a framed solid tile; the player learns the
   tile language by where the crates are.
3. **Mechanism → reward cadence.** Every floor-button door or ! ride is followed within a few metres by a small
   cluster (S1-c, S2-d, S2-h, S3-b, S3-d). Crates are the receipt for solving the machine.
4. **Teach then test the movement verb.** POINT 1 teaches the tar slide-dash with nothing at stake; POINT 3 and 7 and
   the purple POINT D demand it over holes and pillars.
5. **One deliberate danger-zone cluster per corridor.** S2-f (light beams) and P-b (dart pit) are the only crates
   inside a hazard's line; everything else is off the firing line.
6. **The !-block means "machinery" here, not "crates".** Three of the four ! blocks move platforms; only one
   materialises outlines. No main-route crate depends on a switch — the switch-dependent crates are all behind the
   gem door.
7. **Gem lock holds a quarter of the boxes.** 25 boxes (plus the secret clear gem) need the purple gem, so the
   all-box gem is a return-visit goal; the left-fork Aku crate is visible bait for the locked door.
8. **Checkpoint spacing follows difficulty.** 17 → 35 (with the bonus) → right course; C2 sits exactly at the fork
   so both branches restart from it.
9. **Time trial re-reads the same layout.** "A" crates turn into masks so that three masks land just before the
   corridor where a floating 3-s crate is reachable only with the invincible hitbox (S4-b).
10. **Count trap by momentum.** The tar slide pushes the player past the two floating ? crates; the level expects
    a deliberate return.
11. **Life after the hardest timing section.** The only main-route 1UP (S3-d) sits after the falling pillars and
    moving platforms.

## Transfer to the skate game
1. **Safe-tile crates on a collapsing floor.** Build a drop-tile strip (2–3 m tiles) with crate PAIRs only on the
   solid tiles. A rider who stops to smash (≥ 13 m/s plow or a hop-off) learns which tiles hold. Keep the pairs
   1 crate high so a full-charge ollie (1.7 m) clears them for players who skip.
2. **Start wall as a speed lesson.** A 4-wide × 2-high wall (2.30 m) 10–15 m after spawn: on foot it is a free
   12–8 crate deposit; on the board it teaches the smash threshold (≥ 13 m/s plows, ~20 % speed loss per crate).
   Avoid 3-high (3.46 m) if the wall should stay grindable as a ledge once the top layer is gone.
3. **! bridge over a hazard.** A `bang` that materialises a 2×3 outline bridge over a pit: the bridge is both the
   way across and six crates. Because crate rows are rails, grinding the bridge consumes it — let a rider choose:
   ride across (keeps the bridge) or grind it (gets the crates, loses the way back).
4. **Floating pair over a downhill.** Two crates ~3–4 m above a downhill run (our downhill up to 30 m/s): reachable
   only with a kicker/wooden arrow launch or by walking back up; a deliberate return-trip count trap like S4-a/b.
5. **Gated side corridor with ~25 % of crates.** Lock a side branch (gate opened by a later collectible or a
   switch), exit it onto the main route near the end, and make the main route rideable backwards with camnodes for
   both directions (Tomb Time's reverse run).
6. **Door + floor switch + reward.** A timed gate opened by a floor switch (our slide/grind triggers !): put a crate
   pair 3–5 m past the gate so a rider who makes the window is paid immediately.
7. **Mask before the hazard corridor.** A striped-over-striped sandwich plus a mask crate right before a crusher or
   dart corridor (S2-e).
