## Puzzle rooms and dependency chains

1. **Red timed ! run (S2-b).** Setup: GR's instruction is to stop at the red !. Hitting it makes five pink crates solid
   along the path to its far end. Correct: hit it, sprint, break all five in order. Wrong: detours, or breaking the path
   crates on the same run. Lost: 5 crates. Why: the newly acquired gravity run is tested at once against a clock.
2. **Three-high TNT stack (S2-c).** Knock out the bottom crate; the TNT drops lit; step away; the blast takes the top
   crate. Wrong: spinning the TNT. Lost: a life.
3. **TNT under a strong crate at the bonus door (S5-b).** Jump and whirlwind into the strong crate from above, then light
   the TNT from range. Wrong: a ground-level whirlwind into the stack. Lost: a life, with C4 far back. Why: it punishes
   spinning blindly with the multi-hit tool just after the tool made spinning feel safe.
4. **Bonus loop (B-a → B-f).** Out: blow the framed TNT before its frame turns to iron, bounce to the pillar, hit the !
   under the strong crate, thread the Nitro/fruit pairs, press the Nitro switch. Back: sweep to the entrance for the
   newly solid crates. Out again: the far-side column, then the exit saucer. Wrong: leaving after the Nitro switch, which
   costs 10. Lost: all-box only. Why: a one-screen room is made to be crossed three times, each crossing with new geometry.
5. **Final Nitro-capped TNT stack (S8-d).** Light the TNT and retreat; the blast takes the Nitro and the stack (HUD 148 → 152).

## Design logic of this level

1. Two powers reshape the crate grammar. The Ika-Ika gravity mask, taken at crate ≈ 17, puts crates on a floor you can no longer stand on (S4-a). The Akano whirlwind, at C4, makes long corridors of stacks safe to clear at a run.
2. TNT is the counter-design to the whirlwind: every TNT in a whirlwind stretch sits under or beside crates (S5-a, S5-b, S6-b, S7-a, S8-d), so a spin through the stack is lethal and the player has to light TNT deliberately.
3. The 34-crate bonus (22 % of the level) is a loop: out to a far-end Nitro switch, back to the entrance for crates that only now exist, and out again for a column on the far side of a pillar. Ten of its crates are behind the player at the moment the room looks finished.
4. The bonus mixes two outline behaviours: outline crates that a ! turns into breakable crates (counted), and 'invisible blocks' that turn into iron after a delay. Iron can seal a TNT inside its frame, so the room's first lesson is to act before the frame closes.
5. The shifting-wall room has no breakable crates at all: iron crates are only footholds. A pure platforming beat between two dense stretches.
6. Laser architecture paces the early crates: 2-stacks stand in the safe pockets between blinking gates (S1-b), and ? crates sit between floor beams (S1-c), so crate rhythm equals hazard rhythm.
7. Density curve: 16 / 16 / 6 / 11 / 14 / bonus 34 / 18 / 8 / 29. The final 29 are a 'no tricky crates' victory lap with the reacquired whirlwind, ending on a Nitro-capped TNT stack that pays the last 4 in one chain.
8. Checkpoints at 16, 32, 38, 49 and ≈ 124: the front half is checkpointed every 6–16 crates, then 75 boxes (including the bonus) pass without one. That run is made survivable by the multi-hit tool rather than by checkpoints.
9. The hidden gem uses the new power immediately: flip gravity before the first laser of the second vent and fall into a ceiling pocket (LP2 1:03:31). No crates are tied to it.

## Transfer to the skate game

1. **Bonus loop with a far-end switch and a return sweep.** Build a side zone of about 30–35 crates (≈ 20 % of the
   level). The outward line ends at a `nitrobang` plus a `bang` whose outline group forms the *return* line: floating
   crate rows over the gaps that are one-way grind bridges back toward the entrance. Put 3–4 crates on the far side of a
   central pillar, visible only on the way back, as the last count trap.
2. **Framed TNT.** A TNT in a crate row, with outline blocks around it that turn to steel after a delay. Our `bang`
   materialises instantly, so this needs a delayed-materialise variant. Then the rider must light the TNT (3–12 m/s
   bump) and clear the 2.75 m blast before the frame closes. Without engine support, drop it.
3. **TNT under a multi-hit crate as a speed trap (S5-b).** On a corridor where plowing plain crates at ≥ 13 m/s is the
   norm, a multi-hit crate on a TNT is the exception: the plow detonates the TNT and kills. The correct line is a slow
   bump (3–12 m/s) that lights the fuse, then riding away; the blast breaks the multi-hit crate (break radius ≈ 3.35 m).
4. **Crates below a ceiling path → crates below a wall-ride.** Run a wall-ride line over a hazard floor with single
   crates on the floor between hazard segments, 6–8 m apart. The rider drops off, grabs a crate, and re-enters the line
   via a kicker.
5. **Speed as the multi-hit tool.** In our game, rows of ≥ 2 equal-height crates are grind rails. A "whirlwind corridor"
   maps to crate rows along both walls that the rider grinds through, breaking them at speed. Insert TNT into such rows
