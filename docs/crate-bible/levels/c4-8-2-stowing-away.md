## Puzzle rooms and dependency chains

1. **Red timed ! run (S2-b).** Setup: GR's instruction is to stop at the red !. Hitting it makes five pink crates solid
   along the path to its far end. Correct: hit it, sprint, break all five in order. Wrong: detours, or breaking the path
   crates on the same run. Lost: 5 crates. Why: the newly acquired gravity run is tested at once against a clock.
2. **Three-high TNT stack (S2-c).** Knock out the bottom crate; the TNT drops lit; step away; the blast takes the top
   crate. Wrong: spinning the TNT. Lost: a life.
3. **TNT under a strong crate at the bonus door (S5-b).** Jump and whirlwind into the strong crate from above, then light
   the TNT from range. Wrong: a ground-level whirlwind into the stack. Lost: a life, with C4 far back. Why: it punishes
   spinning blindly with the multi-hit tool just after the tool made spinning feel safe.
4. **Bonus loop (B-a → B-f).** Out: blow the framed TNT before its frame turns to iron, bounce to the pillar, hit the !
   under the strong crate, thread the Nitro/fruit pairs, press the Nitro switch. Back: sweep to the entrance for the
   newly solid crates. Out again: the far-side column, then the exit saucer. Wrong: leaving after the Nitro switch, which
   costs 10. Lost: all-box only. Why: a one-screen room is made to be crossed three times, each crossing with new geometry.
5. **Final Nitro-capped TNT stack (S8-d).** Light the TNT and retreat; the blast takes the Nitro and the stack (HUD 148 → 152).

## Design logic of this level

1. Two powers reshape the crate grammar. The Ika-Ika gravity mask, taken at crate ≈ 17, puts crates on a floor you can no longer stand on (S4-a). The Akano whirlwind, at C4, makes long corridors of stacks safe to clear at a run.
2. TNT is the counter-design to the whirlwind: every TNT in a whirlwind stretch sits under or beside crates (S5-a, S5-b, S6-b, S7-a, S8-d), so a spin through the stack is lethal and the player has to light TNT deliberately.
3. The 34-crate bonus (22 % of the level) is a loop: out to a far-end Nitro switch, back to the entrance for crates that only now exist, and out again for a column on the far side of a pillar. Ten of its crates are behind the player at the moment the room looks finished.
4. The bonus mixes two outline behaviours: outline crates that a ! turns into breakable crates (counted), and 'invisible blocks' that turn into iron after a delay. Iron can seal a TNT inside its frame, so the room's first lesson is to act before the frame closes.
5. The shifting-wall room has no breakable crates at all: iron crates are only footholds. A pure platforming beat between two dense stretches.
6. Laser architecture paces the early crates: 2-stacks stand in the safe pockets between blinking gates (S1-b), and ? crates sit between floor beams (S1-c), so crate rhythm equals hazard rhythm.
7. Density curve: 16 / 16 / 6 / 11 / 14 / bonus 34 / 18 / 8 / 29. The final 29 are a 'no tricky crates' victory lap with the reacquired whirlwind, ending on a Nitro-capped TNT stack that pays the last 4 in one chain.
8. Checkpoints at 16, 32, 38, 49 and ≈ 124: the front half is checkpointed every 6–16 crates, then 75 boxes (including the bonus) pass without one. That run is made survivable by the multi-hit tool rather than by checkpoints.
9. The hidden gem uses the new power immediately: flip gravity before the first laser of the second vent and fall into a ceiling pocket (LP2 1:03:31). No crates are tied to it.

## Transfer to the skate game

1. **Bonus loop with a far-end switch and a return sweep.** Build a side zone of about 30–35 crates (≈ 20 % of the
   level). The outward line ends at a `nitrobang` plus a `bang` whose outline group forms the *return* line: floating
   crate rows over the gaps that are one-way grind bridges back toward the entrance. Put 3–4 crates on the far side of a
   central pillar, visible only on the way back, as the last count trap.
2. **Framed TNT.** A TNT in a crate row, with outline blocks around it that turn to steel after a delay. Our `bang`
   materialises instantly, so this needs a delayed-materialise variant. Then the rider must light the TNT (3–12 m/s
   bump) and clear the 2.75 m blast before the frame closes. Without engine support, drop it.
3. **TNT under a multi-hit crate as a speed trap (S5-b).** On a corridor where plowing plain crates at ≥ 13 m/s is the
   norm, a multi-hit crate on a TNT is the exception: the plow detonates the TNT and kills. The correct line is a slow
   bump (3–12 m/s) that lights the fuse, then riding away; the blast breaks the multi-hit crate (break radius ≈ 3.35 m).
4. **Crates below a ceiling path → crates below a wall-ride.** Run a wall-ride line over a hazard floor with single
   crates on the floor between hazard segments, 6–8 m apart. The rider drops off, grabs a crate, and re-enters the line
   via a kicker.
5. **Speed as the multi-hit tool.** In our game, rows of ≥ 2 equal-height crates are grind rails. A "whirlwind corridor"
   maps to crate rows along both walls that the rider grinds through, breaking them at speed. Insert TNT into such rows
   only where entry speed is ≥ 7 m/s (outruns the blast), and never at the end of a downhill.
6. **End-of-level chain.** Finish on a Nitro-capped TNT stack: a slow bump lights the TNT, and the blast pays the
   Nitro, so the last boxes arrive as a chain.

### Camera and route shape

A ship interior built from corridors rather than gaps. Into-screen tubes (4–5 u wide, walled) alternate with side-view machine halls; vertical transitions are lift tubes and an elevator, never jump climbs. The floor is continuous for most of the level, so the threats are across-path hazards (laser gates, floor beams, presses, pistons, electric strips) rather than pits. Bottomless gaps appear only in the engine room, the bonus and the shifting-wall room. The gravity mask adds a second walkable surface (the ceiling), which S4 turns into a two-sided hazard hall. The route never doubles back except inside the bonus, which is crossed three times.

### Geometry and enemy design logic

1. Corridors, not gaps. Into-screen tubes are 4-5 u wide and walled, and the floor is continuous through most of the level. Difficulty comes from across-path hazards (gates, beams, presses, pistons, strips), not from jump distance.
2. Hazard rhythm equals crate rhythm. 2-stacks stand in the 3-4 u pockets between laser gates (S1-b), and single ? crates sit between floor beams (S1-c), so the player's beat is jump, spin, jump.
3. The gravity mask doubles the floor plan. A ceiling becomes a second walkable surface, and S4 builds a two-sided hall: electric strips run on both floor and ceiling in stretches, with 2-3 u safe pads and mid-height blocks. Crossing a "gap" means choosing the safe surface, not jumping.
4. New powers are used immediately. The gravity mask is taken at the pink tunnel and tested at once by the red-! timed run on the ceiling, then by the hidden gem (flip into a ceiling pocket before the first laser).
5. The engine room is a gallery of timing gates with no enemies. Moving blocks under crouch-height lasers, presses with a red-hot warning, and a laser cannon in the lift shaft. Every one is lethal and every one is wait-and-go.
6. Vertical movement is tool-assisted. Lift tubes (S3, S6) and the elevator (S8) carry the player between floors; the only jump climbs are inside the bonus and the shifting-wall room.
7. Enemies arrive with their counter. No enemy appears in S1-S4. Shielded lab assistants come only after the whirlwind pickup at C4, in corridors where the multi-hit spin beats them, and TNT hidden in the stacks keeps the tool from being thoughtless.
8. The bonus concentrates the level's gaps. Small 2-3 u ledges hang over an electrified void, with a tall central pillar, a bounce block and floating Nitro/fruit pairs. The same ledges are crossed out, back and out again as outlines solidify.
9. Closing geometry is a timer: invisible-block frames turn to iron after a delay (bonus), which teaches acting before the frame closes, much like the red-! timed crates in S2.
10. The shifting-wall room is a deliberate breather. It is player-paced (blocks slide in response to the player), has no hazard and no crate, and steel crates are its only fixed footing, set between the dense bonus or whirlwind stretch and the final corridors.
11. The finale has no geometry challenge at all. A red corridor and a whirlwind corridor with stacks and a Nitro-capped TNT stack make the end a reward run.
12. Slide and crouch are used as obstacle counters (the block ride's horizontal lasers), not as distance moves. No slide-jump gap was seen.

### Geometry transfer to the skate game

1. Laser-gate pockets: in a 4-5 u (4.6-5.8 m) walled corridor, put timed `phasepad`-style gates (or a `crusher` line if no beam primitive exists) every 4-5 u with a 2-stack in each pocket. On the board at 12 m/s, a 5 m pocket lasts 0.4 s, too short, so for skate lines stretch pockets to 15-20 m and sync the gate cycle to cruise speed.
2. Floor beams to ollie: low across-path hazards (≤ 0.5 m) every 5-7 m in a tube, with a single crate between each pair. A tap ollie peaks at about 0.56 m and a full ollie at about 1.7 m, so a floor-level bar is a tap-ollie test, and a crate between bars must be passed beside or smashed at ≥ 12.5 m/s.
3. Two-surface hall without gravity flip: give a side-scroll zone two lines, a lower floor and an upper ledge or grind rail 3-4 m above it, and alternate a hazard strip between them every 6-8 m. The rider must switch lines with ollies (up, a 1.7 m apex onto a ledge lip or rail) and drops, and the crates sit on the short safe pads of the lower line.
4. Heated presses: a row of `crusher`s with a visible glow about 0.5 s before each slam and a crate on the floor beneath. On foot at 9 m/s a 3 m press is crossed in about 0.35 s; on the board at 12 m/s in about 0.25 s. Make the up-window at least 1 s and phase the presses so a cruising rider passes them all in one window.
5. Breather room: after a dense crate or enemy stretch, add a crate-free, hazard-free climbing room of `mover` blocks and steel footholds. It lets intensity drop before the last stretch without breaking the flow.
6. Enemy plus counter tool together: put the first group of `spinner` or `charger` enemies right after the board pickup, on a straight where plowing at ≥ 13 m/s is the intended answer. Seed one TNT under a multi-hit crate among them, so the plow line needs a slow, deliberate pass there.
