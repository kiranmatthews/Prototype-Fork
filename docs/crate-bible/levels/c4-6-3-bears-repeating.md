# Crash 4 — 6-3 Bears Repeating

Sources: GR 6-3 (Gameranx "All Crates & Hidden Gem" guide, id 210744); LP1 3:31:08–3:36:40 (archive.org PS4
completionist longplay, **Part 1**), read every 2 s at 854×480 and every 1 s for the bonus and the bear ride, plus HUD
crops and the exit crate sign; gamepressure.com for enemy and hazard corroboration only (its "137 boxes" is wrong).

**Locating note.** The brief expected this level in Part 2 after Stay Frosty; it is in Part 1. The HUD reads 1/227 at
LP1 3:31:18, the polar bear is mounted at 3:35:07, and "Level complete" shows at 3:36:35. It was a no-death
N.Sanely Perfect run. The flashback level that follows (3:37) is not part of this map.

Evidence level: the total (227), the bonus (27), the 163 transfer, **205** just after the bear checkpoint, and the exit
sign's **214** were read directly. Between those, GR's tallies are used: 46, 62, 70, 80, 82, 97, 133, 136 and 204.
GR's 204 is the count *before* the bear checkpoint, and the HUD's 205 includes it. GR labels 62→70 a "yellow gem bonus
room". In the LP those are ordinary main-route crates, and the run never had the yellow gem yet earned ALL. The bear
ride was read at 1 fps: 25 of its 42 first-half boxes are located individually, and 17 are an unresolved balance. The
36 in the giant Nitro block come from GR's tally jump (97 → 133); the HUD never showed there.

## Puzzle rooms and dependency chains

1. **TNT as a tool at the start (S1-c).** Stomp the TNT and step back, and its blast takes the two floating crates.
   Wrong: lingering, or jumping for the pair first. Lost: a life, or the pair. Why: the first explosive of the level
   helps the player before any explosive threatens them.
2. **Three TNT stacks (S2-b, S2-c, S4-e).** TNT on top of wood: spin the base and step back. Strong crate on top of TNT
   on a cable car: light the TNT and leave, and the blast opens the strong crate. TNT under a pair: take the pair from
   above first. Wrong: slamming the strong crate with the TNT beneath you. Lost: a life on a small, moving footing.
3. **Slow-mo explosives (S5-d, S5-e).** Break the crate at the end of the Nitro row in slow-mo and get clear. For the
   giant block, set it off in slow-mo, jump past, and cancel slow-mo as it goes. Wrong: walking past (the run needs
   the detonations for 44 boxes of credit). Why: the new power is introduced by making the deadliest object the biggest reward.
4. **Bonus closing lid (B-a → B-f).** Hit the floating timed ! with slow-mo active, detonate the Nitro bridge, and
   sweep the dock row before the outline lid turns to iron. Finish with the floating pair (Nitro under slow-mo). Wrong:
   no slow-mo at the switch, or crossing the bridge. Lost: the dock crates that are still sealed when the timer ends.
5. **All-or-nothing bear run (S7).** 41 crates before the checkpoint, no counter on screen. Memorise lanes, jump the
   lip stacks, hug the left wall in the cavern. Wrong: over-steering while the camera swings, or passing the checkpoint
   after a miss. Lost: the box gem, unless the player dies on purpose and retries before the checkpoint (GR).
6. **Exit sign and Nitro switch (S9-a).** The sign shows 214/227, and the green ! pays the 13 Nitro dodged on the ride.

## Design logic of this level

1. Four acts: on-foot snowfield and caverns (80 crates in about 2 min), a slow-mo explosives set piece (53, of which 44 are Nitro), a 27-crate bonus, and the bear ride (51 crates plus 13 Nitro credited at the exit).
2. Hazards are box credit: about 64 of the 227 boxes are Nitro (row 8, block 36, bonus 7, exit switch 13), plus at least 7 TNT. One slow-mo detonation of the giant block is worth 36 crates, 16 % of the level.
3. Cable-car platforms carry tall columns and blocks (S1-j, S1-k): many crates per landing, on a footing that is moving, so the player commits to a stomp chain.
4. TNT stacking is varied rather than repeated: TNT next to floating crates (tool), TNT on top of wood, a strong crate on top of TNT, TNT under a crate pair. Four variants in three minutes.
5. The bear ride's first half holds 41 crates and the HUD never shows; it is designed for memorisation (GR: die and retry before the checkpoint). Crates sit in left/centre/right lanes, 2-stacks are centred on lips where a JUMP prompt appears, and two crates hide behind ice pillars against the left wall.
6. The second half is light (9 crates) and seeded with Nitro: the ride turns from collecting to dodging, and the dodged Nitro are paid by the exit switch, so dodging never costs the box gem.
7. A full-width wall of 6 iron arrow crates on a rise is terrain, not a collectible: a forced launch gate on a vehicle section.
8. Rope-bridge and bumper stretches use one crate per post (S3), spacing pickups to the rhythm of the posts rather than clustering them.
9. The yellow-gem plate shows a hint banner without the key; the run had no yellow gem yet earned ALL, so that path carries no counted crates.
10. Checkpoints sit at 46, ≈ 71, 134 and 205: the front-loaded C1 follows the densest on-foot stretch, and the bear checkpoint locks in the hardest all-box segment.
11. The hidden gem is marked by a red lantern beside the arrow crate: a light as a visual tell for a drop.

## Transfer to the skate game

1. **Lane grammar for a skate speed-line (S7).** In a 6–8 m wide downhill corridor at 15–23 m/s, put single crates
   in left/centre/right lanes every 25–35 m (about 1.5 s of travel), so each beat is one steering decision. Use a
   "one each side" beat (S7-c) as the all-box test: two lanes, one pass. Above 13 m/s the rider plows plain crates and
   loses about 20 % speed each time, so follow dense beats with a downhill to rebuild speed.
2. **Stacks on kicker lips (S7-d, S8-c).** A 2-stack is 2.3 m, above the 1.7 m full-charge ollie. Plowing the base at
   speed makes the top crate settle behind the rider. Put 2-stacks on the lip of a kicker, so the takeoff carries the
   rider into the upper crate. Spend 3-stacks only where the rider is airborne at that height (vert/kicker exit).
3. **Crate–Nitro weave (S8-b).** Lay a diagonal W–N–N–W line across the lane, with lane changes at least 15 m apart at
   15 m/s. Nitro kills on any touch (mask excepted), so this is a steering test, not a speed test. Pay the dodged Nitro
   with a `nitrobang` at the exit (13 of 227 here), so dodging never costs the box gem.
4. **Arrow-wall launch gate (S7-h).** A full-width row of 6 unbreakable arrow crates on a rise works as terrain: a
   forced launch over a gap, with a floating crate line on the arc. In our rules steel at speed is a wall crash, so
   probe how a steel arrow behaves under the board first. If it crashes the rider, use a kicker with the same arc.
5. **Mid-ride checkpoint after the dense half.** Put the checkpoint right after the hardest crate run (41 here) and
   make the second half light and Nitro-seeded. All-box players then retry only the hard half.
6. **Giant Nitro block as remote credit (S5-e).** We have no slow-mo. The equivalent is a large Nitro block 2–3 m from
   the end of a TNT crate row. Grinding the row at ≥ 7 m/s outruns each blast, and the last one chains into the block:
   dozens of boxes for one committed grind, with no Nitro ever touched.

### Camera and route shape

Four acts in four camera grammars. On foot, the route runs into-screen through a canyon, an ice cave and a chasm, then turns round for a toward-camera rope-bridge run back past Cortex's drill tower (S3). The slow-mo cavern is side view, with moving footing hung from the ceiling. The bonus is a side-view harbour. The bear ride is a chase-camera downhill run in three lanes. The on-foot half has many small gaps over dark water or chasms; the bear half has almost no gaps, only lane hazards and a few prompted ravine jumps. The route never doubles back except the toward-camera bridge, which re-crosses the area the player just saw from the tower.

### Geometry and enemy design logic

1. Four camera grammars carry four platform grammars. On foot, into-screen: small gaps over water and chasms. Slow-mo cavern, side view: moving footing hung from the ceiling. Bonus, side view: a timed slot. Bear, chase camera: lanes, with almost no gaps.
2. Hanging footing is the on-foot theme, in strict escalation. Static rafts (P-S1-c), then hung pads and decks (S1-S2), then lift discs that carry you vertically (S3), then crumbling slabs and tipping pads under slow-mo (S5), then the bear, where the footing is forced.
3. Decks carry crates; pads carry none. The 1.5-2 u pads are pure stepping stones; every crate group sits on a 3x3 u or larger deck (columns, 3x2 block, 2x2 ? block). Collecting never happens on the smallest footing.
4. The drone owner is a protected-projectile archetype, taught in four steps. Open floor (E-S1-a), then across a raft gap (E-S1-b), then hovering inside the gaps between pads (E-S3-a), then hurdles on a narrow toward-camera bridge (E-S3-c). The counter (hit the owner) never changes; the geometry around it does.
5. Camera reversal is used as difficulty. The rope bridge is run toward the camera on a 2.5 u deck with drone pairs, so depth (gap and drone distance) is read late. That is why the bridge has railed rope sides and posts every 6-8 u as rests.
6. The mask arrives exactly at the crumble run. Slow-mo is first used on rope-strung ice slabs (3:33:37), the archetypal timing gap, and only later on the Nitro set pieces. The tool is taught on footing before it is used on explosives.
7. Explosive set pieces are built as gaps. The giant Nitro block sits beside 2-3 u steel blocks with ~2 u gaps (P-S5-g), so the explosion is a moving hazard you traverse beside, not a wall in front of you.
8. Rest spacing on foot: a railed scaffold rest or wide deck every 3-4 jumps in the cavern (P-S5-b), and checkpoints on solid ground above or after each hanging stretch (C1 above the block deck, C2 on a snow path, C3 after the steel blocks).
9. The bonus is a geometry timer. The crate row fills a slot exactly one crate high under an outline row that turns to iron. The sweep is run along the crate tops inside that slot, and the closed lid becomes the bridge to the exit.
10. Bear-lane grammar: the valley is 10-14 u wide, with corridors and tunnels of ~5-6 u. Hazards (trees, rocks, pillars, a hole, skiers) take one lane at a time and alternate sides, and the crates sit in the free lane, often right beside the hazard.
11. Bear jumps are few and announced. Two ravines and a ridge (with a JUMP prompt and a 2-stack on the lip), plus one forced launch off a full-width wall of iron arrow crates. Everything else is steering.
12. The second half swaps enemies for mines. Nitro pairs, a diagonal weave and fences replace skiers, and the dodged Nitro are paid at the exit, so the geometry tests steering without costing the box gem.
13. Height on foot is double-jump scale. Scaffold risers are ~2 u with fruit columns marking the jumps (P-S2-f, P-S4-d); the hand-over-hand pipe frame (P-S2-d) and the lift discs are the only non-jump traversals.

### Geometry transfer to the skate game

1. Protected projectile: pair a `sentry` (or two `floater`s) with a `grunt` owner and switch them off when the owner dies. Teach it on a 10-12 u (11-14 m) open floor first, then put the same pair across a 2-3 u water `pit`, then hover `floater`s in the 2.3-3.5 m gaps between `mover` pads.
2. Lift discs map to vertical `mover` platforms about 2 u (2.3 m) across. Use them to move between a lower walkway and a raised grind line, since a skater cannot climb 4-6 m any other way. A slow spin is cosmetic; keep the rider's facing stable.
3. Crumble run without slow-mo: four `crumble` slabs about 2x1 u (2.3x1.2 m), 2-3 u (2.3-3.5 m) apart, which a 9 m/s runner crosses at about one slab per 0.35 s, with a ≥ 0.5 s collapse delay and a railed scaffold `platform` rest after them. Follow it with `spinbridge` tipping pads at the same spacing.
4. Bear lanes as a skate downhill: a 12-16 m wide `terrain` slope with three lanes. Put one lane hazard (a tree prop, a `stone`, or a 1.7 m `pit` hole) every 25-35 m, alternating lanes, with a crate in the free lane beside it. Use a full-width `trampoline` row on a rise as the forced launch, and prompt only two ravine ollies.
5. Giant Nitro block as a traversal hazard: place a 7x3x3 Nitro block beside a line of 2-3 u `platform` blocks with ~2 u gaps, and start the chain from a TNT at the near end (3 s fuse). The blast front (≈ 7.9 m/s) is slower than a 9 m/s runner, so a player who lights it and keeps hopping outruns it.
6. One-crate slot under a ceiling: a crate row on a pier under a solid ceiling one crate (1.15 m) above it. The board cannot ollie over the row (1.7 m apex hits the ceiling), so the only clean line is grinding the row, which breaks it (≥ 6.5 m/s if TNT is mixed in).
