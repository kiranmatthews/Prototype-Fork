## Puzzle rooms and dependency chains
**P1 — Ramp flight lines (S2-e, S3-e).** Setup: a ramp with two or three crates strung along its flight path, the
last one higher. Correct: full speed off the ramp and a flip/roll (or back-flip) so the hitbox sweeps every crate.
Tempting: a plain straight jump. Loss: the high crate (1 box) → all-box run fails unless you loop round (the water
is open enough to retry, unlike Orient Express). Why: introduces the jet ski's trick button as a collection tool.

**P2 — Crate hidden behind a ramp lip (S2-f).** Setup: a wood crate tucked against the far side of a ramp. Correct:
pass the ramp low or round it. Wrong: launch off it. Loss: 1 box. Why: ramps are attractive; this one is a trap that
teaches "look past the ramp" — the same lesson as the hidden sailors and mines.

**P3 — Cannon lanes (S1-f, S2-a).** Setup: crates inside the ship's broadside lanes. Correct: wait for a three-shot
volley to pass, then go. Wrong: full throttle into the volley. Loss: a life, not a box.

**P4 — Bait between drifting mines (S2-g).** Setup: a 3-s crate between two wandering mines. Correct: commit to one
side early and clip the crate. Wrong: hesitate. Loss: a life.

## Design logic of this level
1. **Open water, linear count.** The sea looks free but the crates, the wumpa lines (P3 s72: follow the fruit line to
   the first jump) and the screen-top arrow lay a single loop; 37 boxes in 14 / 13 / 10.
2. **Ramps deliver crates.** At least six of the ~11 ramps put crates on their flight path or right behind them: single at the
   landing (S1-d, S2-b), pair at the landing between mines (S3-a), in-line pair needing a flip (S2-e), rising triple
   needing a flip (S3-e), and one crate hidden behind a lip (S2-f).
3. **Danger shares the crate's lane.** Crates sit in cannon lanes (S1-f, S2-a), among mines (S1-e, S3-c, S3-d) and
   between drifting mines (S2-g). Crates are never inside the double mine row by the crystal — that row is pure
   steering.
4. **Gentle all-box goal.** No stacks of explosives, no switches, no lives; the F3 intro calls the placement kind.
   The only count risks are the fold-side singles on corners, the hidden S2-f crate and the high third crate of
   S3-e.
5. **The goal is in view the whole time.** The skull-rock island is visible from the start (P3 s73, TT frames
   02–07) and the route circles it before entering its cavern.
6. **Time-trial overlay:** 31 s of time crates; they reward route craft (fr.02: skip the first ramp and cut inside;
   fr.03–08: some buoys can be driven over, others bounce you), wave control (fr.20) and tricks (fr.22, fr.27).
   TA1 even advises skipping the off-line 1-s crate at the S-bend.

## Transfer to the skate game
1. **Kicker + floating line (S3-e).** After a kicker, float 2–3 crates on the flight arc, rising (e.g. lip height
   +0.6 m, +1.2 m, +1.6 m, 2–3 m apart along the line). The last crate should sit at the top of a full-charge launch
   so only a charged approach collects all three, mirroring "the third box needs a flip".
2. **In-line pair on the arc (S2-e).** Two crates 2–3 m apart on the landing path of a kicker: one is free, the
   second is the skill check. Keep them single (no stacks) so the board plows them at ≥12.5 m/s.
3. **Crate in the kicker's shadow (S2-f).** Put a crate tight against the far side of a kicker: launching skips it,
   rolling round or a soft hop collects it. Use once per level as a "look past the ramp" lesson, never on a forced line.
4. **Hazard-lane pairing (S1-f, S2-g).** Put a crate inside a timed hazard lane, or a single crate between two TNT
   crates 3–4 m apart so the rider must choose a side early; TNT at ≥12.5 m/s detonates on contact, so the choice is
   real, matching MW's drifting mines.
5. **Breadcrumbs in open areas.** In plazas or bowls use single crates and fruit lines to point at the next kicker,
   placing a crate right at each kicker's landing as confirmation that the line is correct.

### Camera and route shape
- **Vehicle ride on semi-open water**: Coco on a jet ski, chase camera behind, with a guide arrow at the top of the screen pointing to the exit (P3 p010R). The skull-rock goal island is visible from the start (P3 s73; TT 01).
- **A clockwise loop**: start channel east → narrow far-left channel north → pirate-ship basin → top channel east → right channel south → bottom channel → bottom-left corner → mine channel north → middle channel north-east into the skull-rock cavern. Each corner is a turn of roughly 90°.
- There is no lethal gap anywhere: water is the floor. Ramps launch over water, sand bars and islets bounce the ski, and the swell lifts and lowers ramps and mines (F3 p041L tip 3).
- Open water allows turning back for a missed crate, unlike Orient Express’s forced ride (first-wave reading); the time trial instead rewards the shortest line round buoys and islets.

### Geometry and enemy design logic
1. **No lethal gaps.** Water is the floor everywhere; the danger is on the surface (mines, cannon fire, boats) and in reading the swell. Ramps are optional launches that deliver crates.
2. **Ramps escalate as crate delivery:** an optional ramp → twin ramps with a crate behind one → a crate at the landing → a landing among mines → a boat behind the ramp → two crates in line (flip or roll) → a crate under the lip (do not launch) → a crate on a post (back-flip on a low wave) → a rising triple (back-flip).
3. **Mines draw the lanes.** Edge singles narrow a channel, three mines ring a crate, staggered double rows make a slalom, pairs at both edges make a gate with a crate in it, a drifting pair adds timing, and a dense mine channel forces a route choice (hug the islet, or ram through with the mask).
4. **Timing hazards are readable from afar:** the broadside fires three-shot volleys into marked lanes, and the swell raises and lowers ramps and mines (F3 tip 3: read the waves from far away).
5. **Few enemies, each at a decision point:** a boat at the tight turn round the ship, a boat right behind a ramp, a seagull perched on a crate, a boat after the final ramp.
6. **Waiting replaces attacking.** The jet ski can hardly attack (F3 tip 2): slow down and let monsters move; a boat can be beaten only from above via a ramp, so the geometry is the only weapon and beating it is rewarded.
7. **Generous recovery.** Open water allows looping back for a missed crate, and F3 calls the crate layout kind; the hard parts are optional (time crates, the high crates on the trick ramps).
8. **The goal is always in view.** The route circles the skull-rock island clockwise and enters it last, so the guide arrow and the island orient the player in open water.
9. **Corners carry crates and hazards together.** The tight turn round the ship combines four crates, a boat and the fire lane; other corners have a ramp and a crate (P3 s76: easy does it round corners).
10. **Buoys and islets are soft walls.** Some buoys can be ridden over and some bounce the ski; islets bounce it. They code the fastest line without killing.
11. **Teaching order:** crates on open water → an optional ramp → a ramp with a crate → crates among mines → a fire lane → boats → the seagull → a mine slalom → a crate hidden by a ramp → drifting mines → a crate on a post → a dense mine channel → the rising triple.

### Geometry transfer to the skate game
1. **Kicker field without pits.** In a plaza or bowl with no lethal gaps, let `ramp` kickers deliver floating crates: one at the landing about 1.2 m up; an in-line pair at 1.2 m and 1.7 m (a full ollie peaks at 1.78 m); a rising triple at lip height +0.6, +1.2 and +1.7 m, the last only on a charged launch. Leave room to pass beside every kicker.
2. **Mines as lane geometry.** Use TNT or `minefields` spots as edge singles, a pair at both edges with a crate between them (a ≈3.5 m gate), and a staggered double row of three as a slalom. TNT touched at ≥ 13 m/s on the board kills, so the slalom is a real speed test.
3. **Fire lane with a crate in it.** A `sentry` firing across a lane (one orb per ≈ 2.7 s, range 32 m, only with a clear line of fire) with a crate inside the lane: the rider waits for a shot to pass or charges through between shots. Put the lane at a corner, as the ship’s broadside is.
4. **A kicker that must not be used.** Tuck a crate tight against the far side of a kicker so that launching skips it, and place an enemy (a `grunt`) 3–5 m past another kicker’s landing, visible from the approach — each once per level.
5. **Perched guard over a crate.** A `floater` hovering over a crate (it swoops every 2.6 s when you are within 12 m along and 6 m across): take the crate as it swoops away, which is the seagull’s timing window.
