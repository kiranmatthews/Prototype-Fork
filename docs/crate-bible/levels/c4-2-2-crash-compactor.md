Evidence level: section counts are exact. They are GR tallies, checked against the HUD: 98 after the bonus = GR 98.

the bonus were seen clearly. The opening's background crates, the red-gem path's TNT share and the post-bonus
"bridge of 6" were not resolved crate by crate, so those sections contain residual clusters (low confidence). The 15

estimated from frames.

## Puzzle rooms and dependency chains
- **P1 TNT on `?` (S1-h).** Setup: a TNT sits on a `?` on a narrow ledge. Correct: spin the `?` out, let the TNT
  drop, stomp it, retreat. Wrong: spinning the TNT first is an instant blast (a life); jumping on the stack lights
  the TNT above the `?`, and the blast still counts both, but you are standing on it. Design: support order taught
  with an explosive on top.
- **P2 Red-gem path (G).** A cross-level gate: the platform needs the red gem from another level, so the all-box
  gem cannot be earned on a first visit. Inside, every main-route idea returns with explosives added
  (moving discs carrying TNT/N, a W/N ring, a W W N stepping row, an iron-arrow column flanked by Nitros).
- **P3 The gem switch under the checkpoint (S6-e, S7).** A `!` hidden in the alcove below C3's walkway opens a
  door one screen ahead. Correct: drop down, hit it, slide-jump back. Lost if skipped: the hidden gem, not crates.
  Design: the checkpoint is the landmark for the secret.
- **P4 There-and-back bonus (B-b, B-d, B-e).** The crates are the footing for the return trip. Correct: go
  to the far end using the float-line and the pyramid's top, then eat the pyramid and the stepping crates on the way
  back. Wrong: eating the stepping crates outbound strands you, and you fall (no life lost in a bonus,
  but no crates either).
- **P5 Column with an iron-arrow base (P-a).** Correct: spin the W, walk away from the dropped and lit TNT,
  then use the iron arrow for the high crate. Wrong: standing by the column when the TNT lands. Lost: a life.
- **P6 Phase-hidden crate (Q-e).** Requires leaving the checkpoint, dropping, and using phase
  toggles and bounce crates to reach a crate inside a waterfall column.

## Design logic of this level
1. **Timing is the theme of the crates.** The opening puts crates inside periodic hazards (flame-jet table,
   turrets, hatches, fan). The player learns to wait before learning to rush.
2. **Neon arrows are the level's hint language.** One points at the foreground crate (S4), others cue the rail
   inputs (S6), one marks the gem window and one points into the phase waterfall (Q-e). Hidden crates are hidden
   from the camera, never from the level's own signage.
3. **The rail is a transition ride, not a crate test.** Only 4 crates, against 26 on 2-1's rails. Its job is
   speed and neon reading; its crates sit exactly at the cue points.
4. **A checkpoint guards a secret.** The `!` for the gem door is under C3's walkway.
5. **The red-gem path is 18 % of the level's crates, gated by another level.** It remixes the main route's
   tools with explosives everywhere and holds about half of the level's Nitros.
6. **Nitros as walk-past bait.** About 8 Nitros sit in an open sand basin around one fruit crate. None
   need touching; they all count when the detonator at the exit fires.
7. **The bonus is a there-and-back.** Crates are the floor of the return trip. The pyramid is bounced on
   outbound and eaten inbound.
8. **Support order with explosives** is the recurring puzzle: TNT on `?` (S1-h), TNT in a floor slot (G-d),
   TNT dropping onto an iron arrow (P-a).
9. **The mask comes late.** It is given at C4 for a short vertical finale. The last real crate (Q-e) uses
   it to hide in plain sight.
10. **Checkpoint rhythm** is about one every 60–90 s of play: C1 after the opening gauntlet, C2 at the branch,
    C3 after the rail, C4 at the mask, C5 halfway up the climb.

## Transfer to the skate game
1. **Timed crate on a swept deck (S1-b).** A wood crate in the middle of a 6–8 m deck crossed by a periodic hazard
   (flame sweep every ~2 s). On foot: wait for the gap. On the board at ≥ 12.5 m/s: plow it in passing (8 % speed tax)
   if you hit the window. One object teaches two strategies.
2. **Hint signage (S4, R-b, Q-e).** Use neon-arrow decor pointing at crates on ledges toward the camera or below the
   deck edge. On rails, hang coloured signs 3–5 m before an ollie point (up = ollie, down = stay low) with the crate
   at the cue.
3. **TNT on a base crate (S1-h).** A 2-stack [TNT on `?`] on a ledge 2 crates wide. On foot: spin the base out, the
   TNT drops, stomp it, retreat. Keep it off the skate line: a board hit at 1.5–12.5 m/s lights it instantly.
4. **Rotating turntable with explosives at the rim (G-b).** A 4 m disc rotating every ~3 s with a TNT and a wood crate
   at opposite rim points: the rotation decides where the TNT points when its fuse ends.
5. **Grid ring of alternating wood and Nitro (G-e).** A square loop of 1-crate-tall crate rows around a hole, with wood
   and Nitro alternating at the corners. Under our rules each straight row is a grind ledge, and a Nitro in a row
   detonates as it is ridden, so grinding the ring is lethal. The player must ride it on foot or hop the corners:
   a readable "do not grind this" pattern.
6. **There-and-back stepping crates (B-d, B-e).** In a bonus or optional room, a floating inverted pyramid
   (rows 5/4/3/2 ≈ 5.8 m wide on top) and a float-line of 3 single crates 4–5 m apart as the only footing back.
   Grinding or plowing them outbound strands the player.
7. **Column with a spring base (P-a).** Steel arrow (unbreakable, 16 m/s ≈ 3.9 m), wood, TNT, steel cap, with a wood
   crate 3.5–3.8 m above the arrow's lid. On foot: spin the wood, the TNT drops onto the arrow, retreat, then bounce.
8. **Nitro field as count bait (N-b).** An open basin with 6–8 Nitros scattered around one wood crate,
   all counted by a green `!` at the exit. Teach the detonator's value by making the field large and harmless to walk
   around.
