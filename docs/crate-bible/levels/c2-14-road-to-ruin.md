## Puzzle rooms and dependency chains
**P1 — Alternate entrance (cross-level).** Setup: outline crates visible from the first seconds of the level. Correct sequence: finish Diggin' It, body-slam the lone plant on a separate platform before its goal (F2 p091R, D2 shot 55), take the secret warp room's stage-14 door, clear the hidden section, hit its ! (H-c), then play the normal course. Wrong sequence: entering from the normal warp room — the outlines and the whole hidden section are unreachable, so the box gem is impossible on the first visit. Loss: all-box only. Why it works: the empty outlines are a promise made two worlds before it can be kept.

**P2 — The crate mountain with a buried TNT (H-a).** Correct: take crates off one by one from the outside until the TNT is exposed, light it, step clear (blast-broken crates still count). Wrong: hopping on top and spinning/slamming — the hidden TNT goes off under you (F2: body slam forbidden). Loss: a life (and on a no-death run, the death route). Why: the most generous-looking pile in the level is the only one that can kill you; greed versus care.

**P3 — Rat-bounce TNT that pays out the mask (S1-d).** Correct: bounce off the rat onto the TNT stack, retreat, let the blast break the adjacent Aku crate and kill the lizard, collect the mask. Wrong: spinning the Aku crate next to the TNT or standing on the stack. Loss: a life. Repeated without the mask as a test in S4-b.

**P4 — POINT 5 switch room (S3-a).** Hit the steel ! first; outlines become crates. Skipping it loses ~4 crates (all-box only).

**P5 — Remote Nitro credit (H-b + S4-c).** The Nitro must not be touched in H; their credit arrives from the green ! at the end of S4. Forgetting the green ! after a perfect hidden-section run loses the gem at the very end.

**P6 — Bonus switch relay (B-a).** Four switches in forced order across crumbling floors; the materialised ? becomes a step (B-b).

## Design logic of this level
1. **The level is built around a promise.** Empty outline crates near the start (HO) and a box gem that is impossible on the first visit turn the stage into a cross-level puzzle: the solution is in a different world (Diggin' It).
2. **Off-course share is extreme**: 58 of 88 boxes (F2) sit off the normal course. The normal course is light (≈30–33 crates) and paced as a timing level; the crate density lives in the hidden section and the bonus.
3. **Crates sit on the safe platforms, not the crumbling ones.** Every cluster in S2–S4 is on a solid platform reached after a crumbling run or a fire lane, so crates act as rest-stop rewards in a level whose hazard is "don't stop".
4. **Timed placement against the fire statues**: S2-a and S4-a are inside fire lanes; the player must spend part of a fire cycle collecting.
5. **Enemy-as-tool grammar**: rats are springboards (onto the walled platform, onto the TNT); the lizard is killed by the TNT the rat lets you light.
6. **Teach → test with the TNT stacks**: S1-d (with a mask reward in the blast) and S4-b (same layout, no reward) bracket the level.
7. **One switch room per section** (POINT 5) uses outlines to make a half-empty room into a reward.
8. **The most generous crate pile is the most dangerous** (buried TNT): a count trap that punishes the spin-spam habit the rest of the game encourages.
9. **Remote explosives**: the four Nitro in the hidden section are credited by the green ! at the opposite end of the level, so the all-box run is only "closed" at the goal.
10. **The death route has no crates**: the no-death challenge and the all-box challenge are separated cleanly; a perfect run never needs to survive the gorilla route.
11. **Checkpoints are evenly spaced (3) and every C crate is on the normal course**; C2 is paired with the level crystal.

## Transfer to the skate game
1. **Outline promise**: place 3–5 outline crates (`bang` group) visible from the spawn on a ledge the rider cannot reach yet; their `!` sits at the end of an optional side loop that returns to the spawn (≈60–120 m loop). The player sees the reward before knowing the route.
2. **Buried-TNT crate block**: a 4×4×2 crate block (≈4.6 m wide) with one TNT in the core. On the board its top layer forms grindable rows; grinding at ≥ 7 m/s across the top breaks the rows safely until the TNT row is reached, which then detonates — teach "peel the outside first" with a 2–3 crate warning gap.
3. **Rest-stop crates after "don't stop" runs**: after crumbling or moving sections, put 2–3 crates on the first solid platform only, never on the hazard itself; skating speed makes a single pickup line natural.
4. **Fire-lane crates**: put a 2-stack inside a timed hazard lane (statue cycle ≈ 2–3 s) so collecting at cruise (12 m/s) costs one cycle.
5. **Enemy springboard to a TNT stack**: a bounce enemy 2–3 m from a 2-stack TNT whose blast (2.75 m) reaches an adjacent mask crate — the explosion pays out the mask; repeat the layout later without the mask.
6. **Remote Nitro credit**: a Nitro row early in an optional loop, with the green `nitrobang` placed near the finish, so the all-box run closes at the end.

### Camera and route shape
- **Into-screen 3D scroll** (F2 p055R 3Dスクロール) along stone walkways on tall stilts and colonnades; the isometric map zig-zags left and right and is split by three continuation jumps (A, B, C). The crate-wall corridor, the 'ape-faced' room and the POINT 5 switch room are short indoor beats.
- The bonus is a side-view room of platforms with switch crates floating overhead (D2 s21–s23). The death route is entered on a skull elevator that carries Crash 'into the far distance' and returns him a little further down the main path (D2 s28, s37). The hidden section (alternate entrance from Diggin' It) is a separate night-sky platform run that ends at the normal start (D2 s56–s58). The HO outline crates add no geometry.
- Shape: walled crate platforms (≈ 3×3 u) alternate with narrow walkways and crumbling runs. Hazard density rises from crumble only (S1 start) to crumble + single statues (S2) to crumble + rats (S3) to twin statues and a monkey on crumbling tiles (S4).

### Geometry and enemy design logic
1. **Footing is the subject.** Patterned tiles hold, plain tiles crumble, some pads wobble; F2's Aku tip tells the player to memorise which types crumble. The texture is the instruction, so every tile can be read before it is stepped on.
2. **Decide before you step.** POINT 4: once on a crumbling tile it is too late to dodge the statue; board it only as the fire dies. So a solid perch always comes right before a crumble + fire beat (D2 s12: 'safe spot before the collapsing platform').
3. **Fire escalates by coupling, not by speed**: a statue beside a walkway with crates in its lane (POINT 3) → a fire lane across a gap → fire over a crumbling tile (POINT 4) → fire across a crumbling run that is jumped mid-run (D2 s13) → twin statues (POINT 8).
4. **Gaps are short and lethal**: ≈ 1.5-3 u, all plain jumps over the drop below the stilts. Difficulty comes from timing and footing; the only long jump (a dash-jump) is in the optional hidden section.
5. **Rest platforms are the crate platforms**: walled or balustraded ≈ 3×3 u squares after every crumble run. Geometry and crates say 'stop here' together, and the walls remove the fall risk while the player breaks crates.
6. **One layout, escalated by one tile type**: S1's five solid hex stepping stones return in S3 as three pillar tops with a crumbling middle (POINT 6).
7. **Enemies are taught on safe ground, then moved onto bad footing**: rats alone on the cross walkway → as springboards → on a crumbling ledge where you cannot wait (S3); the monkey on a long solid walkway (POINT 9) → on crumbling tiles (POINT 10).
8. **Three enemies, three counters**: rats are tools (bounce steps onto platforms and onto TNT), the lizard is a verb gate (slide only, with an over-slide fall risk on 2 u walkways), the monkey is a timing gate (meet it at its landing point).
9. **The slide is taught where it is safe**: the first lizard that must actually be slid sits in the walled ape-faced room; the open-walkway lizards come after, and the very first one can be blown up instead.
10. **Narrow walkways make every counter a precision test**: ≈ 2 u wide with open edges, so a slide, a missed spin or a retreat from a TNT can end in a fall.
11. **The death route swaps static timing for moving geometry** (logs rolling over stepping stones, revolving pads, leaning pillars) and carries no crates, so the no-death challenge never competes with the all-box run.
12. **The bonus is a footing puzzle**: a switch relay across crumbling tiles, then a crate used as a springboard onto collapsing tiles; the payout sits on two stable ledges.

### Geometry transfer to the skate game
1. **Coded crumble tiles.** Use `crumble` tiles in a distinct plain material between patterned solid `platform` tiles, and never put a crate on a crumble tile. On foot (9 m/s) a 5-tile run of 2.3 m tiles (≈ 11.5 m) takes ≈ 1.3 s; on the board at cruise it takes < 1 s, so crumble runs only bite on foot or when coupled to a timed hazard.
2. **Perch → fire → crumble.** A solid perch tile, then a `firetiki` jet crossing a 4-5-tile `crumble` run. Make the jet's off window longer than the on-foot crossing time (≥ 1.5 s for ≈ 11.5 m), and ask for a jump over the jet's line mid-run only after the plain version has been seen.
3. **Twin-jet corridor last.** Two `firetiki` on opposite sides of a 2.3 m walkway with phases that leave one shared off window; place it after the single-jet lessons and reward it with 2-3 crates on a walled 3.5 m square.
4. **Hopper on a walkway, then on crumble.** A `hopper` with a fixed landing pattern on a ≥ 12 m solid walkway (meet it at its landing point), later the same enemy on `crumble` tiles; keep the crumble delay longer than one hop so waiting is possible but costly.
5. **Verb-gate enemy on a narrow walkway.** A `spiker` (no stomp) or `turtle` on a 2.3 m walkway with open edges, first inside a walled room (safe lesson), then on open walkways — the lizard's 'slide, but don't over-slide'.
6. **Rolling hazard on stepping stones.** A `stone` launched along a line of 2.3 m pillar pads with 2.3 m gaps toward the player, thrown by a `sentry` at the far end; the player times each hop so the stone passes underneath.
