## Puzzle rooms and dependency chains
1. **Porcupine → ! → steel steps (S2-d).** Setup: a crate blocks Nick; the ! is behind him. Sequence: break the box, hug the left wall, slide-kick Nick into the back so he hits the !. Wrong: spinning him early or getting quilled. Loss: the steel steps (D2 19) that lead up toward the skull platform and C2. Design: enemies are tools, and the switch rewards using the enemy, not just avoiding it.
2. **Four hidden ! behind two ice pillars → outline row (S4-a).** Setup: two big ice pillars; two steel ! blocks hide behind each. Correct: walk behind both pillars and hit all four. Wrong: run past, since the frames ahead stay empty and the route still clears. Loss: all-box only (≈3 boxes). Design: the camera itself is the puzzle. Aku's on-screen tip exists because the count trap is otherwise unfair.
3. **Nitro-shielded 1UP (S4-e).** Correct: step into the hollow and jump-break the life box. Wrong: spin (chains the Nitro). The green ! will not destroy the life box. Design: a precision reward in a constrained space.
4. **TNT/? 2×2 pile with penguin (S5-b).** Correct: stomp the penguin, stomp the top-right ?, then light a TNT and retreat. Wrong: spin the penguin into the pile, or light the TNT while standing on top. Loss: life; the ? still counts if blasted.
5. **Death-route two-pass (D-b).** Setup: the outline 1UP near the route start fills only from the ! at the route end, and dropping to the gem exits the route. Correct for the box gem: reach the end, spin the ! (stopping yourself on the ice), walk back to the start, break the 1UP, return by the elevator to C2 and finish the level. For gem 2 you jump down at the end (p099L: choose one per run, or die to return to C2 and redo the route). Design: the hardest route in the game is made doubly demanding for the completionist.
6. **Bonus chain demolition (B-a…B-d).** The TNT is first a springboard, later a demolition charge. Order (stomp normal boxes first, TNT last; trigger the exit TNTs first → second → top) decides fruit and the life.

## Design logic of this level
1. **Most boxes in Crash 2, spread over four distinct grammars:** a 3D collection field (posts), long side-scroll strips with small "rooms", a bonus demolition and a Nitro gauntlet. Crate density spikes in rooms and drops to zero in timing corridors (smashers, icicles, bridges, disc rocks), which keeps the challenges separate.
2. **Crates as the reason to take risks.** The 8 post crates exist only to make you climb unstable footing among seals. Without them the field is skippable.
3. **Explosives move from environmental to tool to obstacle.** First the icicle lights a TNT for you (S2-c). Then you use TNT as a weapon (S3-c) and a springboard (bonus). Later TNT and Nitro are obstacles beside enemies (S4, S5) and on ice (death route).
4. **Enemy and switch coupling.** The porcupine-into-! room and the four camera-hidden ! blocks both make switches a perception puzzle rather than a reflex.
5. **Masks come right before gauntlets.** Aku 1 comes before the first enemy fields; Aku 2 comes just before the slide/penguin/smasher descent. The death route recommends bringing a mask (D2 47, 58), and its arrow sandwich may hold one more.
6. **Checkpoints sit at the start of each camera mode** (elevator top, return to 3D, last island). C2 deliberately sits at the death-route gate, where the no-death condition is checked.
7. **All-box goal vs clear goal diverge sharply.** Clearing needs none of: the hidden !s, the Nitro-shielded life, the bonus, the death route or the outline 1UP. The perfect needs all of them, a walk-back through the death route (two passes if you also want its gem), and the green ! at C5 last of all.
8. **Count traps cluster where the camera can't see:** pillars hiding switches, crates above the bonus frame, the outcrop room, and the outline that only the route-end switch fills.
9. **Ice is the difficulty multiplier.** The same Nitro spacing that is trivial on rock becomes lethal when you can't stop. F2 advises jumping as slowly as possible (p099L).
10. **Body slam as a stack tool and a brake:** one slam clears the slide-bottom column (S3-a), and a slam is the way to stop on the death route's arrow crate (D-d).

## Transfer to the skate game
1. **Post-top crate field (S1-b) → skate "pillar pick" room:** 8 posts of 2–4 crate heights (2.3–4.6 m) with one crate each, and patrolling enemies on the floor. At our numbers the 2–3 high posts are reachable by full jump or double jump. Make the 4-high ones need a wooden arrow at the field edge (≈6 m with hold). On the board the posts are too narrow to ride, so this is an on-foot pocket.
2. **Environment-lit TNT (S2-c):** hang a falling hazard over a TNT + ? pair. The player must stop short (our TNT fuse is 3 s). For skaters, put the pair on the main line so cruising at 12 m/s past it when the icicle drops is the tempting mistake.
3. **Enemy-as-switch-press:** a crate releases an enemy that can be kicked (slide) into a ! a few metres behind. With our slide this is a natural on-foot puzzle. The ! can then materialise grindable steel steps.
4. **Camera-hidden switches → outline grind rail:** hide four ! blocks behind two pillars, and make the outline row 4–6 crates long and equal height. Once solid it becomes a crate rail (≥ 2 touching crates). Grinding it collects them all, which rewards finding the switches with a satisfying grind payout.
5. **Nitro on ice → Nitro on a downhill skate line:** space Nitros 2–3 crate widths apart on a 20–30 m/s downhill. The tap ollie (0.56 m) can't clear them, so the player must charge ollies early. Add a mask crate on an arrow before the field, as in the death route, so riding into a Nitro with the mask clears the field.
6. **Two-pass route-end switch:** an optional hard branch whose last ! solidifies a 1UP at the branch start. On the board, make the return trip a different line (reverse grind along the branch rail), so the backtrack is fun rather than dead time.
7. **TNT springboard (bonus):** a lit TNT is a 3 s window to stomp-bounce (14 m/s) onto a higher ledge. Pair it with a crate pyramid below that the TNT clears if the player is late.

### Camera and route shape

- **S1** — into-screen: diagonal snow canyon climbing away from the camera (F2 p067R draws it bottom-left to top-right); ice cliffs wall both sides; pits cut across the full width; ends in a vertical elevator shaft (D2 p059 shot 7).
- **S2** — side-scroll: left-to-right strips (F2 p068L, p068R strip A then strip B). The route climbs a crate stair to the bonus pad, drops to a room under an overhang, climbs switch-made steel steps, then steps down ice ledges to C2.
- **S3** — side-scroll (descending): five alternating ice slides drop the route several storeys (F2 p068R strip B; D2 shot 21), then left to right along ice ledges and a smasher corridor to C3 (F2 p069L strip 1).
- **S4** — side-scroll: left to right (F2 p069R strip 1, p069L strip 2, p069R strip 2); ends by dropping back into the 3D view (F2 つづく↓; D2 shot 34).
- **S5** — into-screen: climb bottom-left to top-right in a walled canyon (F2 p070L); a pit, a deep trench and further pits cut across the corridor; stone-disc channels cross it at right angles near the top.
- **S6** — into-screen: same climb to the goal (F2 p070R): three more disc channels, steel stairs, two stump pillars.
- **BONUS** — side-scroll (descending): entered from the green pillar pad in S2; the camera follows you down "level to level" (D2 shot 11).
- **DEATH** — side-scroll: green-lit ice cave entered by the skull elevator beside C2 (D2 shots 46–47); left to right; ends with a drop shaft to the gem (D2 59) or the ! block that lets you walk back (D2 60).

The level uses three camera grammars, and each owns a hazard family. Into-screen canyons get pits with falling stumps and enemies that slide down toward the camera. Side-scroll strips get vertical smashers, icicles and collapsing bridges. The final into-screen climb gets disc-rock channels that cross the corridor.

### Geometry and enemy design logic

1. Each camera grammar owns its own hazard family. The 3D canyon has pits with falling stumps and enemies that slide toward the camera. The side-scroll strips have smashers, icicles and collapsing bridges. The final 3D climb has disc rocks crossing the corridor, and the death route replays the side-scroll family on ice.
2. One hazard escalates four times. Smasher pair 1 teaches run-under/slide-under on a flat floor (O-S2-a). Pair 2 repeats it with less slack (O-S2-d). Three smashers on ice remove reliable stopping (O-S3-b). The five-smasher colonnade then inverts the rule, because waiting kills (O-S4-b). The death route finally combines smashers with Nitros (G-D-c).
3. Icicles teach bait-and-wait with a proximity trigger: walk close, stop short, let them fall, pass (O-S2-b). The same trigger then lights a TNT (O-S2-f) and later guards a seal on ice (O-D-b). One rule, three contexts.
4. Falling platforms escalate and then relent. The first stump stands alone (P-S1-b). The second has a spinning penguin on its landing (P-S1-f). The last one recovers after dropping, so the player can back off from a mistimed disc crossing (P-S5-f, POINT19). The most generous version comes just before the last checkpoint.
5. Ice multiplies everything and forces a commitment decision. Some places demand slowness: Nitro spacing in the trench and death route (F2 p099L), and smashers on ice (POINT10). Others demand full speed: the colonnade, where D2 33 says stopping or slowing is fatal. The player must read which.
6. Enemy archetype follows camera. In into-screen downhill stretches, enemies slide at you as projectiles (penguins and seals). In side-scroll strips, they stand on landing ledges or in recesses (penguins beside Nitros, porcupines in rooms). The final climb puts porcupines on pit rims, where facing direction decides when you cross.
7. The porcupine is taught in three geometries, and each limits your approach. In the closed recess you hug the wall and use him as a projectile (E-S2-a). In the low passage you lure him out (E-S3-d). On pit rims you cross while he faces away (E-S5-d, E-S6-a).
8. Explosive neighbours change enemy counters. A penguin is spun on open floor, but beside a Nitro or a TNT pile it must be stomped (E-S4-d, E-S5-c). Placing an enemy against an explosive is the cheapest way to make an old enemy a new test.
9. Rest is spaced by geometry rather than by empty floor. The wide pit banks in S1, the overhang ledge between Nitro ledges (P-S4-f) and the C5 island inside the disc field (P-S5-g) are the rests, and every checkpoint sits where the camera changes or a hazard family begins.
10. Non-lethal failure is reserved for precision tasks. A fall from the posts drops you among seals (G-S1-c), and a miss on the crate stair drops you to the floor (G-S2-c). Lethal gaps are kept short (≈1–3 u) and simple in shape.
11. Disc channels are crossing gates with a built-in perch. A ≈1-u curb lines each channel, so you can stand and watch the disc before committing (P-S5-d). The finale doubles the disc (O-S6-b), and the late channels put an enemy on the perch (E-S6-b).
12. Crates act as geometry throughout. The striped stair is the only way up to the bonus (P-S2-e). The TNT is a springboard in the bonus (G-B-a). Nitros shorten landings (O-S4-a) and mine stepping stones (O-S5-a). The arrow in the death route becomes a catapult trap (G-D-b).
13. The death route strips the level's verbs of safe footing. It has ice everywhere, smashers with Nitros under them, porcupines on small ledges and no checkpoint, since dying returns you to C2 (D2 59). Its single exit makes the gem goal and the box goal incompatible in one pass (G-D-d).

### Geometry transfer to the skate game

1. Smasher ladder: put four `crusher` set-pieces through one level, using `phase` offsets on a shared `cycle`. Start with a pair on flat ground offset by half a cycle (run under the first, slide under the second). Repeat the pair with tighter spacing, then three over a `slip` (icy) platform. Finish with a colonnade of five crushers ≈2 crates (≈2.3 m) apart over ≈11 crates (≈13 m). A 12 m/s skate cruise crosses it in ≈1.1 s and a 9 m/s run in ≈1.5 s, so step the phases ≈0.2–0.25 s apart so that only continuous motion passes, as in POINT15.
2. Disc channel: put a `stone` with `axis: "x"` in a trough 2–3 crates wide (≈2.3–3.5 m) so it rolls across the corridor, with a 1-crate curb on each side as a perch (and an edge grind for skaters). The crossing fits inside one full jump (≈5.6 m). For the finale, set two `stone`s in one trough half a patrol apart so they cross in the middle.
3. Falling stump sequence: use a `crumble` pad 1.5 crates wide (≈1.7 m) in the middle of a 3-crate (≈3.5 m) `pit`, with a short `shake` (≈0.3 s) so it "drops the moment you land". Use it alone first, then with a `spinner` on the landing bank. The engine's crumble already returns 10 s after dropping, which is POINT19's recovering stump: place that version before a moving hazard and tell the player they may retreat.
4. Nitro trench: lay narrow pillar tops 1–1.5 crates wide (≈1.2–1.7 m) in a `pit` trench, with Nitros on three of them in a 2 + 1 pattern. Clearing the adjacent pair needs ≈2.3–3.5 m of travel plus a landing, which is in full-jump range. A double jump (+1.74 m) gives the height margin. Keep skate lines out: the tap ollie (0.56 m) cannot clear a crate.
5. Icicle bait-and-wait has no proximity primitive here (`crusher` runs on a fixed `cycle`/`phase`). Approximate it with one slow-cycle `crusher` (≈3–4 s) just past a checkpoint, phased to drop as the player arrives, so the first lesson is "stop short, watch it fall, then pass". Do not rely on it to light a TNT: nothing measured shows a crusher detonating crates.
