## Puzzle rooms and dependency chains
- **P1 TNT pair → TNT triangle (S1-j → S1-k).** Setup: a W and a TNT side by side with a prompt, then a
  courtyard with W · TNT · W spread out. Correct: stomp the TNT, walk away from the blast, collect the
  fragments. Tempting wrong move: spin the TNT, which detonates it instantly; at point-blank range that
  costs a life, and the level gem allows only 3 deaths. What is lost: a life, never a crate. Why it works:
  the same object appears twice, first isolated, then with an escape route to plan.
- **P2 The exit wall (S2-h).** Setup: a 5×3 wall with a `?` in the centre blocks the only exit. Correct: break
  the bottom row; the upper crates settle and can be spun or stomped. Wrong: none is punished; the
  wall is the safe place to learn that unsupported crates drop. Lost: nothing. Design: an
  unskippable gate that also pays out 15 crates (14 % of the level).
- **P3 Held arrow (B-c, B-d).** Setup: a crate floats out of normal arrow range. Correct: hold jump on contact,
  and break the arrow only after using it. Wrong: tap-bouncing falls short, and breaking the arrow first makes
  the floating crate unreachable. Lost: one bonus crate; the bonus can be retried at no cost in lives. Design:
  the first tool crate in the game is taught where failure is free.
- **P4 Bonus pad bait (S5-d).** Setup: the pad and two crates share the cave floor. Wrong: stepping on the pad
  first and forgetting the crates on the way back. Lost: the all-box gem only.
- **P5 Hidden gem (S4, crawl).** A prompt teaches the crawl right at the hut. Sliding does not work; you
  must crawl under the hut and jump up into the hidden space.

## Design logic of this level
1. **Front-loaded density.** 53 of the 104 crates (51 %) sit before the vine, in the first ~90 s of play. The
   first act teaches crates; the later acts use far fewer (9, 10, 5, 8, 8, 3, 8).
2. **One mechanic per beat, and the tests are recombinations.** Plain crate → crab → hidden crate → TNT
   with a prompt → TNT surrounded → mask → checkpoint → stack → crate wall. Each new rule is first shown alone on
   flat, safe ground.
3. **The first count trap comes at crate 8.** It is behind a low wall you have just jumped. The lesson
   ("look behind what you clear") is planted before the player has any reason to care about the all-box gem.
4. **Protection before danger, saves after it.** The Aku crate (S1-l) sits at the foot of the spider temple and
   C1 at the top of its stairs. Every checkpoint follows a "new thing": TNT and wheels, the vine, the
   sinking stones, the cave, and C5 before the final climb.
5. **The exit is a crate wall.** The biggest single cluster in the level (15) is also its only door,
   so every player sees crates drop when their support breaks.
6. **Forced-motion line = crates above the line.** On the vine, crates float one jump above the ride.
   The first sits at ride height beside the line, so it collects itself; the rest need a jump off the moving
   line. Failure is cheap (you cannot fall off the vine) unless a jump drifts over a pit.
7. **Enemies set the timing, not the location.** Throwers and spiders do not guard crates directly.
   Crates sit in their lanes and drop zones, so collecting means waiting for the enemy's rhythm.
8. **Bait sits at bonus pads and exits.** Two crates beside the pad, one beside the bonus exit and three around
   the final summit: each sits where the player's attention is pulled toward a goal.
9. **Advanced crate types are held back.** No Nitro, no `!`, no iron-bound, no bounce crate. The only arrow
   is in the bonus, where lives cannot be lost.
10. **The all-box goal adds about 6 % extra travel.** Only #8 and the pad pair require a deliberate turn back;
    everything else is on the line, which suits the first level.

## Transfer to the skate game
1. **Crate wall as exit gate (S2-h).** Close a temple-exit arch with a 5×3 grid (5.8 m wide × 3.5 m high
   at 1.152 m crates) with one `?` in the centre. On foot: spin the bottom row so the upper rows settle. On the
   board above 12.5 m/s, each plowed crate costs 8 % speed, so a 5-deep plow through a low wall is a real
   choice. Keep it 3 high so it cannot be ollied (a full ollie clears only one crate).
2. **"Behind the thing you jump" count trap (S1-f).** Place a 1-crate-tall carved wall across the
   corridor at the first ollie lesson, with one wood crate directly behind it at 0.5–1 m. At cruise speed
   (12 m/s) the ollie carries the rider about 7 m past it. The player has to stop or turn back, which teaches
   reversing early.
3. **Vine → rail float-line (S3).** On a downhill rail or forced line, put the first crate at board height
   beside the line (collected by passing contact). Put the next 6–8 crates 1.2–1.6 m above the rail top, spaced
   8–12 m apart with fruit arcs between them, so a full-charge ollie off the rail (peak ≈ 1.7 m) breaks each one
   and lands back on the line.
4. **TNT pair then triangle (S1-j/k).** First a TNT beside one wood crate on open ground (on foot: stomp
   and walk off). Then W · TNT · W spaced about 3.5 m (3 crates) apart. On the board, a TNT hit at 1.5–12.5 m/s
   lights the fuse and anything faster detonates it, so the triangle becomes a speed-choice test.
5. **Bonus-pad bait (S5-d).** Two wood crates within 2 m of the bonus pad, on the side the camera does
   not face on arrival.
6. **Held-arrow lesson only in a no-fail space (B-c).** A wooden arrow with a crate 5.5–6 m above it
   (reachable only with the held ×1.25 bounce, about 6.1 m; a plain bounce gives about 3.9 m). Teach it in the
   bonus first.
