superscript (10 = bonus, confirmed by gamepressure and the D2 bonus HUD 0 → 2 → 5) are firm. The crate

## Puzzle rooms and dependency chains

1. **S1-a start trio.** Setup: Nitro, Aku, TNT side by side. Correct: jump on the TNT, step away, take the
   mask, never touch the Nitro. Tempting wrong: spinning through the row (spin on TNT = instant blast;
   touching the Nitro = death). Loss: a life; for the perfect, the TNT must be broken one way or another.
2. **S1-c pillar-top ?.** Correct: stomp the low ? holding jump to reach the pillar top, then slide-plow
   the rest. Tempting wrong: slide through everything first — the low ? is gone and the fallback is a
   crouch-jump + spin. Hazard: a TNT sits among the slide targets on ice.
3. **S3-c outline staircase.** Press the ground ! → four boxes appear over spikes, rising diagonally.
   Break them from below (head-butt). Jumping on top risks a fall onto the spikes.
4. **S4-e/S4-f remote switch with a hidden !.** Outline pair seen first; the switch is above the striped
   box over the next steel arrow, off the top of the screen. Sequence: arrow → head-bonk the striped →
   keep holding jump → hit the ! → walk back to S4-e. Lost if missed: 2 boxes including the only main
   1UP; all-box only (the route is unaffected).
5. **S4-b green ! behind the camera.** On landing at C3 you face forward; the switch is behind you.
   Without it the six Nitros never count, so the perfect is impossible. Pure camera-awareness test.
6. **S4-c wall on ice.** Approach slowly; a running start slides you through the gap into the penguin.
   Break layer by layer; the inner boxes are hidden from the default camera.

## Design logic of this level

1. **The explosive grammar is introduced on the first screen** (Nitro, Aku, TNT in one row), then Nitros
   reappear only where ice makes precise movement hard (S1-d) or where you must drop past one (S3-b).
2. **Ice changes how crates are attacked:** sliding is the tool on ice (F2 POINT2); one TNT among the
   slide targets makes blind sliding costly.
3. **Teach, then twist:** steel arrow + floating striped appears as a visible lesson (S1-b); the second
   copy (S4-f) hides a ! above it. The bonus teaches head-butting floating boxes from below just before
   the main course demands it over spikes (S3-c).
4. **Both remote switches require backtracking and both are hidden from the default camera** (green !
   behind the arrival point; ! above the screen). The all-box goal in this stage is about looking around,
   not about dexterity.
5. **Outline boxes before their switch** (S4-e) are a visible promise that makes the player look for the
   switch; the first outline set (S3-c) has its switch right beside it as the lesson.
6. **Checkpoint spacing:** C1 after the ice lesson, C2 straight after the second crusher, C3 at the bottom
   of the free-fall with the green switch hidden behind it.
7. **Timed hazards frame crates:** S2-c sits right after a drop-pillar with a seal behind it, C2 right
   after another pillar.
8. **The big payout is late and guarded.** The wall (up to a third of the main boxes) sits on ice with a
   penguin behind it, near the end of the stage.
9. **Lives:** the only main 1UP is behind the hardest hidden switch (S4-e); the bonus 1UP is in the
   highest box of the float line (B-b); the pillar-top ? (S1-c) is the early life chance.
10. **Optional routes do not inflate the all-box count:** the red-gem course is excluded from 74.

## Transfer to the skate game

1. **Arrow + floating multi-hit, then the twist.** First instance: wooden arrow (16 m/s, ≈3.9 m bounce)
   with a multi-hit crate whose underside is ≈3.4 m above the arrow lid. Second instance later in the
   level: the same pair plus a `bang` ≈6 m above the arrow, reachable only with a held bounce (×1.25,
   ≈6.1 m), placed above the default camera framing, with its outline group placed *before* it.
2. **Ice-field plow.** A low-traction field with plain crates scattered around a 2-steel pillar (2.3 m)
   topped by a ? crate. On the board at ≥12.5 m/s plain crates are plowed (8 % tax each) but one TNT among
   them detonates instantly at that speed; on foot, a 1-high ? crate's stomp bounce (≈3 m above its lid,
   apex ≈4.15 m) reaches the pillar-top crate (lid 3.46 m). Breaking the low ? first removes the step.
3. **Start-screen explosive row** (Nitro, mask, TNT within 3 crate widths, in front of the spawn) teaches
   that on the board TNT ≥12.5 m/s is lethal while 1.5–12.5 m/s only lights it.
4. **Outline staircase over a hazard:** four outlines rising one crate per step diagonally over spikes,
   switch on the ground in front of them; break from below (head-bump while jumping). On the board the
   materialised staircase is a sequence of 1-crate ollie steps (charged ollie ≈1.7 m clears one).
5. **Wall as a layered grind block.** A 4-wide × 3-high × 2-deep crate block: its top layer is a 4 × 2 grid
   of equal-height crates, i.e. grindable rows; grinding breaks a row and exposes the next layer, so
   "eating the wall" by repeated grinds is the skate version of S4-c. Put a steel column at one end so the
   rows terminate against an unbreakable cap.
6. **Hide the nitrobang behind the checkpoint** (opposite the camera's forward direction) to create a
   deliberate count trap; reward a camera turn.
7. **Crusher-adjacent crates:** place a 2-stack immediately past a timed crusher and a checkpoint right
   after the next one, so stopping for crates is a timing decision.

### Camera and route shape

The stage changes camera mode at one-way transitions: S1 into-screen → round lift → S2–S3 side-scroll (F2 「ここから横スクロールじゃ」) → free-fall shaft → S4 into-screen. Each mode has its own geometry: the into-screen sections are walled ice-cave floors ≈5–8 u wide (start canyon, ice field, Nitro field, crate-wall cave) with a few pits and cliff steps; the side-scroll sections are separate snow ledges (≈2–12 u long) with ≈2 u gaps and crushers. S4 climbs a cliff diagonally (F2 p025L) through ledges joined by plank bridges. Neither the lift nor the shaft can be ridden back, so S4's only backward content is the green ! behind the C3 arrival point (toward the camera).

- **S1** — into-screen: walled ice canyon ≈5–6 u wide at the start (D2 shots 1–2), cliff steps, one bend (D2 shot 7), two ice floors; ends on a round lift
- **S2** — side-scroll: left → right along snow ledges over drops, with two green drop-pillars
- **S3** — side-scroll: continues left → right: twin log smashers, the bonus hole, the outline-staircase ledge, a seal pillar, a free-fall shaft down to S4 (one-way)
- **B** — side-scroll: icy cavern ledges and a float line; exit lift back to S3
- **S4** — into-screen: lands at C3 facing forward with the green ! behind (toward the camera); the path climbs a cliff diagonally through ledges joined by plank bridges

### Geometry and enemy design logic

1. Mode changes happen at one-way transitions (lift, free-fall shaft), and each camera mode has its own geometry grammar: into-screen sections are wide walled floors and ice fields (≈5–8 u); side-scroll sections are separate ledges (≈2–12 u) with ≈2 u gaps and crushers.
2. Ice escalates in four steps: a walled field with one TNT, where nothing can fall (P-S1-d) → a field with Nitro pairs, where contact kills (P-S1-e) → narrow icy bonus ledges, where sliding off is a fall (P-B-a) → ice in front of an enemy (P-S4-b).
3. Every crusher is followed at once by a demand — drop-pillar 1 → gap → occupied landing; drop-pillar 2 → checkpoint; log smashers → gap → penguin — so the reward for timing is flow, never a rest on the far side.
4. Small landings are occupied: seals and penguins wait on ≈2–4 u ledges after gaps (G-S2-a, G-S3-a, G-S3-c). F2 POINT11 names the counter — spin in the air before landing.
5. The two enemy kinds complement each other: penguins are timing enemies (attack when the spin stops; the slide also works), seals are group enemies (hit the first and all die) that double as bounce steps.
6. The twin log smashers move in unison and leave a pocket between them, so the gate accepts both a single dash and two half-dashes (O-S3-a).
7. The bonus entrance is a hole: the one gap meant to be fallen into is marked by a floating ? and guarded by a Nitro on its take-off edge (G-S3-b, O-S3-b).
8. The outline staircase floats over floor, not over a pit (F2 POINT10 screenshot): the guide's safe method (break from below) works because there is floor; climbing on top is the risky option.
9. S4 is a chain of cliff ledges joined by narrow plank bridges (G-S4-a); enemies, crates and arrows sit on the ledges and the bridges are pure chokes.
10. Vertical progress in S4 uses steel arrows as stairs (P-S4-d), then hides a switch above the camera frame (P-S4-e): the vertical framing limit is used as a design tool.
11. The free-fall shaft makes S4 a no-return section, so the only backward content (the green !) is placed directly behind the arrival point.
12. Checkpoints sit at transitions: the lift top (C1), right after drop-pillar 2 (C2) and the shaft bottom (C3).

### Geometry transfer to the skate game

1. Ice escalation with `slip` floors: (a) a walled 8 × 8 m slip field holding crates and one TNT; (b) a 6 m-wide slip approach with two staggered Nitro pairs ending at a `mover` lift (axis y) as a forced stop; (c) 3 m icy ledges with 2–3 m gaps in a side room; (d) a slip strip whose end, 1–2 m past a crate wall, holds an enemy.
2. Crusher → demand: a `crusher` over a 2–3 m raised block, then within 3 m a 2–2.5 m gap (well inside the 5.6 m running jump) onto a 3–4 m ledge holding a `grunt` with range ≤1 m, so neither the crusher block nor the landing is a place to stop; put the checkpoint straight after the second crusher.
3. Twin crushers in phase: two `crusher`s with the same cycle and phase, 2.5–3 m apart, leaving a ≥1.5 m pocket between them — one dash or two half-dashes both work.
4. Occupied pillar landing: a 2.3 m-wide pillar `platform` after a 2.5 m gap with an enemy on it (range ≤0.5 m); the counter is our air spin (0.3 s, +0.8 m reach) before touchdown, as in POINT11.
5. Marked drop-in bonus: in a side-scroll run (`zone` dir E), a 2.5 m hole between two ledges with a floating marker above it and a `returnportal` (airOnly) inside as the bonus entrance; one Nitro on the take-off edge.
6. Ledge-and-bridge cliff for a late section: 6–10 m ledges joined by 2 m-wide, 3–5 m-long bridges (`woodpath` or narrow `platform`), with enemies and crates only on the ledges; steel arrows as stairs to ledges 2.3–3.5 m higher (arrow apex 3.77 m above its lid).
