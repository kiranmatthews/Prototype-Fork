## Puzzle rooms and dependency chains
- **P1 Preserve the arrow (R-a → R-b, hub).** Setup: three floating time crates and an outline above a wooden arrow.
  First visit: take the three crates, leave the arrow. Gem run: break everything except the arrow, finish the green
  course, press G-e, return to the hub, bounce the arrow, spin the materialised top crate in mid-air, then break the
  arrow. Wrong: breaking the arrow on the first visit or before R-b exists. Loss: 1 box for the all-box gem on that
  visit (the top crate becomes unreachable). Why it works: a breakable tool must be read as a future key.
- **P2 Preserve the TNT key (S3-c → R-d).** Setup: W on TNT on a metal step, outlines under the step. First visit:
  "blast the TNT for now" is harmless. Gem run: jump completely over the stack, materialise the outlines via G-e, come
  back and detonate it — the blast reaches the crates under the step. Wrong: breaking it on the gem run before G-e.
  Loss: 2 boxes. The breakable 2×2 twin S3-d next to it is the contrast: one stack to break, one to keep.
- **P3 Switch ahead of its outlines (S2-b ← S2-d).** The ! is past the outlines; you return to break them (callout 3).
- **P4 Ranged switches (S2-g → S2-f).** Two wall !s fill a 2×2 outline group; they can only be shot.
- **P5 Column collapse (S1-c, TT).** Spin the base: two break, two fall; spin again.
- **P6 Bonus explosive logic.** TNT → triggers the ! under it (B-a); Nitro on a ledge (B-b); green ! shot with the
  bazooka clears all 8 Nitros (B-c, B-f); high ! shot for the last bridge (B-e). Every explosive is a switch or a
  counted box; nothing is mere decoration.
- **P7 The loop.** The green course ends at the hub. The all-box route is therefore S1 → S2 (keep the arrow) → S3 (keep
  the TNT stack) → bonus → C3 → green course → G-e → hub (R-a, R-b) → S3 again (R-c, R-d) → C3 → S4 → goal.

## Design logic of this level
1. **Crates teach the new weapon.** The level that hands out the bazooka puts crates and switches out of reach — S2-a
   floating pair, S2-g wall switches, S3-b high on the wall, S4-b over a gap — each with a jump alternative for experts.
2. **"Don't break this" crates invert the default.** Two objects (an arrow box and a W/TNT stack) must survive until a
   later switch exists. The all-box route is a memory and restraint test, not only a collection.
3. **Two-visit structure, announced.** Callout 4 tells the first-time player it is fine to pass the unfinished outlines;
   P3 says to come back with the green gem. Outline groups are placed before the player can know what fills them.
4. **Switch placement controls backtracking:** S2-d sits after its outlines (backtrack), S2-g sits high (ranged), G-e sits
   at the end of a gem course and sends you back to the start of S2 (loop).
5. **Explosives are keys and lids, never filler:** TNT key over hidden crates (S3-c), TNT lid on wood (S3-d, G-a), TNT as a
   remote switch press (B-a). All Nitros are sealed inside the bonus.
6. **Stacks show the collapse rule:** S1-c's 4-stack drops two at a time; S2-c's column floats (no collapse) above a tool.
7. **Density:** S1 10, S2 16 (8 behind switches), S3 14 + bonus 17, S4 9, green course 12, return 9 — the hub area (S2) is
   the dense puzzle core.
8. **Time trial gets two invincibility windows** (masks at S1-a/S1-b/S3-a, then S4-c), placed so invincibility covers the
   side-scroll and the final belts (TA3, TA6).
9. **Enemy-crate rhythm:** every crate pad sits between two MTATs; the belt direction relative to the MTAT tells you
   whether to shoot or spin.

## Transfer to the skate game
1. **Preserve-the-arrow.** A wooden arrow (16 m/s bounce ≈ 3.9 m, held ≈ 6.1 m) under an outline crate at ≈ 5.5 m that a
   later `bang` materialises. Our board smashes wooden arrows at ≥ 13 m/s, so put the arrow at the edge of the fast line:
   a careless charged run destroys the key. (Use a steel arrow where no puzzle is intended.)
2. **TNT key over a hidden group.** A TNT (with a wood crate on top) on a 1-crate-thick steel ledge; directly below it,
   2 outline crates that a later switch materialises. TNT blast radius 2.75 m and crate-break radius ≈ 3.35 m reach the
   group 1.2–2.3 m below; detonating it early wastes the key. Place a breakable twin (W/TNT + ?/?) 8–10 m before it.
3. **Switch after its outlines.** Put a `bang` 10–15 m past its outline group on a straight so the player rides back.
4. **Ranged switches → grind switches.** We have no bazooka; mount `!` boxes on top of a wall rail so a grind flips them
   (our ! triggers on grind-through), filling a 2×2 outline group on the floor below.
5. **Collapse column.** A 4-stack (top 4.61 m, at our jump limit): breaking the bottom drops the rest one level at a time
   (our crates settle), so a skater can take it from ground level in two slides/spins.
6. **Loop course.** An optional gem course whose last switch materialises crates in an earlier hub and returns the player
   there (transfer pad), turning the second half into a revisit.
