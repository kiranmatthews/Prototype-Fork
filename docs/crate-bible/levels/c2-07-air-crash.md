## Puzzle rooms and dependency chains
- **P1 — The stepping line vs. the secret warp (S2-a).** Setup: five crates laid from the pier toward a small platform. Correct (for the secret): leave the board, bounce crate to crate to the platform (the crates break as you use them; the count is kept). Tempting wrong: boarding and smashing them first. Loss: the route to Snow Go's red gem until you restart — and with it Snow Biz's perfect. Why: the same crates serve two goals; order decides which you get.
- **P2 — Nitro key in another stage (S2-f, S4-a, S4-c, S4-e ↔ AE-i).** Setup: 11 Nitro on the normal course, no green ! there. Correct: enter via Bear Down's secret warp, hit the green ! at the end of the alternate entrance, then play the normal course. Wrong: playing the normal course first. Loss: the box gem for that run. Why: turns the stage's crate total into a world-level route puzzle.
- **P3 — TNT pile with a life (S3-d).** Setup: 1UP ringed by 4 TNT, a striped crate at the back. Correct: hop on the striped crate, stomp the 1UP, stomp/spin the rest from a safe distance. Wrong: spinning into the pile (blast at point-blank). Loss: a life or death; the crates still count if blown.
- **P4 — Read before you board (S4-b).** Setup: a 2×2 ? block on a pad only reachable on foot from the pier. Correct: detour on foot, return, then board. Wrong: boarding immediately. Loss: 4 crates; the board cannot come back.
- **P5 — Bonus switch chain (B-b → B-c → B-d).** Setup: a TNT beside a switch, an iron-bound crate on another switch, outline crates beyond. Correct: TNT blast trips the first switch; body slam the iron-bound crate to press the second, which builds the crate stair. Wrong: spinning the iron-bound crate (nothing happens). Why: each crate type is used as a tool exactly once.
- **P6 — One-way trampoline (S3-b).** Jump onto the floating ? and hold jump: the crate breaks and the bounce is the return trip.
- **P7 — Lives for a life (AE-a).** Three floating lives over striped crates cost one death to collect; afterwards the crates are broken from the board.

## Design logic of this level
1. **Crates follow the vehicle.** On the board, crates are singles, pairs and loose fields on the racing line (S2-b, S2-c, S4-c), never stacks taller than two; vertical placement exists only above ramps (S2-d). On foot, crates form blocks, stacks and puzzles (S1-a, S1-b, S3-d, S4-b).
2. **Mode changes happen on piers with checkpoints.** All three checkpoints stand where the player boards or leaves the board; each pier starts a new rule set.
3. **The board cannot backtrack far, so on-foot detours guard count traps.** S4-b (2×2 ? block) is only reachable before boarding; S2-a doubles as an on-foot bridge. Both guides call them out.
4. **Crates as bridges.** Five crates bridge to a secret warp (S2-a), three outline crates bridge a bonus chasm (B-d), three striped crates hold up floating lives (AE-a). Breaking them is how you cross — or how you lose the crossing.
5. **Nitro on a racing line.** Nitro appears as a gate (6), a pair by the tiles (2), one inside a dense field (1), and two timed with piranhas (2). The more inviting the field, the more a single Nitro matters (POINT 11).
6. **Inter-stage locks.** The box gem needs Bear Down's secret warp (green ! in the alternate entrance); the stepping crates open the warp to Snow Go's red gem, which Snow Biz's perfect needs. Air Crash is a hub of crate dependencies.
7. **Masks before dense stretches**: before the piranha run (S1-a), before the Nitro gate (S2-e), at the death-route fork (S3-c).
8. **Lives cost risk**: the 1UP is inside a TNT ring, the alternate entrance's lives cost a death, the bonus life follows the hanging crates.
9. **Count traps are deliberately named**: POINT 6 (floating ?), "before boarding" (S4-b), the ramp crates you circle back for, the crates behind a mine (AE-e).
10. **Density curve**: main crates per section 7 / 21 / 12 / 19 — the two board-heavy sections carry two-thirds of the course's crates, but a third of those are Nitro.

## Transfer to the skate game
1. **Racing-line crates.** On a fast skate run (12–23 m/s), place singles and pairs every 15–25 m on the ideal line between hazards (fences, pits, mines); crates mark the line. Plain crates plow at ≥ 13 m/s, so a charged rider keeps flow; a rider at cruise (12 m/s) trips — the line rewards a commitment to speed.
2. **Kicker crates.** Hang a crate 1.5–2.5 m above the landing arc of each kicker (the board's only vertical tool), and let the course loop so a miss costs a lap, not a death (S2-d).
3. **Read before you ride.** At each board pickup, put a 2×2 crate block on a pad 3–6 m off the dock, reachable only on foot (S4-b); once the rider leaves, no return. Teach with a sign or first instance.
4. **Crate bridge to a secret.** Five crates in a stepping line across a gap/water toward a secret ledge: on foot it is a stomp chain (14 m/s bounce ≈ 3 m, gaps ≤ 2 crate widths); on the board a 2+ crate touching row is a one-way grind bridge consumed as ridden. Either way the crates are the route, and breaking them early closes it (S2-a, AE-a).
5. **Nitro gate.** Two converging Nitro lines with a 2.5–3 m middle lane at board speed; nothing collectible inside (S2-f). Any touch kills on the board, so give a 20–30 m straight approach.
6. **TNT ring with a life, off the board line.** 1UP in the centre of 4 TNT with a bouncy (multi-hit) crate behind as the entry step — an on-foot puzzle placed on a pier, not on the board line (≥ 13 m/s contact with TNT kills).
7. **Switch-built bridges.** A TNT next to a ! (blast trips it, radius 2.75 m) and a reinforced crate sitting on a second ! (body slam presses it) that materialises a 3-crate floating stair (B-b to B-d).
