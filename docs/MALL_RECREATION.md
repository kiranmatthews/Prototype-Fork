# Mall · New York

Source-owned reconstruction of the original 1999 THPS Mall for the fork's existing skater. Select **Mall · New York** in MENU, or open `/?playtest&level=mall`. Add `&lite` for the fast renderer. The shared Geometry Lab and published `public/levels.json` are unchanged by this task.

Arrow keys/WASD steer; hold Space to build speed and release to ollie; E grinds; F spins. K/L move between the five checkpoint stops. M shows the developer controls if MENU is hidden.

## Reference and scale

The supplied VGCartography cutaway is the primary layout reference. A [higher-resolution copy on VGMaps](https://www.vgmaps.com/Atlas/PSX/TonyHawk%27sProSkater-Mall.png) helps locate the upper/lower routes, elevator recess, crossed rails and plaza. The original PlayStation [walkthrough by Tom Hayes](https://www.cheatcodes.com/guide/faq-walkthrough-tony-hawks-pro-skater-playstation-15891/) identifies the car-to-upper-rail approach and fountain-to-gallery sequence. [Original PS1 gameplay by Marcanine](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=n0kosmzFb0A) supplies direct visual checks: the entrance transition around 9:13, the fountain passage at 13:25, straight paired beams at 14:31, and the elevated reward at 14:36. The earlier THAW Classic guide is not used as dimensional evidence for the 1999 level.

The authored route is approximately 412 metres long and descends 25 metres. Ordinary rails are approximately 0.7–1.0 m above their floor; the lower light beams are 1.25 m above the atrium floor. The entrance quarter has a 3.2 m radius; elevator quarters and the paired exit banks use 3 m radii. The blue plaza banks rise 2.2 m. The fountain gap, upper rail catch and mezzanine landing were tuned through geometry against the existing charged ollie and momentum. Movement tuning, forces and the authored steering model remain unchanged. The earlier closed-rail support allows a round grind to cross its seam. The structural pass uses existing mesh and wall primitives without new runtime changes.

The third pass restores the separate left escalator into the Flying Leap, three long fountain-side planters with grindable rims, and the letter position on the lower light beam. Crossrails are now straight diagonals. Source gameplay around 6:12–6:22 also shows the blue bank feature, side awnings, raised passage over the final stairs, banked stair throat and narrow parking doors; these replace the earlier sculpture and isolated exit quarter interpretation. Their exact profiles remain inferred from those views.

The fourth pass adds a 4.2 m round planter to a widened first concourse, moves the first directory onto that island, and adds the two short outward-facing combo poles. A charged lateral ollie links the left pole to Coffee Grind and the right pole to a planter rim. The elevated reward now has an actual transfer between the two separate upper light beams, validated from all three upper approaches.

The fifth pass closes the raised decks with skirts and soffits, gives the escalators solid bodies, adds masonry supports, and preserves the lower routes beneath the first island and upper gallery. The second fountain island now has an approach bank. Three joined ceiling meshes replace the overlapping roof boxes, and the atrium walls meet that roof. The raised exit passage preserves the central banked stair route. Source footage at 1:12–1:15 confirms the offset first escalator and lower aisle; 5:15 shows the open passage and masonry below the second gallery.

The sixth pass adds the chamfered water trough beside the first escalator, including its grindable rim and solid masonry. The first deck supports now leave a clear lower aisle beside it. At the exit, masonry wings join the final banks to the outer walls and close an unintended passage behind those banks; a continuous ceiling covers the stair chamber. A new underpass route completes from spawn, and the mezzanine check requires its first landing to occur beyond all 32 stairs. Source gameplay at 1:13 and 6:20 supports these structural changes.

The seventh pass replaces the decorative fountain centre with the original through-opening: a faceted stone monument, broad cap, falling side sheets and a rideable intermediate floor. The first island descends into its launch, and the receiving island has a flat landing before its climb. A controlled two-jump line lands inside; an unbraked line clears the passage in one flight. The aperture clears the actual rendered skater, and a local feathered follow-camera volume keeps the cap from pushing the lens into his head. No shared runtime or movement tuning changes were needed. The original footage at 13:25 and the original guide’s two fountain transfers support this correction.

Dimensions are inferred from the reference and verified for this skater; the image provides no metric survey or original collision mesh. This is not a certified mesh-identical or dimension-exact extraction. Original textures and THPS challenge logic are not imported: native fruit marks the letter locations, a crystal rewards the hidden rail line, and native checkpoints handle recovery. The five directory signs are scenery.

## Fidelity still requiring evidence

The route tests demonstrate playable connections and safe landings. They do not prove a dimension-exact recreation. The new planter and combo poles are playable, but their precise source dimensions and the first escalator's full side profile still need comparison. The water trough is now playable, while the exact side-access contours need further comparison. The new receiver bank, deck thicknesses and supports are reference-derived estimates. The fountain intermediate landing is now functional, but its exact profile and the exit upper passage still need source comparison. Fountain materials, storefront massing and the concourse silhouette remain visibly simplified. Hall lengths and widths, deck elevations, transition radii, beam heights and entrance/exit contours remain estimates. The supplied cutaway hides some surfaces and supplies no measurement scale. An original PS1 Mall mesh/collision export, or calibrated plans and views, would resolve those uncertainties. The goal remains open.
