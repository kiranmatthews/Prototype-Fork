# Coconut Cart Rally balance — 27 October 2026

The pass reduces feedback loops that let a clean early lead grow while the rest of the pack repeatedly wrecks or misses items:

- The leader retains normal engine power, speed and handling. Distance behind the leader enables at most 4% extra driving assistance, starting beyond 35 m and saturating at 150 m. The same rule applies to human and AI drivers, does not stack with a boost, and is disabled in time trials.
- Ordinary damage keeps its previous speed penalty. Severe engine damage is capped at 18% speed loss in the rally; this does not heal damage or prevent destruction. Other kart levels keep their previous loss curve.
- Cart weapon hits grant 2.2 seconds of recovery, protecting the pack from immediate repeated hits. The first heavy item becomes available after 18 seconds; heavy attacks share their cooldown. Stars and tidal waves respect the existing once-per-racer limit.
- Item odds respond to actual distance, with capped extra chances of boosts and pursuit tools. Last place in a close pack receives the same odds as second place. Leaders retain useful defense and ordinary weapons. TNT and nitro remain available throughout the field.
- Mystery boxes return after 1.2 seconds in cart races, and empty-handed AI aims for an available nearby box. AI saves boosts for supported straights and allows their additional speed before braking for corners. Human item controls remain immediate.
- A blocked AI physically reverses out. A wrecked racer without a usable cart within 65 m can receive a clear nearby loaner; the racer still runs to it and performs the existing entry animation.

`tools/test-rally-balance.mjs` checks the bounds, 50,000 item draws per case, unchanged clean driving and other-cart damage behavior, AI decisions, human controls, solo behavior, hit recovery and a physically blocked driver. `tools/measure-rally-balance.mjs` runs complete production-physics races using an input-only pilot. Candidate and baseline use seeds 11, 741 and 2026. The baseline is an isolated export of commit `6a3e38b3b94e86f0edf1b1c194de25dda1278a2e`, with its own source and imported authored JSON; assets and dependencies are shared unchanged.

Three seeded races are regression evidence, not a guarantee about every race or human skill level. The three baseline races averaged a 32.84-second first-to-last spread; the candidate averaged 29.33 seconds. Lead changes after the opening 20 seconds increased from four to ten across the three runs, while early leaders won one of three in both versions. All 48 racer finishes completed with zero falls. Winning margins averaged 2.90 seconds before and 4.48 after; median-field gaps were 13.53 and 14.75 seconds. These mixed results do not support a claim that every finish is closer. Both versions retained similar destructive action (27 and 28 wrecks).

A separate controlled straight with identical throttle inputs demonstrates the intended bounded effect: a 150 m deficit closes to 140.61 m over eight seconds, and the leader's 36.342 m/s pace remains exactly unchanged. The human and AI chaser both reach 37.783 m/s. A close 15 m gap receives no help; boosts reach the same 42.848 m/s whether close or far behind. Detailed race and controlled-pursuit evidence is in `comparison.json`, `check.json`, `baseline-races.json` and `candidate-races.json`. Geometry, full-height mounds, sponsor layout, controls, animations and visual damage remain unchanged.

The full production controller-pilot race also passes: all eight racers finish in 93.53–142.25 seconds with no falls, real keyboard throttle/steering/braking and X-release behavior pass, and the solo restart and stadium support checks pass. The audio context remains running after the finish, seven occupied parked carts have less than 0.00002 m/s of residual numerical motion, and no engine loops remain. The eighth racer finishes on foot.
