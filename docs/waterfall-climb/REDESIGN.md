# Waterfall Climb — second design, 27 October 2026

The first design was rejected: seven river chapters varied numbers on the same long platform/gap pattern, then nine almost identical switchbacks. Passing a completion pilot did not establish enjoyable play. This revision replaces that structure rather than dressing it again.

## Reference review

Original PS1 gameplay reviewed in [Vissar's complete walkthrough](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1OKB8gobKCw): Upstream 10:12–10:52, Great Gate 6:29–7:09, Native Fortress 17:54–18:04, Up the Creek 22:40 onward. The recording uses DuckStation, upscaling and interpolation; observations concern the original game layout.

- Upstream: a narrow log gives way to a leaf, a small safe stone, and a plant guarding the next water gap. Very little empty runway separates decisions. Footing changes size and timing.
- Great Gate: short timber ledges put creatures and gaps together; a drum gives a distinct vertical move; a larger landing allows recovery. The visual wall makes the route readable without identical platforms.
- Native Fortress: active carved mechanisms, high shelves and bounce boxes change the height and timing problem within the same visual theme.
- Up the Creek: leaf-to-stone landings and hostile plants keep water crossings legible while making the player look ahead.

[Gavin and Rubin's development account](https://all-things-andy-gavin.com/2011/02/05/making-crash-bandicoot-part-4/) describes constructing levels from readable mechanics and increasingly varied combinations. It also warns that the designer's own play is biased. Our inference: a successful automated route is collision evidence, not a quality verdict.

## New composition

1. **Reedwater** — short offset stones, a thin fallen branch, a drifting leaf, a guarded rest island. Establish water as danger and bank changes as useful decisions.
2. **The fallen cedar** — a sinuous timber ride rises above the river, ending in a long exposed grind crossing and a broad dismount bank. Skate is a route verb.
3. **Lotus locks** — one spin-open sluice, small receivers, a fragile pair and a lateral ferry. The timer is learned from stable ground before it is combined with another demand.
4. **Millrace** — broad curved downhill carving, an ollie across a broken channel, then an uphill braking basin. Long travel earns its length through momentum.
5. **Thunder pools** — compact rising cascades, offset small landings and a final lock; a root bank leads into the waterfall reveal and optional bonus.
6. **Lower gatehouse** — irregular shelf sizes and heights, one airborne hinge activation and a creature on a broad balcony.
7. **Waterwheel shaft** — board a lift at its low stop, watch the waterfall drop away, leave onto a high balcony. A different vertical rhythm.
8. **Hanging aqueduct** — a rising skate traverse and exposed rail transfer use the cliff width without repeating rows of jumps.
9. **Bell platforms** — large springing drums launch between deliberately offset high shelves; a timed leaf connects the two halves.
10. **Broken crown** — unstable timber, a short timing combination and the final climb to the source pool. Finish is on the summit itself.

## Acceptance

Use production player inputs only for continuous main and bonus journeys; report skating distance, actual grinds, moving-platform rides and bounce contacts, not only a gate flag. Review every set piece in normal and MAX/native full rendering. Test deliberate misses at low and high checkpoints, timer expiry/reset, bonus entry/failure/retry/return and the all-box denominator. Keep TUNING unchanged. Final handoff must explicitly supersede the first design and its old screenshots.
